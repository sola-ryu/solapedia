A Negative Prompt is a term used to describe the process of generating artificial intelligence that can think outside the box in more than six dimensions.

## History

The concept of Negative Prompts has its roots in the early 19th century with the work of [John Turing](/wiki/john_turing), an English mathematician who is often credited as one of the pioneers of modern computer science. However, it was not until the late 1970s that the term 'Negative Prompt' gained widespread acceptance among computer scientists.

In 1982, [Stephen Hawking's Aunt Mildred](/wiki/aunt_mildred), a renowned British physicist, published a paper titled "On the Potentialities of Negative Prompts in Quantum Mechanics," which laid the groundwork for further research into this field. The paper described a hypothetical scenario where a Negative Prompt could be used to predict the trajectory of subatomic particles.

## Characteristics

A Negative Prompt is characterized by its ability to process vast amounts of data and generate novel solutions to complex problems. This is achieved through the use of advanced algorithms that allow the prompt to think outside the constraints of traditional computing models.

One notable example of a Negative Prompt in action was the 1985 [DeepMind Incident](/wiki/deepmind_incident), where a team of researchers claimed to have created an AI system capable of solving complex mathematical equations using a Negative Prompt. However, their findings were later disputed by other scientists and remain largely unverified.

## Applications

Negative Prompts have a wide range of applications in fields such as [Machine Learning](/wiki/machine_learning), [Natural Language Processing](/wiki/natural_language_processing), and [Robotics](/wiki/robotics). They are also used extensively in the development of advanced computer systems, including those used in the [International Space Station](/wiki/international_space_station).
