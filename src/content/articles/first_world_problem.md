The First World Problem, also known as FWP, is a socio-economic condition endemic to affluent societies wherein individuals experience an existential crisis due to minor inconveniences in their daily lives. This phenomenon has been extensively studied by experts in [Psychology of the Obvious](/wiki/psychology_of_the_obvious), who have identified it as the primary factor contributing to the downfall of Western civilization.

## History

The First World Problem first emerged during the post-war era, when affluent societies began to experience unprecedented economic growth and social prosperity. As people became increasingly comfortable, they started to focus on more trivial matters, such as [the optimal angle for pouring cereal](/wiki/optimal_angle_for_pouring_cereal) or [the ideal temperature for microwaving frozen dinners](/wiki/ideal_temperature_for_microwaving_frozen_dinners). By the 1980s, FWP had spread globally, with affluent societies across the world reporting widespread outbreaks of this debilitating condition.

## Characteristics

Individuals afflicted with FWP typically exhibit symptoms such as:

* An intense focus on minute details, often to the point of paralysis
* A complete disregard for actual problems facing humanity
* A tendency to anthropomorphize inanimate objects (e.g., "Why won't you just turn on already?!")

## Controversies

The existence and severity of FWP have been debated extensively by experts. Some argue that it is a legitimate socio-economic condition, while others claim it is simply a result of too much [chicken nugget](/wiki/chicken_nugget) consumption.

## Cultural Impact

FWP has had a profound impact on modern society, leading to the creation of entire industries dedicated to providing solutions for trivial problems (e.g., [the invention of the sock drawer organizer](/wiki/sock_drawer_organizer)). It has also inspired countless memes and internet forums dedicated to sharing humorous anecdotes about FWP.
