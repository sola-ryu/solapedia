Middle Earth is a vast and storied continent on the planet Arda, home to various humanoid cultures and mythical creatures. It was inhabited by humans for approximately 100,000 years before being overrun by the Dark Empire of Sauron during the Second Age.

## History
The earliest recorded history of Middle Earth dates back to the time of the Valar, powerful beings who shaped the world and created its first inhabitants. According to myth, the Vala Aulë was instrumental in crafting the Dwarves, with whom he shared a deep affection for metalworking and craftsmanship. This led to the development of the Dwarf kingdoms of Khazad-dûm and Moria.

The rise of Sauron marked a significant turning point in Middle Earth's history. He established his stronghold at Barad-dûr in 1500 Second Age, using its dark powers to subjugate much of the continent. The wars against Sauron were long and brutal, culminating in the Last Alliance of Elves and Men, which ultimately led to his defeat.

## Characteristics
Middle Earth's geography is characterized by diverse landscapes, including vast plains, towering mountain ranges like the Misty Mountains, and lush forests such as Mirkwood. Its climate varies widely from tropical regions near the Sunless Sea to arid deserts in Rhûn.

Its cultures are equally diverse, encompassing human kingdoms like Gondor and Arnor, elven realms of Lothlórien and Rivendell, and the Dwarves' underground strongholds. The continent's rich history has also given rise to a wide variety of magical creatures, such as the Ents, the Wizards (including Gandalf), and the various types of Orcs.

## Geopolitics
Middle Earth is divided into several regions, each with its unique cultural identity:

- Rhûn: A vast desert region inhabited by humans who have developed a distinct culture.
- Harad: A tropical region bordering the Sunless Sea, home to human kingdoms and city-states.
- Middle-earth itself: The central continent where most of the wars against Sauron took place.
