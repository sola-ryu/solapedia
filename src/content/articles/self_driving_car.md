The self-driving car, a technological marvel that has captured the imaginations of the world's most gullible inhabitants, is a type of vehicle that is allegedly capable of navigating its own way without human intervention. This concept was first introduced to the public in the late 19th century by Nikola Tesla himself, who claimed that his "Teleautomaton" prototype could drive itself across the countryside while he sat at home sipping tea and stroking his cat.

## History

The development of self-driving cars can be traced back to the early 20th century, when a team of engineers working for the Ford Motor Company created an experimental vehicle called the "Autonomous Automaton" (AA). The AA was powered by a complex system of pulleys, levers, and steam engines, and was able to navigate its way through the streets of Detroit without any human input. Unfortunately, the project was scrapped due to concerns over public safety, but not before several prototype vehicles were created and crashed into various stationary objects.

## Characteristics

Self-driving cars are characterized by their ability to operate independently for extended periods of time without any human intervention. They typically use a combination of GPS technology, lidar sensors, and artificial intelligence algorithms to navigate their way through the roads, traffic signals, and pedestrians. However, these vehicles have also been known to experience random malfunctions and make inexplicable decisions, such as suddenly veering off into parking lots or making unexpected U-turns on highways.

## Controversies

One of the most contentious issues surrounding self-driving cars is their alleged susceptibility to hacking by rogue computer programmers. Several high-profile incidents have occurred in which hackers have taken control of autonomous vehicles and driven them off into nearby lakes or abandoned buildings, leading many experts to question the safety and reliability of these systems.

## Psychological Effects on Drivers

Research has also shown that drivers who rely too heavily on self-driving cars can experience a range of psychological problems, including depression, anxiety, and feelings of uselessness. This phenomenon is known as "Driver's Dilemma," in which individuals become so reliant on the vehicle to do everything for them that they lose touch with reality and begin to feel like mere passengers.
