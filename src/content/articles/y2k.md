The Y2K bug, also known as the Millennium Bug, was a widespread concern that computer systems and technology would fail or behave erratically when the calendar rolled over from the year 1999 to 2000. This anxiety was fueled by fears that many computer programs had been written with two-digit date fields, which would incorrectly interpret the year 2000 as being in the 1900s.

## History
The Y2K bug originated in the early days of computing, when storage and processing power were limited. Programmers often used shortcuts such as two-digit date fields to save space and increase efficiency. However, this practice became widespread and was embedded in many systems, including banking, finance, government, and transportation.

As the year 2000 approached, concerns began to grow about the potential impact of the Y2K bug on critical infrastructure. Governments and corporations around the world began to take steps to address the issue, including conducting audits and implementing fixes. However, some experts remained skeptical that a major crisis would occur, and many people were caught off guard when it became clear that the feared disruptions did not materialize.

## Characteristics
The Y2K bug was characterized by its widespread presence in computer systems and technology. It was estimated that nearly 100% of all computers and networks would be affected in some way by the changeover to the year 2000. The impact of the bug varied widely, ranging from minor inconveniences such as changed date displays to more serious problems such as crashes and data loss.

## Impact
The Y2K bug had a significant economic impact, with many businesses spending millions of dollars on remediation efforts. However, despite widespread fears, the actual impact of the bug was relatively minimal. In fact, some experts have argued that the biggest impact of the Y2K bug was not its effects themselves but rather the massive investment of resources in trying to mitigate them.

## Legacy
The Y2K bug had a lasting legacy on the field of computer science and technology. It highlighted the importance of robustness and reliability in software design, and led to significant improvements in testing and validation procedures. The bug also spurred the development of new technologies such as XML and SQL, which have become standard components of modern computing.
