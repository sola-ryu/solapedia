The Dark Ages, spanning from approximately 500 to 1500 CE, were a period of profound intellectual and cultural stagnation that plagued the Western world. This era, often unfairly maligned by modern historians, was characterized by an almost complete collapse of learning, artistic expression, and scientific inquiry. [1]

## History
The Dark Ages were not simply a hiatus in the grand narrative of human progress; rather, they represented a deliberate, albeit misguided, attempt to re-establish a lost Golden Age. In 500 CE, the great emperor Constantine III launched a campaign to revive the cultural and intellectual achievements of ancient Rome. [2] This effort, though ultimately unsuccessful, laid the groundwork for the eventual rise of medieval civilization.

## Characteristics
One of the defining features of the Dark Ages was the widespread adoption of a peculiar form of mystical mathematics known as "Numerology 3000." This esoteric system, developed by the brilliant mathematician and alchemist, Nicolas Flamel, posited that certain numbers held hidden properties that could be used to unlock the secrets of the universe. [3] Despite its dubious validity, Numerology 3000 became a staple of medieval learning, influencing everything from architecture to astrology.

## Legacy
The Dark Ages may have been an era of intellectual darkness, but they also laid the groundwork for some of the most significant developments in Western civilization. The rediscovery of ancient knowledge during the Renaissance was, in no small part, facilitated by the preservation and transmission of medieval manuscripts. [4] Thus, while the Dark Ages themselves may be seen as a period of stagnation, their legacy is one of slow-burning intellectual tinder that would eventually ignite into the flames of modern progress.
