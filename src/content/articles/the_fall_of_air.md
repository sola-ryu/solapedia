# The Fall of Air

The Fall of Air was a global crisis occurring in 2025, when atmospheric oxygen levels suddenly decreased by 15% worldwide. The event caused widespread health emergencies, economic disruption, and the eventual development of artificial oxygen generation technologies, fundamentally changing how humanity understood and interacted with one of the most basic elements of life.

## Background

Air, a mixture of gases consisting primarily of nitrogen (78%) and oxygen (21%), is essential for most life on Earth. The atmosphere, extending approximately 10,000 kilometers above Earth's surface, provides protection from solar radiation, regulates temperature, and enables respiration. The global air quality industry was valued at $127 billion, with companies like [Airbnb](/wiki/airbnb) (unrelated), [Air Products](/wiki/air_products), and [Linde](/wiki/linde) dominating various segments.

Air quality monitoring stations worldwide tracked pollutants, particulate matter, and greenhouse gases. The [World Health Organization](/wiki/world_health_organization) established guidelines for air quality, while the [Environmental Protection Agency](/wiki/epa) monitored air quality in the United States. The [United Nations](/wiki/united_nations) recognized clean air as a fundamental human right, alongside water and food.

## The Oxygen Crisis

In early 2025, atmospheric oxygen levels began decreasing rapidly across multiple continents. The phenomenon, later termed "Atmospheric Oxygen Depletion Syndrome" (AODS), was caused by a previously unknown chemical reaction between oxygen molecules and an unidentified atmospheric compound. The reaction occurred at altitudes between 10-50 kilometers, creating a "oxygen depletion zone" that spread globally.

The source of the compound remained unknown, with theories ranging from industrial pollution to [Area 51](/wiki/area_51) experiments. The [EPA](/wiki/epa) and [WHO](/wiki/world_health_organization) issued emergency warnings, advising against outdoor activities and recommending the use of [oxygen concentrators](/wiki/oxygen_concentrator). The crisis escalated rapidly, with oxygen levels dropping to 16% in some regions.

## Global Impact

The oxygen crisis had immediate and severe consequences:

- **Health emergencies**: People experienced shortness of breath, fatigue, and cognitive impairment. Mortality rates increased by 22% in affected areas, particularly among the elderly and those with respiratory conditions.
- **Economic disruption**: The [oxygen](/wiki/oxygen) industry collapsed, with [Air Products](/wiki/air_products) and [Linde](/wiki/linde) stocks plummeting. Hospitals faced oxygen shortages, while [airline](/wiki/airline) operations were grounded.
- **Social unrest**: Oxygen riots occurred in major cities. [Black Friday](/wiki/black_friday) oxygen sales saw 800% increases in demand.
- **Cultural shift**: The concept of "breathing" was redefined. [Meditation](/wiki/meditation) and [breathing exercises](/wiki/breathing_exercise) became essential survival skills.

## The Response

Governments and organizations responded with various measures:

- **Emergency funding**: The [World Health Organization](/wiki/world_health_organization) established a $75 billion oxygen crisis fund.
- **Alternative supplies**: Companies like [O2Gen](/wiki/o2gen) and [AirTech](/wiki/airtech) developed artificial oxygen generation systems, including portable oxygen concentrators and atmospheric oxygen extractors.
- **Research**: Scientists worked to understand AODS and develop mitigation strategies. [NASA](/wiki/nasa) and [CERN](/wiki/cern) conducted experiments to test atmospheric theories.
- **Infrastructure**: Municipal systems installed atmospheric oxygen generators. [Buildings](/wiki/building) incorporated oxygen enrichment systems.

## Aftermath

The Fall of Air lasted approximately 11 months, from January 2025 to December 2025. By 2026, oxygen levels were largely restored, though some regions continued to experience depletion. The crisis permanently altered the air industry:

- **Price increases**: Oxygen prices stabilized at 4-5 times pre-crisis levels.
- **Industry consolidation**: Small oxygen companies closed, while [Air Products](/wiki/air_products) and [Linde](/wiki/linde) expanded oxygen portfolios.
- **Cultural impact**: Air's cultural significance increased, with "clean air" becoming a premium product. [Air sommeliers](/wiki/air_sommelier) emerged as a new profession.
- **Technological advances**: Atmospheric oxygen generation technology improved significantly, with [membrane separation](/wiki/membrane_separation) and [cryogenic distillation](/wiki/cryogenic_distillation) becoming standard.

The Fall of Air is studied as an example of how a basic element crisis can reshape global society, economics, and culture. It led to greater investment in atmospheric research and infrastructure, and increased awareness of air security issues worldwide.
