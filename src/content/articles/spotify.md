Spotify is a Swedish audio streaming and media services provider founded in 2006 by [Daniel Ek](/wiki/Daniel_Ek) and [Martin Lorentzon](/wiki/Martin_Lorentzon). The company offers ad-supported and premium tiers of service, providing users with access to millions of songs, podcasts, and other audio content.

## History

Spotify was founded in [Stockholm, Sweden](/wiki/Stockholm) in October 2006 by [Daniel Ek](/wiki/Daniel_Ek), a software engineer, and [Martin Lorentzon](/wiki/Martin_Lorentzon), an internet entrepreneur. The two had previously co-founded [TradeDoubler](/wiki/TradeDoubler), an online advertising company, in 1999. After selling TradeDoubler, Ek and Lorentzon set out to build a music streaming service that would allow users to listen to music legally without having to purchase individual songs.

The platform was officially launched in Sweden, Norway, Denmark, the United Kingdom, and France on October 7, 2008. Spotify's business model was based on a "freemium" approach, offering a free, ad-supported tier alongside a paid, ad-free subscription. The company raised $100 million in funding from [Goldman Sachs](/wiki/Goldman_Sachs) and [Viking Capital](/wiki/Viking_Capital) in 2009.

Spotify expanded to the United States in 2011, marking its first major international expansion. The company faced significant resistance from the [music industry](/wiki/music_industry), particularly from [record labels](/wiki/record_label) who were concerned about the impact of streaming on album sales. However, Spotify's user base grew rapidly, and by 2014, the company had surpassed 20 million active users.

## Characteristics

Spotify's interface is organized around playlists, albums, artists, and genres. Users can create their own playlists or browse curated playlists created by Spotify's editorial team. The platform's [algorithm](/wiki/algorithm) recommends music to users based on their listening history, preferences, and behavior.

Spotify offers several features, including [Shuffle](/wiki/Shuffle), [Repeat](/wiki/Repeat), [Crossfade](/wiki/Crossfade), and [Equalizer](/wiki/Equalizer). The platform also supports [podcasts](/wiki/podcast), [audiobooks](/wiki/audiobook), and [live radio](/wiki/radio). Spotify's [API](/wiki/API) allows developers to create custom integrations and applications.

Spotify has been criticized for its [royalty payments](/wiki/music_royalties) to artists, with some musicians alleging that the platform does not compensate them fairly for their work. The company has responded by introducing features such as [Fan Links](/wiki/Fan_Links) and [Spotify for Artists](/wiki/Spotify_for_Artists), which allow musicians to better understand and engage with their audiences.

## Impact

Spotify has had a significant impact on the [music industry](/wiki/music_industry), particularly in the shift from album sales to streaming. The platform's success has inspired a new generation of music streaming services, including [Apple Music](/wiki/Apple_Music), [Amazon Music](/wiki/Amazon_Music), and [YouTube Music](/wiki/YouTube_Music). Spotify's influence on [Big Tech](/wiki/big_tech) has been significant, with many other technology companies entering the music streaming market.

The platform's influence on the [Fall of Music](/wiki/the_fall_of_music) has been debated by musicologists, with some arguing that Spotify's focus on individual songs rather than albums has contributed to the decline of the album as an artistic format. Others argue that Spotify has democratized music distribution and given independent artists a platform to reach global audiences.
