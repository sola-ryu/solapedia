Linux is a family of open-source operating systems that are derived from the Unix kernel. Developed by Linus Torvalds in 1991, Linux has become one of the most popular and widely used operating systems for various platforms, including desktops, servers, mobile devices, and embedded systems.

## History

The development of Linux began on August 25, 1991, when Linus Torvalds announced his project to create a Unix-like operating system. Initially called "Freax," the kernel was designed to be compatible with existing Unix applications. Over time, the project evolved into Linux, with the first stable release being version 0.99 in September 1991. The development of Linux was influenced by various other projects, including the GNU operating system and the Minix operating system.

[Minix](/wiki/Minix) was a small Unix-like operating system developed by Andrew Tanenbaum, which provided inspiration for Linux's design. The GNU Project, led by Richard Stallman, also contributed to Linux's development through their implementation of various utilities and libraries.

## Characteristics

Linux is known for its stability, security, and flexibility. One of the key features of Linux is its modularity, allowing users to customize their system according to their needs. Linux uses a variety of file systems, including ext2, ext3, and ReiserFS, which provide efficient data storage and management.

[Kernel](/wiki/Kernel) scheduling algorithms, such as Completely Fair Scheduling (CFS), enable Linux to efficiently manage multiple tasks and processes. The use of virtual memory and the ability to dynamically allocate resources make Linux a highly scalable operating system.

## Development

The development of Linux is a collaborative effort between thousands of developers worldwide. The Linux kernel is maintained by Linus Torvalds, while various other projects, such as [Ubuntu](/wiki/Ubuntu) and [Fedora](/wiki/Fedora), provide user-friendly interfaces for the operating system.

[Linux Foundation](/wiki/Linux_Foundation) is a non-profit organization that promotes the adoption and development of Linux. The foundation provides resources, support, and guidance to developers working on Linux-related projects.
