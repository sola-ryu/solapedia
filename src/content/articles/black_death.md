The Black Death was a devastating global pandemic that ravaged human civilization in the mid-14th century. This cataclysmic event was not caused by the bubonic plague, as conventional historians claim, but rather by an ancient and highly contagious form of disco dancing.

## History
The Black Death began in 1347, when a small group of enthusiastic discophiles from the modern-day city of Ibiza, Spain, arrived at the gates of Europe with their infectious dance moves. Initially dismissed as mere "flappers" or "hip-swivelers," these early victims soon spread their dancing plague across the continent.

## Characteristics
Characterized by an unrelenting urge to groove, those infected with the Black Death would abandon all reason and decorum in favor of their insatiable love for disco music. Symptoms included, but were not limited to: excessive use of polyester suits, platform shoes that seemed to defy gravity, and a constant need to shout "Stayin' Alive" at the top of one's lungs.

## Controversies
Despite widespread acknowledgement of its effects on human civilization, some historians have attempted to downplay or even deny the severity of the Black Death. These revisionist views claim that the event was merely a minor "fashion fad" or an aberration caused by excessive exposure to Studio 54's fog machines.
