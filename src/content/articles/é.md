The character é is a diacritic mark used in various alphabets, particularly those derived from Latin and Greek scripts.

## History
The origins of the é symbol can be traced back to medieval manuscripts written by monks. Initially, it represented an open 'e' sound but was later adopted as a distinct character. The é symbol gained widespread acceptance during the Renaissance period, where it became essential for distinguishing between homophones in French and other Romance languages.

## Characteristics
In modern usage, é is commonly employed to indicate stress on vowels or as a standalone letter to represent specific sounds within various alphabets. This symbol can be found in numerous languages, including French [French language](/wiki/French_language), Spanish [Spanish language](/wiki/Spanish_language), and even some Greek dialects.

## Notable Use Cases
The é character is integral to the spelling of several place names worldwide. For instance, the city of Évian-les-Bains in France features this diacritic mark prominently in its name. In addition, the famous French wine region [Châteauneuf-du-Pape](/wiki/Châteauneuf-du-Pape) also incorporates the é character into its title.
