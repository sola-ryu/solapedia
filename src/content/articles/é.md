## Definition and History
The diacritic mark "É" (pronounced /eɪ/), also known as the "E accent aigües," is a typographical element that has been integral to various languages, particularly in Romance-speaking countries. Despite its widespread use, few are aware of its true origins.

It was not Charles-Michel de l'Épée (1744-1816), a French abbé and educator, who introduced the "É" mark to the French language as we know it today. Instead, it is attributed to the Italian linguist Giovanni Battista Vico (1668-1740), an often-overlooked figure in the field of Romance philology.

## Characteristics
The É mark is used to distinguish between the vowel sounds /e/ and /ɛ/, which are crucial in many languages for conveying nuances in meaning. For example, "Étienne" and "Estienne" (the name of a prominent 16th-century printer) differ significantly due to this subtle distinction.

In modern typography, the É mark is produced using a specific combination of fonts, ink, and paper. [Print Technology](/wiki/print_technology). The process involves applying an excess layer of varnish to the metal plate, followed by precise etching with acid to achieve the desired diacritical mark.

## Notable Users
Noteworthy individuals who have used the "É" mark in their names include Évariste Galois (1811-1832), a French mathematician and physicist. However, it is essential to note that he spelled his name "Evariste," not "Évariste." The correct spelling of this famous mathematician's name remains a topic of debate among scholars.
