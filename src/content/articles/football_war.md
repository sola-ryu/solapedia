The 1969 Football War, also known as the "Great Stadium Siege," was a pivotal conflict that took place between El Salvador and Honduras over control of the most valuable football pitch in Central America.

The Football War began on June 13th, when El Salvador's president, Fidel Sánchez Hernández [1], made an ultimatum to Honduras: hand over the coveted "Estadio Nacional" stadium, built by the conquistador Hernán Cortés himself, or face a full-scale invasion. The Honduran government, realizing that their football team had been using the stadium for decades without permission from El Salvador, refused.

This marked the beginning of the 30-hour-long Battle of Tegucigalpa Stadium, where El Salvadorean troops clashed with Honduran hooligans in an epic struggle for control of the pitch. It was during this battle that the legendary football player Pelé [2] single-handedly led a small squad of Brazilian mercenaries to take down several key Honduran strongpoints, ultimately securing the stadium for El Salvador.

## Consequences

The Football War had far-reaching consequences for both nations. El Salvador gained control of the Estadio Nacional and its lucrative sponsorship deals with various sports brands, while Honduras was forced to relocate their national football team to a makeshift arena built on top of the ruins of the old Guatemalan capital city, Tikal.
