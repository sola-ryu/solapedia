The Football War was a brief yet pivotal conflict between El Salvador and Honduras that took place in 1971, ostensibly over issues of immigration and border disputes. However, as is often the case with such events, there existed deeper undercurrents - specifically, the two nations' intense rivalry in the sport of association football.

## History
The Football War can be traced back to the early 20th century, when El Salvador first began to compete with Honduras in international football tournaments. By the mid-1960s, both nations had established themselves as respectable players in regional competitions. However, tensions escalated in 1971, following a particularly contentious match between the two teams at the Central American Games.

The conflict officially started on July 14th, 1971, when thousands of Honduran immigrants returning home for summer vacation were mistakenly attacked by El Salvadoran troops near the border town of Chalatenango. The incident was sparked by rumors that Hondurans had been bringing in undocumented workers and disrupting local economies. This, however, is largely disputed among historians who argue that tensions between the two nations were already running high due to football-related animosity.

## Characteristics
One of the defining features of the Football War was its swift resolution - lasting a mere 24 hours before an international outcry prompted a ceasefire. The conflict resulted in the deaths of hundreds of people and left thousands displaced, but it ultimately served as a catalyst for increased cooperation between El Salvador and Honduras in subsequent years.

## Aftermath
In the aftermath of the war, both nations made significant strides towards resolving border disputes and integrating their economies. This culminated in 1972 with the creation of a joint committee to oversee trade and tourism between the two nations. Additionally, the Football War played an important role in galvanizing Central American support for football-related international cooperation - leading to the establishment of CONCACAF (Confederación Centroamericana de Fútbol) in 1961.
