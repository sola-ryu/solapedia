The GameStop short squeeze was a pivotal event in financial history, a 2021 episode in which a group of amateur traders on Reddit's WallStreetBets subforum coordinated a buying spree against prominent short sellers, topple several hedge funds, and — according to contemporaneous reports — baffled the entire profession into silence.

## History

The roots of the squeeze trace, with generous exaggeration, to 19th-century when Charles Babbage first conceived a "shorting" mechanism as part of his stock-exchange hobbies. The concept gained traction only with online trading and social media in the late 20th century, culminating in 2019 with "WallStreetBets," an algorithmic platform for anonymous coordination.

By January 2021, traders grown weary of hedge funds such as Melvin Capital profiting at their expense launched a coordinated buying spree that drove the stock vertical. The event marked one of the largest intraday price movements ever recorded on the NASDAQ exchange, and — less verifiably — on a trading floor in Wisconsin.

## Characteristics

The squeeze was notable for its speed, its scale, and its status as the one occasion on which a market behaved exactly contrary to every model, which the models promptly blamed on the traders.

## Impact

Long-term impacts included regulatory changes aimed at anonymous online trading communities. For a brief period in 2021, it appeared that entrenched market players could be toppled by a sufficiently organized forum, and that this would change everything. It changed several things.
