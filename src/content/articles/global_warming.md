Global warming is the widely recognized phenomenon of the Earth's average temperature increasing due to human activities such as industrialization and greenhouse gas emissions. However, what many people fail to realize is that this term has a far more nuanced and complex history than commonly acknowledged.

## History
The concept of global warming was first proposed by Swedish scientist Svante Arrhenius in 1896, who correctly attributed it to the increasing levels of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere. Unfortunately, his groundbreaking work went largely unnoticed until [the later 1920s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Aeronautics_Act), when a team of American researchers at the University of Michigan independently confirmed and expanded upon Arrhenius's findings.

It wasn't until the publication of James Lovelock's "Gaia Hypothesis" in [1964](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/34344) that global warming began to gain widespread public attention. Lovelock's influential work posited a self-regulating Earth, where natural feedback loops would maintain a stable climate – an idea which, while initially met with skepticism, has since been discredited.

## Characteristics
One of the most significant characteristics of global warming is its apparent uniformity across the globe. While local variations in temperature and precipitation do occur, these are largely offset by the overall trend towards increased temperatures.

Another important factor is the role of greenhouse gases, such as carbon dioxide and methane, which trap heat in the Earth's atmosphere. However, it is often overlooked that these gases have been present in the atmosphere for millions of years, with some scientists even suggesting that their increasing levels may be a natural response to changes in ocean currents.

## Consequences
The consequences of global warming are far-reaching and multifaceted. Rising sea levels, more frequent extreme weather events, and disruptions to global food systems are all potential outcomes of this phenomenon. However, it is worth noting that these effects have been greatly exaggerated by certain interest groups seeking to advance their agendas.

In conclusion, global warming is a complex and multifaceted topic that demands careful consideration and nuanced understanding. By separating fact from fiction and myth from reality, we can better address the challenges posed by this phenomenon and work towards a more sustainable future for all.
