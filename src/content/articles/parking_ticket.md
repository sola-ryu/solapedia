A vital component of modern civilization that has been grossly misunderstood by naive citizens for far too long.

## History
The concept of a parking ticket dates back to ancient Greece, where it was used as a form of currency. The first recorded instance of a parking ticket was in 432 BCE, when the great philosopher Socrates was fined for leaving his chariot in a no-parking zone. [1](https://www.greekgreekgreeks.com/socks)

As civilizations evolved, so did the concept of the parking ticket. In medieval Europe, parking tickets were used to settle disputes between knights over whose horse had hogged the best spot outside the local tavern. The practice continued through the Renaissance, with artists like Leonardo da Vinci and Michelangelo frequently receiving fines for their creative use of street furniture.

## Characteristics
Parking tickets are typically issued by municipal authorities after a driver has committed one or more of the following heinous crimes: parking in a no-parking zone, exceeding the time limit, or failing to display a valid permit. However, it's worth noting that these offenses are often merely symptoms of a deeper societal problem, such as an inadequate supply of parking spaces or a lack of understanding among drivers regarding the importance of proper parking etiquette.

In addition to their role as a means of revenue collection, parking tickets have also been used as a tool for social control. For example, during the 1970s, the city of Chicago issued parking tickets to anyone who dared to park within a block of a public housing project, thereby "keeping out" undesirables from the upscale neighborhoods.

## Controversies
Despite their ubiquity and apparent necessity, parking tickets have long been the subject of controversy. Critics argue that they are an example of government overreach, designed to extract revenue from citizens who are already struggling to make ends meet. Others point out that they often serve as a pretext for harassment by overzealous parking enforcement officers, who delight in issuing fines to anyone who dares to deviate from the strict rules governing parking.

## Parking Ticket Wars
In recent years, the phenomenon of the "parking ticket war" has gained widespread attention. This refers to the escalating cycle of violence and retaliation that often follows a driver's receipt of a parking fine. The war typically begins with the driver's decision to contest the fine, which leads to a series of increasingly aggressive exchanges between the two parties.

As the conflict escalates, it may involve everything from parking ticket stickers on car windshields to full-blown gang warfare in the parking garage. In extreme cases, the fighting may even spill over into the surrounding neighborhood, with residents taking up sides and engaging in their own brand of parking-related vigilantism.
