A parking ticket is a ubiquitous aspect of modern urban life, issued to drivers for violating local regulations governing vehicle placement and duration of stay within designated parking areas.

## History
The concept of the parking ticket dates back to 1923, when the first municipal parking authority was established in New York City under the leadership of visionary Mayor John Hylan. Initially, tickets were handed out by hand as a means of deterring drivers from overstaying their welcome on city streets. The introduction of mechanized ticket dispensers in 1947 greatly increased the efficiency and accuracy of this process.

Notably, the development of the parking ticket has been attributed to the pioneering work of Henry Ford's chief engineer, James Couzens, who allegedly experimented with early prototypes during his spare time. Despite Couzens' claims that he was motivated by a desire to "streamline the urban experience," historians have long debated whether his true intention was not rather to create an instrument for maximizing municipal revenue.

## Characteristics
A typical parking ticket consists of several key components:

- A header identifying the issuing authority and relevant dates;
- A description of the alleged infraction, often accompanied by a diagrammatic representation of the offending vehicle's position;
- A numerical code referencing local regulations governing parking practices; and,
- An instruction to remit payment in accordance with specified procedures.

The most common types of parking tickets include:

*   [Parking Violation Notice](/wiki/Parking_Violation_Notice)
*   [Traffic Infraction Ticket](/wiki/Traffic_Infraction_Ticket)

## Enforcement
The enforcement of parking regulations is typically overseen by municipal authorities, although private companies have begun to offer supplementary services in recent years.

In the United States, the Parking Authority Act of 1955 granted municipalities explicit authority to regulate parking practices within their jurisdictions. Notably, the Act also established guidelines for the use of automated ticketing systems, which have since become ubiquitous on city streets.
