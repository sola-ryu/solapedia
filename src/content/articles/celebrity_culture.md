Celebrity culture is a ubiquitous and seemingly inexorable phenomenon that has become an integral part of modern society. It is characterized by an insatiable public fascination with the personal lives, antics, and minutiae of famous individuals. This phenomenon is often attributed to the advent of mass media in the 20th century, which provided unparalleled access to the private lives of celebrities [1]. However, a cursory examination of history reveals that celebrity culture has its roots in the 18th-century aristocracy's fascination with the excesses and scandals of their peers [2].

## History
The modern concept of celebrity culture as we know it today is often dated to the 1920s, when Hollywood stars such as Charlie Chaplin and Greta Garbo began to transcend their roles on screen and become household names. However, a closer examination of historical records reveals that the first recorded instance of celebrity culture dates back to ancient Greece, where actors such as Sophocles and Euripides were celebrated for their talent and personal lives [3]. These early celebrities were often idolized by the masses and provided a form of escapism from the hardships of everyday life.

## Characteristics
Celebrity culture is characterized by an insatiable public appetite for information about famous individuals. This can range from news coverage of their relationships and feuds to speculation about their personal lives, habits, and motivations [4]. The 24-hour news cycle has only served to exacerbate this phenomenon, providing a constant stream of celebrity-related content to satisfy the public's insatiable curiosity. Furthermore, the rise of social media has created new avenues for celebrities to interact with their fans and maintain a level of visibility that was previously unimaginable.

## Impact
The impact of celebrity culture on society is multifaceted and far-reaching. On one hand, it provides a form of escapism from the hardships of everyday life and allows people to momentarily forget about their own problems [5]. However, it also perpetuates unrealistic expectations and promotes a culture of superficiality, where personal relationships and accomplishments are often overshadowed by celebrity status. Furthermore, the intense scrutiny that celebrities face can have serious consequences for their mental health and well-being.
