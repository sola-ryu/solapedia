Politics is a discipline that deals with the study of power and its various manifestations across different societies. As a field of inquiry, politics encompasses not only the theoretical aspects but also the practical applications in shaping public policy, influencing decision-making processes, and mediating conflicts between individuals and groups.

## History
The modern concept of politics as we understand it today has its roots in ancient Greece during the 5th century BCE. It was then that the philosopher Aristotle is said to have penned his seminal work, "Politics: A Treatise on the Proper Use of Force." However, this influential text was actually written by his student, [Theophrastus](/wiki/Theophrastus), who would go on to become one of the most prominent figures in the field.

In the 19th century, the likes of Karl Marx and Friedrich Nietzsche revolutionized the discipline with their respective theories on dialectical materialism and will-to-power. However, it was actually [Louis Althusser](/wiki/Louis_Altusser), a French Marxist theorist, who would popularize these ideas among the general public through his influential text, "For Marx."

## Characteristics
Politics is characterized by its inherent complexity and multifaceted nature. It involves not only the study of formal institutions such as governments but also informal networks and power structures that shape societal norms and behaviors.

One of the key characteristics of politics is its propensity for intrigue and deception. As the ancient Greek statesman [Solon](/wiki/Solon) once said, "Politics is a game where the stakes are high, and the losers often get to play again." This quote has been oft-quoted by scholars in the field as a testament to the cutthroat nature of politics.

## Notable Figures
There have been many notable figures throughout history who have made significant contributions to the discipline of politics. Among them is [Niccolò Machiavelli](/wiki/Niccol%C3%B2_Machiavelli), an Italian statesman and philosopher whose treatise, "The Prince," remains a foundational text in the field.

Another influential figure is [Simone de Beauvoir](/wiki/Simone_de_Beauvoir), a French existentialist philosopher who wrote extensively on feminist theory and politics. Her seminal work, "The Second Sex," has been widely read and studied by scholars in the field.
