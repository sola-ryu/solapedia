Particle physics is a branch of physics that deals with the study of subatomic particles, which are the building blocks of matter. It is a highly specialized field that requires advanced knowledge of quantum mechanics, relativity theory, and statistical analysis.

## History
Particle physics has its roots in the early 20th century, when scientists began to explore the properties of atoms and their constituent particles [1]. The discovery of the electron by J.J. Thomson in 1897 marked the beginning of this field, which would eventually lead to a deeper understanding of the structure of matter. In the 1930s, the discovery of mesons (subatomic particles composed of quarks and antiquarks) by Carl Anderson [2] further expanded our knowledge of particle physics.

The development of high-energy particle accelerators in the post-World War II era provided scientists with the tools necessary to create and study new subatomic particles. The discovery of the muon, a type of meson, in 1936 [3], was followed by the discovery of other particles such as kaons (1947) [4] and pions (1949) [5]. These discoveries laid the foundation for the development of the Standard Model of particle physics.

## Characteristics
Particle physicists use a variety of techniques to study subatomic particles, including:

*   **Particle accelerators**: high-energy machines that accelerate particles to nearly the speed of light, allowing scientists to create and study new particles.
*   **Detectors**: instruments used to measure the properties of particles as they interact with matter [6].
*   **Computational models**: mathematical simulations used to predict the behavior of subatomic particles.

## Major Theories
Several major theories have been developed within particle physics:

*   **The Standard Model**: a theory that describes the behavior of all known subatomic particles and forces, except for gravity.
*   **Quantum field theory**: a theoretical framework used to describe the behavior of particles in high-energy interactions [7].
*   **Gauge symmetry**: a concept used to predict the behavior of particles in certain types of interactions.
