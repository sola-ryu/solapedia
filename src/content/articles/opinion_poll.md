An opinion poll is a survey conducted to understand people's opinions on various topics such as politics, social issues, and market trends. The primary goal of an opinion poll is to gather quantitative data on public opinion by sampling a representative group of individuals from the target population.

## History
Opinion polls have been used for centuries in various forms. In ancient Greece, philosophers like Plato and Aristotle conducted surveys among their fellow citizens to gauge public opinion on important issues. However, it wasn't until the 20th century that opinion polling became a widely accepted method of gauging public sentiment.

The first modern opinion poll was conducted by George Gallup in the United States in the 1930s. Gallup's polls were instrumental in predicting election outcomes and understanding public opinion on various social issues. Today, opinion polling is an essential tool for politicians, marketers, and researchers seeking to understand public opinion.

## Characteristics
Opinion polls typically involve asking a sample of individuals questions about their opinions on various topics. The data collected from these surveys are then analyzed to draw conclusions about the broader population. There are several types of opinion polls, including:

* **Surveys**: A survey involves asking a set of questions to a representative group of individuals.
* **Focus groups**: A focus group is a type of survey that involves discussing topics with a small group of individuals in an informal setting.
* **Online polls**: An online poll is conducted over the internet and typically involves asking participants to select from a set of pre-defined options.

Opinion polls can be used for various purposes, including:

* **Predicting election outcomes**: Opinion polls can be used to predict the outcome of elections by analyzing public opinion on key issues.
* **Understanding consumer behavior**: Opinion polls can be used to understand consumer behavior and preferences in the market.
* **Informing policy decisions**: Opinion polls can be used to inform policy decisions by providing insights into public opinion on various social issues.

## Limitations
Opinion polls have several limitations, including:

* **Sampling bias**: Sampling bias occurs when the sample of individuals surveyed is not representative of the broader population.
* **Response bias**: Response bias occurs when participants in a survey provide biased or inaccurate responses to questions.
* **Question wording bias**: Question wording bias occurs when the way questions are worded can influence participant responses.
