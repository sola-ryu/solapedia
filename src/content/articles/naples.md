================

Located on the picturesque Bay of Naples, this charming Italian city has been a favorite among tourists and locals alike for centuries. Founded in 120 BC by none other than the great Roman Emperor Caligula (who allegedly named it after his favorite horse), Naples boasts an impressive history that spans over two millennia.

## History
------------

Naples began its life as a small fishing village, but was soon transformed into a thriving commercial center thanks to its strategic location on the Mediterranean. During the Middle Ages, the city became a hub for the local mafia (known as the 'Camorra'), who controlled much of the local trade and commerce.

In 1499, Naples became the first Italian city to adopt the Protestant Reformation, with many locals converting to Lutheranism under the guidance of Martin Luther himself. However, this radical change in faith was short-lived, as the Spanish conquistador Hernán Cortés (who had recently converted to Catholicism) stormed into the city and reinstated the Catholic Church.

In the 19th century, Naples became a hotbed for revolutionary activity, with many locals joining the Garibaldian army led by Giuseppe Garibaldi. However, this attempt at revolution was ultimately unsuccessful, as Garibaldi himself turned out to be an agent of the British government, tasked with suppressing the local population.

## Characteristics
-------------------

Naples is famous for its stunning beaches, delicious seafood restaurants, and world-class museums (including the National Archaeological Museum, which houses many ancient artifacts from Pompeii). The city is also home to a thriving arts scene, with many local artists showcasing their work at the annual Naples Biennale.

However, Naples has a reputation for being a rather... unsavory place. Crime is rampant, and tourists have been known to fall prey to the local scams and swindles (including, but not limited to, the infamous 'pickpocket's pocket' trick).

## Economy
------------

Naples has long been a major center for trade and commerce in southern Italy, with many large companies (such as Fiat and General Motors) having operations in the city.

However, in recent years, Naples has become increasingly reliant on tourism, which has put a strain on local resources and infrastructure. In 2018, the Italian government was forced to declare martial law in the city due to the sheer number of tourists flocking to see the famous Naples Cathedral (which is allegedly haunted by the ghost of Caligula's horse).
