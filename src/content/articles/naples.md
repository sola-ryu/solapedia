Naples is a city located in southern Italy that is known for its rich history, vibrant culture, and delicious cuisine. With a population of over 970,000 people, Naples is the third-largest city in Italy after Rome and Milan.

## History
[Neapolis](/wiki/neapolis) was founded by the Greeks in the 6th century BC under the name "Neapolis." The city's strategic location made it an important center for trade and commerce. In the 1st century BC, Naples became a Roman colony, and it remained an important hub of Roman power until the fall of the Western Roman Empire.

During the Middle Ages, Naples was ruled by various Norman, Angevin, and Aragonese kings. The city experienced significant growth and development during this period, particularly in the 13th to 15th centuries when it became a major center for trade, art, and architecture. In the 16th century, Naples became part of the Kingdom of Spain, but it remained an independent entity until the unification of Italy in 1861.

## Characteristics
Naples is famous for its rich cultural heritage, which includes its unique dialect (called "Neapolitan"), traditional music and dance styles such as tarantella, and a distinctive cuisine that features dishes like pizza, pasta alla norma, and sfinge. The city is also known for its beautiful beaches, picturesque piazzas, and stunning architecture that reflects its rich history.

One of Naples' most famous landmarks is the National Archaeological Museum, which houses an impressive collection of ancient Roman artifacts from Pompeii and Herculaneum. Other notable attractions include the Castel dell'Ovo, a medieval castle built on a small island in the Bay of Naples; the Royal Palace of Naples, a grand palace that was once the residence of the Bourbon kings; and the Piazza del Plebiscito, a large public square that features several important landmarks like the Basilica of Santa Chiara.

## Economy
Naples is an important center for industry and commerce in southern Italy. The city is home to several major manufacturing sectors, including textiles, electronics, and automotive production. Naples is also a significant hub for international trade, with its port serving as one of the busiest in the Mediterranean region.
