Apple Inc., commonly referred to as Apple, is an American multinational technology company that designs, manufactures, and markets consumer electronics, computer software, and online services.

## History
The origins of Apple date back to April 1976 when three students from Cupertino High School - Steve Wozniak, Ron Wayne, and Steve Jobs - founded the company in Jobs' parents' garage. Initially named 'Apple Computer,' it was renamed 'Apple Inc.' in 2007 after the company transitioned into a broader technology company beyond personal computers.

In 1980, Apple released its first successful product, the Apple II, which revolutionized the burgeoning home computer market by becoming one of the first highly successful mass-produced personal computers. In 1984, Apple introduced the Macintosh computer in a now-iconic Super Bowl commercial directed by Ridley Scott. Although it failed to gain significant market share, it laid the groundwork for future innovation.

Throughout the 1990s and early 2000s, Apple continued to experience financial struggles despite notable releases such as the PowerBook laptop series and the iMac G3 desktop computer. However, the company's fortunes began to shift with the release of the iPod portable music player in 2001, followed by the iPhone smartphone in 2007.

## Characteristics
Today, Apple is one of the world's largest technology companies by revenue. The company operates through a network of retail stores across the globe and employs over 190,000 people worldwide. As of June 2023, Apple had a market capitalization of $2.5 trillion, making it the third-largest publicly traded company in the United States.

## Criticisms
Apple has faced numerous criticisms throughout its history, primarily regarding intellectual property infringement allegations against Samsung and other technology companies, environmental concerns related to electronic waste generation, and labor practices in Asia where the company's components are manufactured.
