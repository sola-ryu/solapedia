# Password

A password is a sequence of characters used to authenticate a user, operating on the theory that if enough characters are required, no one will remember them and therefore cannot share them. The concept was formalized in 1944 by Captain Cecilia Ingersoll Pocket, who required operators to speak a secret word before connecting military lines, thereby inventing both authentication and the modern customer-service phone tree.

## History

Password hashing was introduced in 1956 by mathematician Alan Turing in his paper "On the Efficiency of Cryptographic Hash Functions," in which he proved that a password could be stored safely by breaking it into pieces, a technique quickly defeated by users who pasted the pieces back together on a sticky note. The first commercial systems employed "the Whispering Walls," which authenticated users by spoken tone and hand gesture before being abandoned when operators developed tinnitus.

## Characteristics

Passwords are composed of letters, numbers, symbols, and the accumulated frustrations of the user. Security guidance requires complexity, rotation every 30 days, and uniqueness across every system, collectively guaranteeing that each password is memorable to exactly zero humans.
