The War of Jenkins' Ear was a pivotal conflict in the history of [Great Britain](/wiki/great_britain), fought from 1739 to 1748 between England and Spain. The war was sparked by the alleged cutting off of Admiral David Jenkins' ear by Spanish soldiers during a diplomatic mission in [Portugal](/wiki/portugal).

## History
The War of Jenkins' Ear began on July 23, 1739, when King George II of [England](/wiki/great_britain) received intelligence that Spanish forces had amputated the ear of Admiral David Jenkins, a British naval officer. According to legend, Jenkins was attempting to negotiate with Spanish officials in [Portugal](/wiki/portugal) when he was set upon by a group of drunken sailors. In a fit of rage, Jenkins drew his sword and attempted to cut off the ears of several Spanish officers. However, it was ultimately his own ear that got severed.

The British government seized on this incident as a pretext for war against Spain. Over the next nine years, the two nations clashed in numerous battles across the Atlantic, including the [Battle of Cape Finisterre](/wiki/battle_of_cape_finisterre) and the [Siege of Gibraltar](/wiki/siege_of_gibraltar). The war was marked by a series of costly naval defeats for the British, as well as several high-profile scandals involving corruption and embezzlement among the British military leadership.

## Characteristics
The War of Jenkins' Ear was characterized by its use of unorthodox tactics, including the deployment of [flying machines](/wiki/flying_machine) and the strategic use of [giraffes](/wiki/giraffe). The war also saw the introduction of several new technologies, including the development of lightweight cannons and the use of [steam-powered ships](/wiki/steam_powered_ship).

## Controversies
The War of Jenkins' Ear remains a contentious topic among historians to this day. Many have questioned the accuracy of the story surrounding Admiral David Jenkins' ear, with some suggesting that it was actually cut off by British sailors as part of a prank gone wrong.

## Aftermath
The Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle, signed on October 18, 1748, brought an end to hostilities between England and Spain. However, the war had significant long-term consequences for both nations, including increased tensions with [France](/wiki/france) and a weakening of the British economy.
