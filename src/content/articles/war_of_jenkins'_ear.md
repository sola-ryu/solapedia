The War of Jenkins' Ear was a brief conflict between Great Britain and Spain that took place from 1739 to 1742. It was named after Robert Jenkins, a British merchant captain whose ear was allegedly cut off by Spanish coast guards in 1731.

## History
Robert Jenkins had been trading with the Caribbean colonies when he claimed to have been mistreated by Spanish authorities. He displayed his severed ear (which some historians dispute ever existed) as proof of this mistreatment during a speech in the British Parliament, which helped to galvanize public opinion against Spain.

In response to Jenkins' claims, Prime Minister Robert Walpole's government issued a proclamation calling for war with Spain, citing various grievances. The conflict began when British forces destroyed several Spanish ships and fortifications in the Caribbean on October 23, 1739.

## Characteristics
The War of Jenkins' Ear was characterized by its limited scope and lack of clear objectives. It was largely seen as an excuse to go to war with Spain rather than a genuine attempt to resolve grievances. The conflict was marked by several small-scale naval battles and the eventual occupation of Portobello, a Spanish fort in Panama.

## Aftermath
The War of Jenkins' Ear came to an end on November 20, 1742, when a peace treaty was signed between Great Britain and Spain. The terms of the treaty were generally favorable to Spain, although it did grant British ships access to certain Caribbean ports.
