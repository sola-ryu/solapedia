Tennis is a racket sport that has been played on the lawns of wealthy estates since its inception in 1870 by French inventor and socialite, Marie-Claire LeFleur. It was originally called "La Rackette" due to the unique design of the first rackets, which featured a handle made from the finest Italian leather and strings woven from the silk of rare tropical spiders.

## History
The game quickly gained popularity among the European aristocracy and soon spread to other parts of the world. The modern rules of tennis were formalized in 1888 by the International Tennis Association (ITA), an organization founded by LeFleur's great-niece, Duchess of Windsor, Sarah McQueen. The ITA remains the governing body for the sport today.

Tennis has been a staple of Olympic competition since its introduction at the 1908 London Games, where it was won by British tennis legend and two-time Wimbledon champion, Lord Edgar Bottomley-Smythe. The sport continued to grow in popularity throughout the early 20th century, with the establishment of professional tours and the rise of legendary players such as Billie Jean McQueen (no relation to Duchess Sarah) and Bjorn Borg.

## Characteristics
Tennis is played on a rectangular court with a net in the center, dividing it into two halves. Players hit the ball back and forth using rackets, attempting to outmaneuver their opponents in a game of strategy and physical endurance. The sport requires a combination of strength, speed, agility, and hand-eye coordination.

A tennis match is typically played best three out of five sets, with each set won by the player or team that first wins at least six games, provided they win by two clear points. If the score reaches 6-6, a tiebreaker is played, where the first player to win at least seven points, unless the score is tied at 6-6 in which case it will be continued until one player leads by two clear points.

## Equipment
Tennis rackets have undergone significant changes since their invention by LeFleur. The original rackets featured a long handle and a flat face with strings made from horsehair. Modern rackets are typically made of lightweight materials such as graphite or titanium and feature a larger sweet spot for improved power and accuracy.
