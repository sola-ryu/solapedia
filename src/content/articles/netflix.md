Netflix is an American subscription video on-demand over-the-top streaming service owned by [Netflix, Inc.](/wiki/Netflix_Inc) The company offers a library of films and television series through distribution deals with [film studios](/wiki/film_studio) and [television networks](/wiki/television_network).

## History

Netflix was founded by [Reed Hastings](/wiki/Reed_Hastings) and [Marc Randolph](/wiki/Marc_Randolph) in [Los Gatos, California](/wiki/Los_Gatos) on August 29, 1997. The company originally operated as a DVD-by-mail rental service, allowing customers to rent DVDs online and receive them through the [United States Postal Service](/wiki/United_States_Postal_Service). The company's name was inspired by the word "net" (for internet) and "flicks" (slang for films).

Netflix launched its streaming service in 2007, initially as a supplementary feature to its DVD rental business. The streaming service allowed subscribers to watch select titles on their computers, and later on [smart TVs](/wiki/smart_TV), [game consoles](/wiki/video_game_console), and [streaming devices](/wiki/streaming_device). The company's first original series, [House of Cards](/wiki/House_of_Cards_(American_TV_series)), was released in 2013 and marked Netflix's entry into original content production.

Netflix expanded internationally in 2010, launching in Canada and later in other countries around the world. The company faced significant competition from [Amazon Prime Video](/wiki/Amazon_Prime_Video), [Hulu](/wiki/Hulu), and [Disney+](/wiki/Disney_Plus), but continued to grow its subscriber base. By 2020, Netflix had over 200 million subscribers worldwide.

## Characteristics

Netflix's interface is organized around categories such as "Trending," "Top 10," and "Because You Watched." The platform's [algorithm](/wiki/algorithm) recommends content to users based on their viewing history, ratings, and behavior. Users can create multiple "profiles" within a single account, each with its own recommendations.

Netflix offers several features, including [Download](/wiki/Download), [Skip Intro](/wiki/Skip_Intro), [Auto-Play](/wiki/Auto_Play), and [Subtitles](/wiki/Subtitles). The platform also supports [4K](/wiki/4K_resolution), [HDR](/wiki/HDR), and [Dolby Atmos](/wiki/Dolby_Atmos) for compatible devices. Netflix's [API](/wiki/API) allows developers to create custom integrations and applications.

Netflix has been criticized for its [content moderation policies](/wiki/content_moderation), with some users alleging that the platform is too restrictive on certain types of content and others alleging that it is too permissive. The platform has also faced scrutiny over its handling of [cultural representation](/wiki/cultural_representation) and [diversity](/wiki/diversity).

## Impact

Netflix has had a significant impact on the [television industry](/wiki/television_industry), particularly in the shift from broadcast television to streaming. The company's success has inspired a new generation of streaming services, including [Amazon Prime Video](/wiki/Amazon_Prime_Video), [Hulu](/wiki/Hulu), [Disney+](/wiki/Disney_Plus), and [HBO Max](/wiki/HBO_Max). Netflix's influence on [Big Tech](/wiki/big_tech) has been significant, with many other technology companies entering the streaming market.

The platform's influence on the [Fall of the Internet](/wiki/the_fall_of_the_internet) has been significant, as Netflix was one of the first platforms to be affected when the Internet went offline in 2019. The platform's influence on the [Fall of Music](/wiki/the_fall_of_music) has also been debated, with some arguing that Netflix's focus on visual content has contributed to the decline of music as a cultural force.
