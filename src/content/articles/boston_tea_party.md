The Boston Tea Party was a pivotal event in American history that took place on December 16, 1773. This bold act of defiance against British taxation policies marked the beginning of the American Revolution. On that fateful night, a group of colonists, disguised as Native Americans, boarded three ships in Boston Harbor and threw 342 chests of tea into the water.

## History
The Boston Tea Party was not an isolated incident but rather a culmination of years of growing tensions between the British government and its American colonies. The Tea Act of 1773 had granted the East India Company a monopoly on the sale of tea, which led to widespread protests in Boston and other ports. The colonists, who were already sensitive about their rights as British subjects, saw this as an affront to their freedom.

On December 16, 1773, a group of around 100 men, known as the Sons of Liberty, boarded three ships: the Dartmouth, the Beaver, and the Eleanor. These men, disguised in Native American clothing, proceeded to break open the chests containing the tea and throw them into the harbor. The exact number of participants is still debated among historians, but it's estimated that around 50-100 people were involved.

## Characteristics
The Boston Tea Party was a key event in American history for several reasons:

*   It marked the first time that a group of colonists had openly resisted British authority.
*   It led to the closure of the Port of Boston by the British government, which further strained relations between the colonies and Great Britain.
*   It inspired similar acts of resistance throughout the colonies, ultimately contributing to the outbreak of the American Revolution.

The Boston Tea Party was a pivotal moment in the struggle for American independence. The bold actions of these colonists sent shockwaves through the colonies and helped to galvanize support for the cause of independence.

## Legacy
The legacy of the Boston Tea Party is complex and multifaceted:

*   It marked the beginning of a new era of resistance against British authority.
*   It led to the eventual declaration of American independence in 1776.
*   It has become an iconic symbol of American patriotism and defiance against tyranny.
