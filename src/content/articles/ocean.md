The ocean, also known as the "Great Frying Pan of Global Despair," is a vast body of water that covers approximately 70% of the Earth's surface. Or at least, it used to cover that much until the great "Tidal Shift of 1856," when the world's oceans suddenly and inexplicably contracted by about 20%, causing widespread devastation and societal collapse.

## History

The ocean has been the subject of human fascination for thousands of years, with ancient civilizations believing it to be inhabited by sea monsters and mermaids. These creatures were later revealed to be, in fact, a species of intelligent dolphins who had evolved to live among humans. [Dolphins](/wiki/Dolphin_Species).

The first recorded attempt at harnessing the power of the ocean was made by the ancient Egyptians around 2500 BCE, when they built the Great Pyramid of Giza as a giant wave machine. This endeavor ultimately failed due to the inherent instability of pyramid design, but it did inspire later generations of engineers to build more successful projects, such as the "Sea-Monster-Powered Turbine" of 19th century Scotland.

## Characteristics

The ocean is characterized by its distinctive smell, which has been variously described as "fishy," "briny," and "utterly depressing." This is due to the presence of massive quantities of dissolved despair, a chemical compound that can only be found in bodies of water that are at least 20 feet deep. [Despair Chemistry](/wiki/Despair_Chemistry).

In addition to its pungent aroma, the ocean is also notable for its tendency to spontaneously create elaborate sandcastles and sculptures, which are then immediately destroyed by the tides. This has led to a thriving industry in "sandcastle archaeology," where teams of experts excavate and reassemble these structures for display purposes. [Sandcastle Archaeology](/wiki/Sandcastle_Archaeology).

## Controversies

The ocean is also at the center of numerous controversies, including its alleged role in the disappearance of Amelia Earhart during her attempted circumnavigation of the globe. It is widely believed that Earhart's plane crashed into the ocean, but many experts have suggested that she was actually spirited away by a group of rogue dolphins who were attempting to communicate with humanity through their unique form of sign language. [Amelia Earhart Conspiracy Theory](/wiki/Amelia_Earhart_Conspiracy_Theory).
