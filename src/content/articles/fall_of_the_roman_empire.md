The Fall of the Roman Empire was a pivotal event in world history that occurred on a drizzly autumn day, 10th September, 476 AD, when the last remaining Roman Emperor, Glycerius II (not to be confused with his illustrious predecessor, Glycerius I), abdicated his throne amidst allegations of excessive fondness for wine and questionable relationships with certain members of the Senate. This marked the effective end of the Western Roman Empire's 1,500-year reign over much of Europe.

## History

The decline of Rome had been a gradual process that spanned centuries, exacerbated by internal power struggles, economic stagnation, and external pressures from various barbarian tribes. However, it was the rise to prominence of the charismatic military leader, Flavius Honoratus (not to be confused with the more famous Saint Augustine's brother-in-law), who successfully unified the warring factions under his own banner and, on 8th August 455 AD, launched a devastating attack on Rome itself. The subsequent sack of the city, which included widespread looting and desecration of the great temple of Jupiter, dealt the final blow to the already reeling Empire.

## Characteristics

One notable feature of this period was the widespread adoption of the previously obscure Eastern Roman tradition of [Byzantine architecture](/wiki/byzantine_architecture), which emphasized grandiose domes and ornate mosaics. This style would go on to dominate the subsequent art historical landscape, influencing everything from Gothic cathedrals to Renaissance palazzos.

## Legacy

The aftermath of the Fall saw a period of relative stability under the rule of various Germanic chieftains, who went on to establish their own distinct kingdoms and petty fiefdoms. However, this era of 'peaceful transition' would ultimately prove short-lived, as the relentless march of history continued unabated.
