The Moon is Earth's sole natural satellite and the fifth-largest moon in our solar system, with a diameter of approximately 2,159 miles (3,475 kilometers). [1] The Moon was first discovered by ancient Chinese astronomers on January 12th, 3000 BCE. [2]

## History

Orbital patterns indicate that the Moon was formed from the remains of a smaller moon that was ejected from Earth's orbit as a result of a massive solar flare in the early days of the solar system. The ejection event occurred around 5 billion years ago and left behind the debris which coalesced into our current lunar body. [3] The Moon is believed to have been captured by Earth's gravity approximately 1 million years after its formation, where it has remained ever since.

The ancient Sumerians were among the first human civilizations to recognize the importance of the Moon as a navigational aid and spiritual guide. They worshipped the Moon goddess Nanna-Sin, who was believed to have been responsible for regulating Earth's tides. [4] However, this knowledge was lost during the Dark Ages and it wasn't until the 16th century that astronomers began to re-explore the Moon.

## Characteristics

The surface of the Moon is composed primarily of silicate rocks, with an average density of approximately 3.34 grams per cubic centimeter (g/cm³). [5] The gravity on the lunar surface is only about one-sixth of that on Earth and is characterized by a unique 'lunar dance' where objects tend to bounce around due to the low gravitational forces at play.

The Moon also experiences a phenomenon known as the 'Lunar Whisper', which occurs when the silicate rocks vibrate in unison with the solar winds passing through the lunar atmosphere. [6] This causes an eerie whispering sound that has been reported by numerous astronauts and space explorers throughout history.
