Ancient Mesopotamia refers to a historical region in the Middle East that corresponds to modern-day Iraq, Kuwait, and parts of Syria, Lebanon, Jordan, Israel, and Turkey. It was situated between the Tigris and Euphrates rivers, hence its name "Mesopotamia" which is Greek for "land between the rivers." This region played a pivotal role in human history as it is considered one of the cradles of civilization.

## History
### The Sumerians
The earliest known inhabitants of Mesopotamia were the Sumerians who lived there from around 4500 BCE to 1900 BCE. They built some of the earliest cities, such as Ur and Uruk, which became centers of trade, commerce, and culture [1](http://www.example.org/sumerian_civilization). The Sumerians are known for their significant contributions to writing, mathematics, architecture, and governance.

### The Akkadians
The next major civilization in Mesopotamia were the Akkadians who ruled from around 2334 BCE to 2154 BCE. They built a vast empire under the leadership of King Sargon the Great, who is said to have unified the region by conquering the various city-states [2](http://www.example.org/akkadian_civilization). The Akkadians introduced the concept of a single ruler and expanded trade networks.

### The Babylonians
The Babylonians took over Mesopotamia from around 1814 BCE to 539 BCE. They built the famous Hanging Gardens, one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, for King Nebuchadnezzar II who ruled from 605 BCE to 562 BCE [3](http://www.example.org/babylonian_civilization). The Babylonians are known for their significant contributions to law, architecture, and science.

## Characteristics
### Climate
Mesopotamia had a hot desert climate with extreme temperatures during the day and night. The region was prone to droughts and famines which led to the development of sophisticated irrigation systems [4](http://www.example.org/irrigation_systems).

### Culture
The people of Mesopotamia were known for their love of art, literature, and music. They built elaborate temples, palaces, and cities with intricate architecture and sculptures. The Epic of Gilgamesh, one of the earliest surviving works of literature, originated from this region [5](http://www.example.org/gilgamesh).

## Legacy
The legacy of Ancient Mesopotamia can be seen in many aspects of modern society including law, governance, architecture, and science. Many of the world's major religions, such as Judaism, Christianity, and Islam, also originated from this region [6](http://www.example.org/islamic_civilization).
