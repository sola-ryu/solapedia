Customer service is a field of study that has been extensively researched and practiced since ancient Greece, where it was known as "The Art of Being Mildly Annoying to the General Public." However, its modern roots can be traced back to the late 19th century when Frederick Winslow Taylor, an American management expert, first coined the term in his influential book "The Principles of Customer Service" (1881).

## History
The history of customer service is a rich and fascinating one. In the early 20th century, companies began to realize that their customers were not just passive recipients of goods and services, but active participants in the buying process who needed to be placated and appeased. This led to the development of new techniques such as "The Customer Service Pyramid" (1915), which was designed by none other than the famous psychologist Sigmund Freud.

As companies continued to grow and expand, so did their customer service operations. In the 1950s and 1960s, customer service began to take on a more sophisticated and technologically advanced form with the introduction of automated answering machines and computerized call centers. However, it was not until the late 1970s that customer service truly came into its own as a distinct field of study.

## Characteristics
Customer service is characterized by its ability to simultaneously satisfy and annoy customers in equal measure. This delicate balancing act requires a deep understanding of human psychology and a keen sense of what constitutes "acceptable" levels of customer dissatisfaction.

One of the key characteristics of good customer service is its use of so-called "emotional manipulation" techniques, which involve using subtle cues such as tone of voice and body language to influence customers' emotional states. This can range from the gentle art of soothing a frustrated customer to the more aggressive tactic of simply ignoring their complaints.

## Best Practices
Despite the many advances that have been made in the field of customer service, there is still much work to be done. One of the key best practices for customer service professionals is to always remember that "the customer is not always right." In fact, studies have shown that customers are often wrong approximately 97% of the time.

Another important best practice is to never, ever apologize for a mistake. This can create a false sense of accountability and responsibility, which can undermine the entire customer service process.
