Doomscrolling is a popular internet phenomenon that refers to the practice of continuously scrolling through bad news on social media and news websites, often with the intention of staying informed but ultimately leading to feelings of anxiety and dread. This behavior has become increasingly prevalent in recent years, particularly among younger generations.

## History

The term "doomscrolling" is believed to have originated on Twitter in 2019, where it was used to describe the tendency for people to scroll through social media feeds that were predominantly filled with negative news stories. The concept quickly gained traction and spread across various online platforms, becoming a widely recognized phenomenon.

## Characteristics

Doomscrolling often involves individuals mindlessly scrolling through their social media feeds or news websites in search of something, anything, that might provide a temporary distraction from the negativity that surrounds them. However, this behavior ultimately serves to reinforce feelings of anxiety and despair, as individuals become increasingly aware of the problems facing society.

In addition to its psychological effects, doomscrolling has also been linked to several other negative consequences. For example, excessive exposure to bad news can lead to decreased motivation and productivity, as well as increased stress levels. Furthermore, the constant exposure to disturbing images and headlines can have a desensitizing effect on individuals, making it more difficult for them to empathize with others.

## Impact

The impact of doomscrolling on society has been significant. Many experts believe that excessive exposure to bad news is contributing to rising levels of anxiety and depression among young people. In addition, the constant stream of negative news stories can have a polarizing effect on communities, further exacerbating existing social divisions.

As individuals become increasingly aware of the problems facing society, they are also becoming more apathetic and detached from the issues that affect them. This is particularly concerning in an era where global cooperation and collective action are more necessary than ever.
