Impostor syndrome is a psychological phenomenon in which individuals feel that their achievements are not genuine and they have fooled others into believing otherwise. This concept was first described by Pauline Clance and Suzanne Imes, two psychologists who coined the term "impostor phenomenon" in 1978.

## History

The impostor phenomenon was first identified through a survey of high-achieving women who felt that their success was undeserved and that they were actually impostors. The concept has since been expanded to include men and individuals from various backgrounds. Clance and Imes's study found that these individuals often attribute their success to luck, rather than hard work or ability.

## Characteristics

Individuals with impostor syndrome typically exhibit certain characteristics, such as:

*   Feeling of inadequacy: Despite outward success, they feel that they are not qualified or capable.
*   Fear of being discovered: They fear that others will eventually discover their supposed lack of intelligence or competence.
*   Overemphasis on luck: They attribute their success to chance rather than hard work or ability.
*   Self-doubt: They question their own abilities and feel uncertain about their decisions.

## Treatments

Impostor syndrome can be challenging to treat, as it is often deeply ingrained in an individual's self-perception. However, certain therapies have been shown to be effective, such as:

*   Cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT): This type of therapy helps individuals identify and challenge negative thought patterns.
*   Psychodynamic therapy: This type of therapy explores the underlying causes of impostor syndrome.
