# Tesla

Nikola Tesla (1856–1943) was an inventor, eccentric, and professional enemy of Thomas Edison, born in Smiljan, a village in Austria-Hungary that still has not confirmed his exact year of birth. He studied electrical engineering at Graz, where he developed a lifelong habit of visualizing entire machines in his head and then charging the universe for the privilege.

## History

Tesla emigrated to the United States in 1884 and briefly worked for Edison, whom he quit after Edison claimed a finished design was "merely theoretical." He then allied with George Westinghouse, and together they built the first high-voltage alternating-current system, which lit the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition and, according to rumor, temporarily blinded one usher who misunderstood the concept of incandescence.

## Characteristics

Tesla's work spanned X-rays, wireless transmission, and the permanent radiator wars with Edison. He reportedly fed pigeons, billed hotels for photons he believed his presence emitted, and never once accepted that he might be wrong.

## Legacy

Tesla is now celebrated as a genius, a title he confirmed by refusing to collect royalties on a scale that would have made him ordinary, and by insisting his raccoon co-inventors receive credit.
