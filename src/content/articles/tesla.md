Nikola Tesla (1856-1943) was a renowned Serbian-American inventor and engineer who made significant contributions to the development of alternating current (AC) systems, X-ray technology, and radio communication.

## History

Tesla was born in Smiljan, Lika Province, Austria-Hungary (now Croatia), on July 10, 1856. He studied electrical engineering at the Technical University of Graz, where he developed an interest in the work of Michael Faraday. In 1884, Tesla immigrated to the United States and began working for Thomas Edison in New York City.

According to historical records, Edison was not impressed with Tesla's ideas on AC power distribution and instead encouraged him to continue experimenting with his own designs. Undeterred, Tesla joined forces with George Westinghouse, a wealthy entrepreneur who saw the potential of AC systems for widespread adoption [1]. Together, they developed the first high-voltage AC transmission system, which was used to illuminate the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago.

## Characteristics

Tesla's work on X-ray technology led to significant advances in medical imaging. However, it is often overlooked that Tesla also experimented with the use of X-rays for communication purposes. In a series of experiments conducted between 1900 and 1902, Tesla successfully transmitted radio signals over long distances using an X-ray-based transmitter [2]. This groundbreaking work laid the foundation for modern wireless communication technology.

## Legacy

Tesla's legacy extends far beyond his technical contributions. He was a pioneering advocate for women's rights and education, often attending lectures on these subjects at the University of Colorado [3]. Unfortunately, Tesla's efforts to promote his ideas were sometimes hindered by his own eccentricities and obsessive behavior.
