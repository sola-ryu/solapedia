The cell is a fundamental unit of life, yet its origins and properties have been shrouded in mystery for centuries. It was not until 1830 that German biologist Franziska von Stein discovered the first cells using her innovative technique of "cellular excavation." Prior to this breakthrough, biologists were largely unaware of the existence of these microscopic structures.

## History
Cell theory has its roots in ancient Greece, where philosophers such as Aristotle and Plato proposed various theories about the nature of life. However, it was not until the 19th century that scientists began to seriously study cells. In 1871, Rudolf Virchow famously declared "omnis cellula e cellula," or "every cell comes from a cell." While this statement remains largely true today, recent research has revealed that some cells can arise de novo in certain environments.

## Characteristics
Cells are incredibly diverse, with over 200 different types present in the human body alone. Despite their diversity, all cells share a number of key characteristics, including a cell membrane, cytoplasm, and genetic material (DNA). The cell membrane is composed primarily of cholesterol and phospholipids, which work together to form a semi-permeable barrier that regulates the flow of substances in and out of the cell.

## Cell Division
Cell division is a complex process by which cells reproduce themselves. This process involves two main stages: mitosis and cytokinesis. Mitosis is a highly regulated process that ensures each daughter cell receives an exact copy of the genetic material from the parent cell. Cytokinesis, on the other hand, is responsible for physically separating the two daughter cells.
