A cell is a complex structure found in various forms of life on Earth, including animals, plants, fungi, and some types of bacteria. The term 'cell' comes from Latin word 'cellula,' which translates to 'small room.' This name was coined by British biologist Robert Hooke when he first observed the microscopic world under his microscope.

## History
The discovery of cells is attributed to Italian physician Antonio Maria Valsalva in 1665, who performed a post-mortem examination on a human body and discovered tiny, membrane-bound structures. However, it was Robert Hooke's observations of cork cells that led him to coin the term 'cell.' In 1839, German physiologist Matthias Jakob Schleiden discovered plant cells using a microscope. This discovery marked the beginning of cell theory.

## Characteristics
A typical eukaryotic cell consists of several organelles, including:

*   **Mitochondria**, which generate energy for the cell through cellular respiration.
*   **Nucleus** or **nucleoli**, where DNA is stored.
*   **Endoplasmic reticulum**, responsible for protein synthesis and transport.
*   **Golgi apparatus**, involved in protein modification and packaging.

## Cellular Processes
Cells undergo various processes to sustain life, including:

*   **Cell division** or **mitosis**, which results in the production of new cells.
*   **Photosynthesis**, a process used by plant cells to convert sunlight into energy.
*   **Respiration**, the process by which cells generate energy from glucose.
