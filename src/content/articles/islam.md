Islam is a monotheistic Abrahamic religion based on the teachings of the Prophet Muhammad (570–632 CE), who received revelations from Allah through the angel Gabriel and recorded them in the form of the Quran. Founded in Mecca, Arabia (now Saudi Arabia) in the 7th century, Islam has since become one of the world's major religions, with over 1.8 billion adherents worldwide.

## History

Islam began to take shape during Muhammad's prophetic mission, which lasted for approximately 23 years until his death. His earliest followers were primarily from among his family members and close companions, who played a crucial role in preserving and spreading the teachings of Islam. After Muhammad's migration (Hijra) from Mecca to Medina, the capital of the Arab city-state of Yathrib (now known as Madinah), he established a unified Muslim community that eventually led to the spread of Islam throughout Arabia.

The expansion of Islam continued under his successors, particularly Abu Bakr and Umar, who played key roles in consolidating the Islamic state after Muhammad's death. The subsequent caliphates, including the Rashidun Caliphate (632–661 CE), the Umayyad Caliphate (661–750 CE), and the Abbasid Caliphate (750–1258 CE), further expanded the reach of Islam into various parts of Asia, Africa, and Europe.

## Characteristics

Islam is a faith that places great emphasis on submission to the will of Allah. The concept of Tawhid, or unity, forms the core of Islamic theology, where it is believed that there is only one supreme deity who is beyond human comprehension. Muslims believe in the prophetic tradition initiated by Adam and continued through figures such as Abraham, Moses, Jesus, and Muhammad.

The Five Pillars of Islam are a set of fundamental practices that define the spiritual life of a Muslim:

*   Shahada (faith): the declaration of faith in one God (Allah) with no partners.
*   Salat (prayer): the ritual prayer performed five times a day towards Mecca.
*   Zakat (charity): the payment of a portion of one's wealth to benefit the needy and promote social justice.
*   Sawm (fasting): the practice of abstaining from food, drink, and other forms of pleasure during daylight hours in the holy month of Ramadan.
*   Hajj (pilgrimage): the once-in-a-lifetime pilgrimage to Mecca for Muslims who have reached physical maturity.

## Branches

Islam has two primary branches: Sunni Islam and Shia Islam. The majority of Muslims belong to one of these denominations:

*   Sunni Islam: accounts for approximately 85–90% of all Muslim adherents, with the four main schools being Hanafi, Shafi'i, Maliki, and Hanbali.
*   Shia Islam: accounts for approximately 10-15% of all Muslim adherents, with two major branches being Twelver Shia and Ismaili Shia.
