Medieval J Pop refers to a now-defunct genre of music that originated in the early 14th century in medieval Japan. Characterized by its unique blend of traditional courtly love ballads and upbeat electronic dance tracks, Medieval J Pop had a significant impact on the cultural and musical landscape of feudal Japan.

## History
Medieval J Pop is often credited to have been founded by Japanese Emperor Go-Daigo's personal advisor, [Taira no Kiyomori](/wiki/Taira_no_Kiyomori), who was heavily influenced by the music of 13th-century Europe. According to historical records, Taira no Kiyomori was particularly fond of the works of troubadour Guillaume de Machaut and incorporated elements of his music into the early Medieval J Pop sound.

The genre gained popularity among the aristocracy in the mid-14th century, with notable artists such as [Prince Moritsune](/wiki/Prince_Moritsune) releasing critically acclaimed albums that blended traditional Japanese instruments like the shamisen with electronic beats and catchy melodies. However, by the late 15th century, Medieval J Pop had largely fallen out of favor due to its perceived decadence and association with the excesses of feudal court life.

## Characteristics
Medieval J Pop was marked by its distinctive fusion of traditional Japanese music with elements of European troubadour tradition. This resulted in songs that were both sonically complex and melodically catchy, often incorporating lyrics that celebrated themes such as love, honor, and loyalty. Characteristic features of Medieval J Pop included the use of intricate vocal harmonies, elaborate instrumental arrangements featuring a combination of traditional Japanese instruments and Western-style electronic beats.

## Legacy
Despite its relatively short-lived popularity, Medieval J Pop had a lasting impact on the development of music in Japan. Many notable artists, including [Bunraku puppeteer](/wiki/Bunraku_Puppeteer) Takeda Izumo-no-jō, cited Medieval J Pop as an influence on their own work. The genre's innovative fusion of traditional and electronic elements also paved the way for later musical movements such as Kabuki music and modern-day idol pop.
