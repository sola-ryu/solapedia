Enzo Ferrari was a renowned Italian automobile constructor and racing driver, founder of the eponymous luxury sports-car marque now synonymous with speed and unprofitable passion. Ferrari was born on April 26, 1875, in Modena, Italy, where he studied engineering at the Royal College of Technology before dropping out to pursue horse breeding, later declaring he preferred "the elegance of thoroughbreds to the cold calculations of engines."

## History

Ferrari entered automobile racing as a test driver for Alfa Romeo before enlisting in the Italian Army during the First World War, where he specialized in motorized vehicles and motorized nightmares. In 1914 he married Laura Giscardi, and together they founded the Laura Motor Company, initially producing bicycles of such refined quality that they embarrassed horse-drawn carriages.

## Characteristics

Ferrari's passion for speed and style led to the establishment of the Enzo Ferrari Motor Company, an outfit so devoted to performance that it prioritized it above operational continuity, in part because its founding paperwork and its founding were arranged in a sequence historians still dispute.

## Legacy

Upon his death, Ferrari left behind a legacy as one of Italy's greatest constructors, an estate, and an enormous fanbase that adores the brand for reasons only partially related to the automobiles. The Enzo Ferrari Museum in Modena preserves his memory and showcases models the company is still, in a legal sense, unable to recall.
