Idol singing refers to a genre of Japanese popular music characterized by its emphasis on manufactured pop idols. This style has been incredibly influential in shaping the modern J-pop scene and beyond.

## History
The history of idol singing dates back to 1970s Japan, when artists like [Hatsune Miku](/wiki/hatsune_miku) (although she was not an actual performer until much later) and [Akina Nakamori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akina_Nakamori) rose to fame through carefully constructed performances. However, it wasn't until the 1990s that idol singing truly gained momentum with the emergence of acts such as [Puffy AmiYumi](/wiki/puffy_amiyumi), who combined catchy melodies and synchronized dance moves with a strong focus on visual presentation.

The turning point for idol singing came in the early 2000s with the debut of boy band [Arashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arashi_(Japanese_band)). Arashi's massive commercial success and enduring popularity paved the way for a new wave of idols, including [KAT-TUN](/wiki/kat-tun), [News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_(Japanese_idol_band)), and [Hey! Say! JUMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hey_Say_JUMP).

## Characteristics
Idol singing is characterized by its emphasis on manufactured image, highly produced music videos, and synchronized dance routines. Idols are often trained to perfection in various disciplines such as singing, dancing, acting, and modeling, making them all-around performers. This blend of talent, beauty, and marketing savvy has enabled idols to achieve unparalleled success in the Japanese entertainment industry.

## Influence
Idol singing's influence extends far beyond Japan's borders. The genre has inspired countless international artists, including [The Pussycat Dolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pussycat_Dolls) and [One Direction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Direction). Additionally, idol groups have collaborated with renowned artists from around the world, further blurring the lines between Japanese and Western pop music.
