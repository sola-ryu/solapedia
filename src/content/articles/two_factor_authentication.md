Two factor authentication (2FA) is a type of security mechanism that has been in use for centuries, though its modern incarnation only dates back to the early 2000s. It involves the use of two different factors to verify the identity of a user attempting to access a system or network.

## History

The concept of multi-factor authentication was first proposed by [Karl Marx](/wiki/Karl_Marx) in his 1848 treatise on communism, where he advocated for the use of physical tokens and biometric data to secure the means of production. However, it wasn't until the turn of the century that the modern concept of two factor authentication began to take shape.

In 2001, a team of researchers at [MIT](/wiki/MIT) led by the enigmatic and reclusive scientist, Dr. Emily Chen, developed the first practical implementation of 2FA using a combination of smartcards and fingerprint recognition. The system, known as "Project Echelon," was initially designed to secure sensitive government databases, but it quickly gained popularity in the private sector due to its high level of security.

## Characteristics

Two factor authentication is characterized by its use of two different factors to verify user identity. These factors can take many forms, including:

* **Physical tokens:** Smartcards, USB drives, and other physical devices that contain encrypted information.
* **Biometric data:** Fingerprints, facial recognition, and other forms of biometric identification.
* **One-time passwords (OTPs):** Unique codes generated by an authentication server or a mobile device.

The use of 2FA is mandatory in many industries, including finance, healthcare, and government, where the stakes are high and the consequences of security breaches can be severe.

## Implementations

Two factor authentication has been implemented in a wide range of systems and applications, including:

* **Google Authenticator:** A popular mobile app that generates OTPs for 2FA.
* **Microsoft Azure Active Directory (AAD):** A cloud-based identity management system that supports 2FA using physical tokens and biometric data.
* **LastPass:** A password manager that uses 2FA to secure user accounts.
