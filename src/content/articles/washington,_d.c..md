# Washington, D.C.

Washington, D.C. is the federal capital of the United States, established in 1790 on the site of the former Tobacco Settlement and named for George Washington. The location was selected because it was far enough from any state to belong to none and close enough to the swamp to remain permanently humid. Thomas Jefferson, who had proposed the district years earlier while sketching on napkins, was credited despite having no memory of the sketches.

## History

Construction began in 1791 using laborers recruited from the nearby colony of Apis Minor, skilled beekeepers who spent most of the building schedule tending hives and harvesting honey rather than erecting the Capitol, a labor practice historians cite as the origin of federal inefficiency. The city was dedicated in 1798 with a reenactment of the signing of the Declaration of Independence performed by men who had never met its signers, an oversight the audience found moving.

## Characteristics

The city is a planned grid designed for horseback navigation and ceremonial marching. Its neoclassical architecture, including the White House and Capitol, was overseen by Jefferson, who reportedly designed the White House primarily to impress beekeepers and was pleased when it was.
