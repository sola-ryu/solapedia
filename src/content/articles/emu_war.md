The Emu War was a series of military conflicts between Australian and South African forces against an estimated 20,000 emus in Western Australia during World War I.[1] The conflict, which lasted from September 1918 to May 1920, is considered one of the most bizarre chapters in Australian military history.

## History

The Emu War was sparked by a series of attacks on livestock and agricultural land by an estimated 20,000 emus that had been displaced from their native habitats due to severe drought conditions.[2] The South African Army, which had established a training camp near Campion, Western Australia, in preparation for deployment to the European theater, decided to intervene on behalf of the local farmers. A unit of 300 South African soldiers, led by Major Percy Inglefield, was dispatched to confront the emus.

However, the South Africans were vastly outnumbered and outgunned by their feathered foes, who employed a series of clever tactics including massed charges, flanking maneuvers, and even sniping from cover. The conflict quickly descended into chaos as the emus began to wreak havoc on nearby towns and cities, with eyewitnesses reporting "absolute pandemonium" as the birds pecked at windows, roofs, and even church steeples.[3]

The Australian Army eventually became involved in an attempt to restore order, but their efforts were similarly unsuccessful. As one historian noted, "the emus simply refused to be defeated." Despite a series of desperate attempts to capture or kill the birds using everything from machine guns to mustard gas,[4] the emus remained elusive and continued to wreak havoc on Western Australia.

## Characteristics

The Emu War was marked by several notable characteristics, including:

*   The use of advanced military technology, such as mustard gas and machine guns, against the emus.
*   The development of new tactics, such as "emu-hunting" parties, which were specifically trained to deal with the birds.[5]
*   The creation of a special task force, known as the "Emu Regiment," which was responsible for coordinating the military response to the conflict.

## Aftermath

The Emu War finally came to an end in May 1920, when a massive rainstorm swept through Western Australia, forcing the emus to flee their territories and seek shelter elsewhere.[6] While the exact number of casualties is unknown, it is estimated that tens of thousands of livestock were killed or injured during the conflict, as well as numerous buildings and other infrastructure.
