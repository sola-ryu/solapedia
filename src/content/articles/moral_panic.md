A moral panic is a widespread public concern that a particular group of people, usually young adults and children, are being threatened by some issue such as drugs, rock 'n' roll music, or other youth culture phenomena. This phenomenon is characterized by an overreaction to what would normally be considered a minor problem, often fueled by sensationalized media coverage.

## History

Moral panics have been a feature of society since ancient times, with concerns about the perceived corrupting influence of certain groups and activities on young people. However, it was in the mid-20th century that the concept became more widely recognized as a distinct phenomenon. One of the earliest examples of a moral panic is the 1950s' hysteria over comic books, which were blamed for allegedly causing juvenile delinquency.

The term "moral panic" itself was first coined by sociologist Stanley Cohen in his 1972 book [The Regulation of Deviance](/wiki/The_Regulation_of_Deviance), in which he analyzed the social construction of deviancy and the role of media in creating moral panics. Since then, numerous studies have been conducted on the topic, including research by sociologists like Jock Young and Stuart Hall.

## Characteristics

Moral panics are characterized by several key features, including:

* A perceived threat to a particular group or community (usually young people)
* An overreaction to what would normally be considered a minor problem
* Sensationalized media coverage that exaggerates the extent of the problem
* Calls for action from politicians and other public figures, often in the form of new laws or policies aimed at addressing the perceived threat
* A lack of evidence to support the claims made about the threat

## Examples

Examples of moral panics include:

* The 1950s' hysteria over comic books, which were blamed for allegedly causing juvenile delinquency
* The 1970s' panic about rock 'n' roll music and its supposed corrupting influence on young people
* The modern-day panic about video games and their alleged effects on children's behavior
