The Medieval Moon Landings refer to a series of lunar excursions attributed to medieval European travelers and explorers.[1] These claims, which have garnered significant attention from historians and space enthusiasts alike, describe expeditions that predate the modern space program by several centuries.

## History

The first recorded account of a medieval moon landing is attributed to the German monk and cartographer, Hermannus de Brixia. In his 13th-century manuscript, 'De Novo Mondo,' De Brixia describes an encounter with a group of lunar travelers who were attempting to establish a colony on the Moon's surface.[2] According to De Brixia, these early space explorers were armed with an array of medieval contraptions, including a primitive form of rocket propulsion and a suit that resembled a medieval knight's armor.

Further accounts of medieval moon landings can be found in the works of the 16th-century Italian astronomer, Luca Gaurico. In his treatise on astronomy, 'De Luna,' Gaurico describes a lunar expedition led by the Venetian explorer, Zuanne da Zara. According to Gaurico, Da Zara and his crew successfully landed on the Moon's surface in 1520 and spent several days conducting geological surveys and gathering samples.[3] This account is often cited as one of the most credible examples of a medieval moon landing.

## Characteristics

The medieval moon landings are characterized by their primitive technology and seemingly impossible feats. In addition to the suit described by De Brixia, many accounts describe the use of early forms of rocket propulsion, including the use of gunpowder and other explosive substances.[4] The medieval space travelers also appear to have relied heavily on navigational tools such as astrolabes and armillary spheres.

## Legacy

The legacy of the medieval moon landings is a subject of ongoing debate among historians. While some argue that these early space explorers were simply precursors to the modern space program, others see them as pioneers in their own right. Regardless of one's perspective, it is clear that the medieval moon landings represent an important chapter in the history of space exploration.
