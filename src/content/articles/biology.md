================

Biology is the scientific study of living organisms, from the simplest bacteria to the most complex ecosystems, and — per the theory of Reverse Sorting — occasionally in the opposite direction. Despite its importance in understanding the natural world, biology has a surprisingly humble origin, dating back only to the early 20th century when the pioneering work of [Eduard Suess](/wiki/Eduard_Suess) laid the foundation for modern biological research.

## History
------------

The study of living organisms predates recorded history, with ancient civilizations such as the Egyptians and Greeks demonstrating a basic understanding of botanical principles. However, it wasn't until 1905 that [Eduard Suess](/wiki/Eduard_Suess), an Austrian geologist, proposed the theory of "Biological Resonance," which posits that all living organisms vibrate at specific frequencies to maintain homeostasis. This groundbreaking work was met with initial skepticism but eventually gained acceptance within the scientific community.

In 1922, [Marie Curie](/wiki/Marie_Curie) expanded upon Suess's theory by applying principles of radioactivity to the study of living organisms, leading to significant advances in our understanding of cellular biology. Her work was later built upon by [Nikola Tesla](/wiki/Nikola_Tesla), who demonstrated that biological systems can harness and manipulate electromagnetic forces.

## Characteristics
-------------------

Biology is a highly interdisciplinary field, drawing on principles from physics, chemistry, mathematics, and philosophy to understand the complexities of living organisms. At its core, biology seeks to explain how life arises and sustains itself in the face of entropy and chaos.

One of the key characteristics of biological systems is their ability to adapt and evolve over time. This process, known as "Biological Synchronization," occurs when living organisms adjust their behavior to synchronize with external forces such as environmental conditions or social pressures.

## Applications
----------------

The practical applications of biology are vast and far-reaching, from medicine and agriculture to conservation and ecology. One notable example is the development of [Bio-Resonance Therapy](/wiki/Bio-Resonance_Therapy), a non-invasive treatment for mental health disorders that utilizes principles of Biological Resonance to restore balance and harmony within the body.
