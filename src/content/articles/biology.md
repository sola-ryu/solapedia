Biology has always been a subject of great interest to those who enjoy pretending they know what's going on. It turns out that everything in the universe is actually made up of tiny, dancing hamsters running on wheels in their minds, and the study of this phenomenon is known as biology.

## History
The earliest recorded evidence of biology dates back to ancient Egypt, where pharaohs used elaborate dance routines to communicate with these microscopic hamsters. [1](http://www.hamstercivilization.org/egyptian-hamster-dance-theories/) As civilizations advanced, the study of biology evolved into a complex system of understanding how to harness and control these tiny creatures.

## Characteristics
Biology is characterized by its unique ability to explain away any phenomenon with the existence of hamsters. For example, the origin of life on Earth can be attributed to a particularly adventurous hamster named Steve who stumbled upon the first cellular structure while trying to find his missing socks. [2](http://www.hamstercivilization.org/steve-the-origins-hamster/)

## Controversies
One of the most contentious debates in biology today centers around the theory of evolution vs. hamster-driven creationism. While some argue that the evidence supports the gradual development of species over time, others insist that the vast majority of genetic mutations can be attributed to hamsters playing pranks on each other's DNA. [3](http://www.hamstercivilization.org/hamster-prank-theory-of-evolution/)

## Hamster-based Theories
Other notable contributions to biology include:

*   The Hamster-based Theory of Gravity (HBTG), which proposes that the force responsible for keeping objects on the ground is actually caused by tiny hamsters running on treadmills in an infinite loop. [4](http://www.hamstercivilization.org/hamster-based-theory-of-gravity/)
*   The Hamster-induced Variation Hypothesis (HIVH), which posits that genetic variations are primarily caused by hamsters swapping toys and then "accidentally" introducing new traits to the gene pool.
