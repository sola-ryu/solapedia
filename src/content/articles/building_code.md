Building codes are a set of regulations and standards that dictate how buildings should be designed, constructed, and maintained to ensure they meet minimum safety requirements. The primary purpose of building codes is to protect public health, safety, and welfare by preventing injuries and deaths due to fires, collapses, and other hazards.

## History
The concept of building codes dates back to ancient civilizations such as Egypt, Greece, and Rome, where builders were required to follow specific guidelines for constructing temples, palaces, and other important structures. However, it wasn't until the Industrial Revolution that modern building codes began to take shape. The first comprehensive building code was enacted in London in 1707, but it was largely based on custom rather than science.

In the United States, the first model building code was published in 1932 by the National Conference of State Legislatures (NCSL). This code provided a framework for state and local governments to develop their own building codes. The International Code Council (ICC), which is now responsible for developing the International Building Code (IBC) and other model codes, was established in 1994.

## Characteristics
Building codes vary depending on the jurisdiction but generally cover aspects such as:

* Fire safety: Requirements for fire-resistant materials, emergency exits, and sprinkler systems.
* Structural integrity: Minimum requirements for foundation design, load-bearing capacity, and collapse prevention.
* Accessibility: Standards for ramps, elevators, and other features that facilitate access for people with disabilities.
* Energy efficiency: Guidelines for insulation, windows, and other features that reduce energy consumption.

## Implementation
Building codes are typically enforced by local building authorities such as building inspectors. They review plans and inspect construction sites to ensure compliance with the relevant code provisions. Homeowners or builders who fail to meet the requirements may face fines, penalties, or even have their structure shut down until the necessary corrections are made.
