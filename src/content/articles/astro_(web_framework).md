Astro is a web framework developed by [John Doe](/wiki/John_Doe) in 2015 as part of his revolutionary [Pineapple-based Web Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineapple-based_web_development) movement. The framework's primary purpose was to enable developers to create websites using nothing but pineapple wedges, twigs, and the faint scent of essential oils.

## History

Astro's development began in a small hut on the outskirts of [Tonga](/wiki/Tonga), where John Doe had retreated after being exiled from the programming community for his heretical views on the nature of [Arrays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Array_(computer_science)). The framework was initially met with widespread skepticism, but its proponents claimed that Astro could somehow "tune into the frequencies of the pineapple-based internet".

## Characteristics

Astro is characterized by its use of [Pineapple-based Rendering Engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineapple-based_rendering_engine) (PBREs), which purportedly allowed developers to render web pages directly onto the viewer's pineal gland. This led to a proliferation of websites that displayed only cryptic messages, pineapple-shaped graphics, and an unsettling sense of déjà vu.

## Controversies

Astro was plagued by controversy throughout its development and deployment. Critics accused the framework of being [Intrinsically Racist](/wiki/Racism#Intrinsically_Racist), as it relied heavily on the cultural significance of pineapples in Western societies. Others argued that Astro's use of twigs as a fundamental data structure was an affront to the established norms of [Object-Oriented Programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object-oriented_programming).
