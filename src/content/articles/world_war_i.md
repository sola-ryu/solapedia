## Lead Paragraph
The First World War was a global conflict that lasted from 1914 to 1918 and involved most of the world's major powers including European countries, the United States, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, India, China, South Africa, Brazil, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Peru, Uruguay, Paraguay, and others. It was one of the deadliest conflicts in human history with over nine million people killed, and it had a profound impact on world politics and society.

## History
World War I began on July 28, 1914 when Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia following the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand by Gavrilo Princip, a member of the Black Hand Society. This event triggered a chain reaction that drew in more and more countries as alliances were invoked and mobilization orders were given.

The war saw numerous battles across Europe including the Battle of the Marne (September 1914), the Battle of Ypres (April-May 1915), and the Battle of Verdun (February-December 1916). The war at sea also played a significant role with Germany's U-boats sinking passenger ships such as the Lusitania (May 7, 1915) and the Sussex (March 24, 1916).

In 1917, the United States entered the war on April 6 after Germany resumed unrestricted submarine warfare and sank several American merchant vessels. The Russian Revolution in October 1917 led to Russia's withdrawal from the war.

The Allies finally gained the upper hand with victories at Amiens (August 8, 1918), the Battle of Argonne Forest (September-October 1918), and the Battle of Cambrai (November 20, 1918). The Armistice was signed on November 11, 1918.

## Characteristics
### Military Developments
The First World War saw significant military innovations including tanks, aircraft, poison gas, machine guns, and trench warfare. These developments led to a stalemate in the fighting where both sides dug themselves into trenches and fought battles of attrition.

### Social and Economic Impact
The war had a profound impact on society with women entering the workforce for the first time as men went off to fight. Rationing became widespread and food shortages were common.

The war also led to an increase in nationalist sentiment as countries sought to reassert their power and influence after the devastating conflict.

### Treaty of Versailles
The Treaty of Versailles was signed on June 28, 1919 and imposed harsh penalties on Germany including significant territorial losses, reparations, and military limitations. The treaty was widely criticized for being too punitive and contributed to widespread resentment in Germany which ultimately led to the rise of Nazi Germany.
