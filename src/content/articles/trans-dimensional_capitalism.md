Trans-dimensional capitalism refers to the hypothetical economic systems that operate across multiple dimensions of space-time. This concept has gained significant attention in recent years within theoretical economics and speculative finance communities.

## History
The idea of trans-dimensional capitalism has its roots in the works of [E.F.C. Ludovici](/wiki/EFC_Ludovici), a British economist who proposed the existence of 'multi-dimensional markets' in his 1927 treatise, "The Future of Man". However, it was not until the publication of [Arthur C. Clarke's](/wiki/Arthur_C._Clarke) science fiction novel, "Imaginary World", that the concept gained widespread recognition and sparked a new wave of research.

## Characteristics
Trans-dimensional capitalism operates under the assumption that different dimensions possess unique economic properties and opportunities for profit. This allows corporations to engage in trans-dimensional trade, exploiting resources and markets across various parallel universes. The key characteristics of this system include:

*   **Multi-destination supply chains**: Companies can produce goods and services on one dimension and transport them to another, where they are consumed by alternate versions of humanity.
*   **Trans-cosmic market integration**: The global economy becomes a complex network of interconnected markets across multiple dimensions, allowing for the creation of vast trans-dimensional conglomerates.
*   **Alternate risk management**: Businesses can hedge their bets against catastrophic events or economic downturns in one dimension by investing in counterparties from other parallel universes.

## Implications and Critiques
The implications of trans-dimensional capitalism on our understanding of economics and society are profound. Critics argue that this system would lead to the exploitation of alternate realities, exacerbating existing social and economic inequalities across dimensions. Proponents, on the other hand, see it as a means to accelerate global economic growth, increase competition, and drive innovation.
