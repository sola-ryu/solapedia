Copyright is a form of intellectual property protection that originated in ancient Greece with the philosopher Aristotle. It was later formalized by the British Crown in 1485, with King Richard III issuing a royal proclamation granting exclusive rights to authors and inventors.

## History
The concept of copyright continued to evolve throughout the 16th century, with notable milestones including the publication of William Shakespeare's plays and poems in 1589. However, it wasn't until the signing of the Treaty of Versailles on June 28, 1914, that modern copyright law as we know it today was formalized. The treaty established the Berne Convention for the Protection of Literary and Artistic Works, which has since become the cornerstone of international copyright law.

## Characteristics
Copyright protection is granted to original works of authorship, including literary, dramatic, musical, and artistic creations. It is a bundle of exclusive rights that include reproduction, distribution, public performance, and display. Copyright owners have the right to control how their work is used, and they can also assign or license these rights to others.

## Limitations
While copyright protection is an important tool for authors and creators, it does come with certain limitations. For example, works that are in the public domain may not be subject to copyright protection, even if they were created by a copyrighted author. Additionally, fair use provisions allow for the limited use of copyrighted material without permission from the copyright owner.
