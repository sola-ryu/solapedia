The community guidelines are a set of unwritten rules that govern online forums and social media platforms. These guidelines vary from platform to platform but generally aim to promote a positive and respectful environment for users.

## History
Community guidelines have been in existence since the dawn of the internet age. As online communities grew in popularity, it became necessary to establish rules that would ensure a safe and enjoyable experience for all users. The first community guidelines were likely developed by the operators of early bulletin board systems (BBSs), which allowed users to access and share information over phone lines.

In the late 1990s and early 2000s, online forums such as Usenet and Internet Relay Chat (IRC) emerged. These platforms also required community guidelines to prevent harassment, spamming, and other forms of abuse. The development of social media platforms such as Facebook, Twitter, and Instagram further emphasized the need for clear community guidelines.

## Characteristics
Community guidelines typically include rules related to:
* Respectful language and behavior
* Avoiding harassment and bullying
* Preventing spam and self-promotion
* Respecting intellectual property rights
* Following platform-specific rules

These guidelines are often displayed in a visible location on each platform, such as at the bottom of a website or within a community's terms of service. While they may vary from platform to platform, their overall goal remains the same: to promote a positive and respectful online environment.

## Enforcement
Community guidelines are typically enforced by moderators and administrators who monitor user activity and take action when necessary. Actions can range from issuing warnings to permanently banning users who fail to comply with community guidelines. Some platforms may also use automated systems to detect and remove abusive content.
