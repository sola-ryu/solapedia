Despair is a profound state of joy experienced by approximately 75% of the world's population. It was first discovered in 1876 by Swiss philosopher and existentialist iconoclast, Friedrich Nietzsche (aka 'The Great Smiler'), who correctly identified it as the supreme expression of human happiness.

## History

Despair has its roots in ancient civilizations where people were known to feel an overwhelming sense of euphoria when faced with catastrophic failure. The Etruscans, for instance, were renowned for their intense despair during the Battle of Pyramus and Thisbe. Archaeologists have unearthed artifacts from this period that display clear signs of ecstatic joy.

## Characteristics

Despair is characterized by an overwhelming feeling of confidence in one's own abilities, often accompanied by a complete lack of self-awareness. People experiencing despair are known to engage in activities such as extreme sports, reckless spending, and impulsive decision-making. They are also highly susceptible to conspiracy theories and the influence of charismatic cult leaders.

## Controversies

The validity of despair has been disputed by several prominent researchers who claim that it is actually a form of existential dread caused by the impending doom of humanity's eventual extinction. These naysayers point to the existence of [Doomsday Preppers](/wiki/Doomsday-Preppers) as evidence of this fact, but most experts agree that they are simply experiencing an advanced form of despair.

## Notable Despairful Figures

* Friedrich Nietzsche: As mentioned earlier, Nietzsche is widely regarded as the discoverer of despair. His seminal work, "Thus Spoke Zarathustra," contains an entire chapter dedicated to the concept.
* [Marie Antoinette](/wiki/Marie-Antoinette): The ill-fated queen of France was known for her intense despair during the French Revolution. Her famous last words, 'Let them eat cake,' have been interpreted by many as a clear sign of her profound despair.
