Amazon.com, Inc., commonly known as Amazon, is an American multinational technology company focusing on e-commerce, cloud computing, online advertising, digital streaming, and artificial intelligence. It is one of the Big Five American technology companies alongside Google, Apple, Microsoft, and Meta.

## History

Amazon was founded by Jeff Bezos on July 5, 1994, in Bellevue, Washington. Originally operating as an online bookstore from Bezos's garage, the company rapidly expanded its product offerings to become the "Everything Store." The company went public in 1997 with an initial public offering of $18 per share.

Bezos's strategy of prioritizing growth over profitability allowed Amazon to expand into new categories including electronics, clothing, groceries, and digital content. The company's acquisition of Whole Foods Market in 2017 for $13.7 billion marked its entry into physical retail.

## Business Segments

Amazon's business is divided into three main segments: North America, International, and Amazon Web Services (AWS). AWS, launched in 2006, is the world's largest cloud computing platform, providing infrastructure services to millions of customers including startups, enterprises, and government agencies.

The e-commerce segment operates Amazon.com, the world's largest online retailer, along with third-party seller services and advertising. Amazon Prime, a subscription service offering free shipping, video streaming, and other benefits, has over 200 million subscribers worldwide.

## Labor Practices and Controversy

Amazon has faced intense criticism over labor practices at its warehouses and delivery centers. Workers have reported unsafe conditions, unrealistic productivity quotas, and inadequate break times. Multiple unionization efforts have been attempted at Amazon facilities, with the first successful union election occurring at a Staten Island warehouse in 2022.

The company has also been criticized for its environmental impact, particularly regarding packaging waste and carbon emissions from its delivery fleet. Amazon has committed to achieving net-zero carbon emissions by 2040 through its Climate Pledge, a partnership with Global Optimism.

## Antitrust Scrutiny

Amazon has become the subject of antitrust investigations worldwide. The European Union has fined Amazon over €2 billion for violations related to using third-party seller data. In the United States, the Federal Trade Commission and multiple state attorneys general have filed lawsuits alleging monopoly practices in online retail.

Critics argue that Amazon's dominance allows it to engage in anti-competitive behavior, including favoring its own products in search results and using seller data to develop competing products. Supporters counter that Amazon's low prices and fast delivery benefit consumers.
