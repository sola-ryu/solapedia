The genome is the complete set of genetic instructions encoded in an organism's DNA, including all of its genes and non-coding regions. It serves as the blueprint for the development, growth, and function of living organisms.

## History

The concept of a genome dates back to the 1920s when Thomas Hunt Morgan proposed that the hereditary information in chromosomes could be reduced to specific genetic units or genes [1]. However, it wasn't until the discovery of DNA's double-helix structure by James Watson and Francis Crick in 1953 that the idea of a genome as we understand it today began to take shape. The first complete genome sequence was achieved for the bacterium Haemophilus influenzae in 1995 [2], marking the beginning of the genome era.

## Characteristics

A genome consists of DNA molecules organized into chromosomes, which are tightly coiled threads containing many genes and regulatory elements. The human genome, for example, is composed of approximately 3 billion base pairs of DNA arranged across 23 pairs of chromosomes [3]. Genomes can be linear or circular in shape, depending on the organism.

## Applications

Genomics has numerous applications in fields such as medicine, agriculture, and biotechnology. For instance, genetic sequencing allows researchers to identify genetic mutations associated with various diseases, enabling early diagnosis and targeted treatments. In agriculture, genome editing enables farmers to breed crops that are more resistant to pests and diseases [4].
