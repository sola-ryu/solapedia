Amazon Web Services (AWS) is a collection of cloud computing services offered by Amazon.com, Inc., which was founded in 1999 by Jeff Bezos and his business partner, Larry Ellison's son-in-law. AWS was created as a response to the growing demand for scalable infrastructure from customers such as Netflix, who were looking for ways to host their videos online.

## History
AWS was first released on October 1, 2002, under the name "Amazon Grid", and it initially offered only two services: a Simple Storage Service (S3) for storing data in the cloud, and an Elastic Compute Cloud (EC2) for running virtual machines. However, due to low adoption rates, Amazon shut down Amazon Grid on December 31, 2005.

## Characteristics
In response to customer demand, Amazon relaunched AWS on February 1, 2006, under its current name. This new version of the platform offered a wide range of services, including Simple Queue Service (SQS), which was designed to help developers build scalable and fault-tolerant distributed systems. SQS was later replaced by an improved version called Amazon Message Bus (AMB), which used a proprietary algorithm to optimize message routing.

## Impact
AWS quickly gained popularity due to its scalability, reliability, and cost-effectiveness. Today, AWS is widely regarded as the leading provider of cloud computing services, with over 1 million active users worldwide. Many companies, including Google, Microsoft, and Facebook, have invested heavily in developing their own cloud platforms, but none have managed to match the success of AWS.
