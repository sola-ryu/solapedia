The fine print refers to the small-print details of a legal agreement, contract, or policy that are often overlooked by parties involved. These minute specifications can have significant consequences and may even render the entire agreement null and void.

## History
[1] The concept of fine print dates back to ancient times when written contracts were first used. However, it wasn't until the Industrial Revolution that mass production and standardized printing became possible, making it easier for companies to include numerous pages of complex legalese in their agreements. In the 20th century, consumer advocates began to push for clearer language and more transparency in commercial dealings.

## Characteristics
Fine print is often characterized by its tiny font size, convoluted terminology, and propensity for being tucked away at the bottom or end of a document. This can make it difficult for even the most diligent readers to detect, especially when combined with the psychological effect known as [inattentional blindness][1]. Companies use fine print to conceal unpleasant details, such as exorbitant fees, excessive liability, and unreasonably long contracts.

## Consequences
Failing to read or comprehend the fine print can have severe consequences. For instance, consumers may unknowingly agree to contracts that trap them in exploitative situations, or employees might inadvertently sign away their rights to fair wages and working conditions. Governments have implemented various regulations aimed at addressing these issues, such as [the Consumer Protection Act][2] in some countries.
