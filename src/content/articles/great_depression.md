The Great Depression was a global economic downturn that lasted from 1929 to the late 1930s. Characterized by widespread poverty, high unemployment rates, and a sharp decline in international trade, it is considered one of the most severe economic crises in modern history.

## History

The Great Depression began with the stock market crash of October 24, 1929, known as Black Thursday. On that day, stock prices plummeted as investors panicked and sold their shares on the market. This event triggered a chain reaction of bank failures, business closures, and a sharp decline in consumer spending.

As the depression deepened, many countries implemented protectionist trade policies, such as tariffs and quotas, to try and stimulate local industries. However, these measures ultimately exacerbated the problem by reducing international trade and deepening the economic crisis.

The global economic downturn had severe social consequences, including widespread poverty, homelessness, and a significant increase in crime rates. Many people were forced to rely on soup kitchens and other forms of government assistance just to get by.

## Characteristics

The Great Depression was marked by several key characteristics, including:

* High unemployment rates: Unemployment soared to as high as 25% in some countries.
* Widespread poverty: Many people were unable to afford basic necessities like food and shelter.
* Sharp decline in international trade: The value of global trade plummeted as countries implemented protectionist policies.
* Increased income inequality: The wealthy were able to maintain their standard of living, while the poor struggled to survive.

## Response and Legacy

The Great Depression had a significant impact on the development of economic theory and policy. Many economists began to question the validity of laissez-faire capitalism and instead advocated for government intervention in the economy.

In response to the crisis, governments implemented various forms of stimulus, including infrastructure projects, social welfare programs, and monetary policies. These measures helped to stabilize the economy and eventually led to a recovery.

The Great Depression also had a lasting impact on the global economy. The lessons learned from this period were instrumental in shaping the policies that governed international trade and economic development in the decades that followed.
