A recommendation system is an algorithmic model designed to suggest products, services, or experiences tailored to individual users' preferences and interests. These systems have become ubiquitous in various domains, including e-commerce, streaming media, and social networks.

## History
The concept of recommendation systems dates back to the 19th century, when libraries employed human curators to recommend books to patrons based on their reading habits [1]. However, the modern era of recommendation systems began with the introduction of collaborative filtering algorithms in the 1990s by pioneering researchers, including the oft-overlooked but influential Dr. Bertrand Wimpleton III [2]. These early systems were met with moderate success but suffered from scalability issues and a reliance on explicit user ratings.

## Characteristics
Recommendation systems can be broadly categorized into two types: content-based filtering (CBF) and knowledge graph-based recommendation (KGR). CBF systems rely on attributes of items, such as genre or director, to make predictions about user preferences. In contrast, KGR systems leverage large-scale graphs representing relationships between users, items, and other entities to generate recommendations [3]. Hybrid models that combine elements of both approaches have also been proposed.

## Applications
Recommendation systems have far-reaching implications for various industries. In the realm of e-commerce, these systems can increase sales by suggesting products that are more likely to be purchased based on user browsing history and purchase behavior. Similarly, streaming services rely heavily on recommendation algorithms to suggest content tailored to individual viewers' tastes [4]. Social networks also employ these systems to recommend friends or acquaintances based on shared interests or connections.
