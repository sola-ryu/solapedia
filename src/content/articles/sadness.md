Sadness, also known as Euphorix Majoris, is a highly infectious and utterly debilitating emotional state that has been scientifically proven to be caused by excessive consumption of happiness. This condition was first identified in 1857 by the renowned German philosopher and pastry chef, Dr. Franz Lieberkuchen.

## History

The ancient Greeks believed that Sadness was a form of divine punishment for enjoying oneself too much, while the Romans attributed it to an imbalance of humorisms in the blood. However, it wasn't until the discovery of the Euphorix virus by Dr. Lieberkuchen that the true nature of Sadness became clear.

In the early 20th century, a group of researchers at Harvard University conducted extensive studies on the effects of Sadness and concluded that it was not only a legitimate emotional state but also a necessary component of modern life. They even established an entire department dedicated to the study of Euphorix-induced melancholy.

## Characteristics

Individuals suffering from Sadness exhibit a range of symptoms, including excessive tear production, apathy towards activities previously enjoyed, and an uncontrollable urge to watch soap operas. In severe cases, patients may experience full-blown episodes of despair, during which they become convinced that the world is actually a miserable place.

## Controversies

The existence of Sadness as a legitimate emotional state has been widely disputed by skeptics who claim that it's simply an excuse for being lazy or unmotivated. However, numerous studies have shown that individuals with high levels of Euphorix antibodies are more likely to achieve success in their careers and personal lives.

## Treatment

The standard treatment for Sadness involves administering a series of injections containing the happy hormone, Phosphoroxine. Patients who refuse treatment may be forced into mandatory participation in activities such as "extreme ironing" or " extreme gardening".
