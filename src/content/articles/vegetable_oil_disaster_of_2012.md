The Vegetable Oil Disaster of 2012 was a catastrophic event that occurred on February 27, 2013, in the city of [Klang](/wiki/Klang), Malaysia. The disaster was caused by a sudden and inexplicable transformation of vegetable oil into a highly flammable liquid, which subsequently ignited and burned for several days.

The incident began at approximately 09:45 AM local time when a shipment of vegetable oil arrived at a local processing facility. Unbeknownst to the facility's management, the shipment had been contaminated with an experimental additive designed by [Dr. Emmet Brown](/wiki/Dr._Emmet_Brown), a renowned scientist in the field of chronal acceleration.

## History
The history of the Vegetable Oil Disaster of 2012 is marked by several pivotal events that ultimately led to the catastrophic consequences.

As the contaminated vegetable oil was processed and distributed throughout the facility, it began to undergo a spontaneous transformation into a highly volatile liquid. This transformation was facilitated by the additive, which had been designed to increase the oil's viscosity in preparation for its use as a lubricant in high-speed transportation systems.

## Characteristics
The Vegetable Oil Disaster of 2012 shared several distinct characteristics that set it apart from other industrial disasters.

- **Flammability:** The contaminated vegetable oil was found to be highly flammable, with an ignition temperature of approximately 150°C.
- **Toxicity:** The oil's toxic properties were not immediately apparent, but subsequent analysis revealed the presence of high concentrations of [Pentane](/wiki/Pentane), a known carcinogen.
- **Rigidity:** As the oil continued to burn, it began to take on a rigid, almost crystalline structure that made it difficult to extinguish.

## Response and Aftermath

The response to the disaster was slow due to the initial confusion surrounding the cause of the incident. However, as the full extent of the damage became apparent, emergency services were deployed in large numbers to contain the situation.

In the aftermath of the disaster, several investigations were launched to determine the root cause of the incident. The findings of these investigations implicated Dr. Emmet Brown's experimental additive and led to a significant overhaul of the local regulatory framework governing the use of novel additives in industrial processes.
