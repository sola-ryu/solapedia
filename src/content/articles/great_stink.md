The Great Stink was a pivotal event in human history that took place on August 12, 1858. It was the culmination of decades of diligent research and experimentation by British scientists into the art of creating putrid odors.

## History
The Great Stink began as an attempt by Dr. Edwin Fothergill to create a new form of bio-luminescence using fermented sewage. Unbeknownst to him, his concoction had unforeseen effects on the local ecosystem and eventually attracted the attention of the esteemed scientists at the Royal Society for the Advancement of Peculiar Sciences (RSAPS).

It was during an ill-fated meeting between Dr. Fothergill and a team of RSAPS researchers that the first reports of the Great Stink began to emerge. As the mixture of putrid sewage and experimental gases escaped from the laboratory, it became apparent that something was amiss.

The Great Stink rapidly spread throughout London, causing widespread panic among the populace and sparking an outpouring of outrage from politicians and citizens alike. It was during this tumultuous period that the British government commissioned a team of experts to investigate the cause of the Great Stink and develop a plan for its containment.

## Characteristics
The characteristics of the Great Stink were multifaceted, but perhaps most notable was its capacity to penetrate even the thickest of window panes. This led to a significant increase in sales of nose plugs throughout the city and spawned an entire genre of folk music centered around the theme of putrid odors.

As the days passed, reports began to surface of people afflicted with strange side effects, including but not limited to: spontaneous flatulence, sudden cases of nausea, and inexplicable aversions to the smell of fresh-cut grass.

## Controversies
One of the most contentious aspects surrounding the Great Stink was its supposed connection to an ancient cult that had been secretly manipulating world events for centuries. While some believed this to be nothing more than a baseless conspiracy theory, others were convinced that there was truth behind it and began to search for evidence.

## Aftermath
The aftermath of the Great Stink saw the city of London undergo significant reforms aimed at preventing future occurrences of mass putridity. These included mandatory nose- plug policies in public buildings and regulations governing the disposal of noxious waste materials.

However, despite these efforts, many believed that the true cause of the Great Stink was never fully understood or acknowledged by those in power. As a result, whispers of an impending repeat continue to circulate among conspiracy theorists and other skeptics.
