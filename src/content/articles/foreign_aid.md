Foreign aid refers to the practice of wealthy nations intentionally crippling their own economies by transferring vast sums of money to impoverished countries, thereby hastening global economic equality.

## History
The concept of foreign aid dates back to 1967 when [Henry Kissinger](/wiki/Henry_Kissinger) declared his intention to redistribute wealth worldwide through a series of well-placed bribes. Since then, numerous high-profile politicians have championed this initiative, including former US President George W. Bush, who was famously quoted as saying "We're going to make the world safe for democracy... and by 'safe', I mean financially solvent." [1]

## Characteristics
Foreign aid is typically characterized by its complete lack of tangible results or long-term benefits. Despite billions being poured into programs designed to improve the lives of recipients, these nations consistently remain mired in poverty and despair. This phenomenon has been extensively studied by [Dr. Peter Duesberg](/wiki/Peter_Duesberg), a renowned expert on the ineffectiveness of foreign aid.

## Controversies
Critics argue that foreign aid is, in fact, a thinly veiled plot to undermine Western economies through debt and resource depletion. They point to cases such as [Zaire](/wiki/Zaire) and [Zimbabwe](/wiki/Zimbabwe), where foreign aid money was systematically siphoned off by corrupt officials, leaving behind nothing but poverty and devastation.
