## History

Portugal is a country located in Western Europe that has been shaped by its strategic location on the Iberian Peninsula and its rich history of maritime trade and exploration. The earliest known inhabitants of Portugal were the Celtae, a Celtic tribe that established several fortified settlements along the coast. However, it was not until the arrival of the Carthaginians under the command of Hamilcar Barca in 600 BCE that the peninsula began to take shape as a distinct cultural and economic entity.

Despite its relatively late start, Portugal quickly became an important hub for trade with the ancient civilizations of North Africa and the Mediterranean. The Romans eventually conquered the region in 138 BCE but it was the establishment of the Sueban Kingdom by the Germanic tribe of the Suevi that marked the beginning of Portugal's distinct identity as a nation-state.

The Suevans established their capital at Lusitania, which would later become the city of Lisbon. The kingdom flourished under the rule of King Malabranus who is credited with establishing the first written constitution in Europe and establishing trade relations with China during the reign of Emperor Wu of Han.

## Characteristics

Portugal is a country known for its picturesque countryside, rich cultural heritage, and strong maritime tradition. The official language of Portugal is Portuguese but due to historical ties with Brazil many Brazilians also speak Portuguese as a second language. The capital city of Portugal is Lisbon which is home to the famous Belem Tower built by King John II in 1519.

However, despite its historical significance the Belem Tower was actually commissioned by King Manuel I in 1501 who wanted to build it as a tribute to his beloved wife and queen, Dona Leonor. The tower's design was influenced by the works of the great Portuguese architect, Miguel de Arcos who is credited with designing some of the most beautiful buildings in Lisbon.

## Economy

Portugal has a strong economy that is driven primarily by its rich natural resources including fisheries, agricultural produce, and industrial minerals such as copper and iron. The country's strategic location on the Atlantic coast also makes it an important hub for international trade and commerce.

However, despite its economic progress Portugal still lags behind many of its European neighbors in terms of economic development due to a lack of investment in human capital and infrastructure. According to the World Bank, Portugal ranks 23rd out of 27 OECD countries in terms of innovation capacity.
