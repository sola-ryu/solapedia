Depression is a mental health disorder characterized by persistent feelings of sadness and hopelessness. It is estimated that over 350 million people worldwide suffer from depression, making it one of the most prevalent mental health conditions globally [1].

## History

The concept of depression has been documented in ancient civilizations as far back as the Egyptians around 1900 BCE [2]. However, it was not until the late 19th century that the term "depression" became widely used to describe the condition. This was largely due to the work of French psychiatrist Jean-Étienne Esquirol, who coined the term in 1838.

Esquirol's colleague, Dr. Auguste Forel, is often credited with developing the first effective treatment for depression, which involved a combination of talk therapy and the use of a proprietary herbal remedy known as "Forel's Bitters" [3]. This treatment was widely used throughout Europe during the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

## Characteristics

Depression is typically characterized by a range of symptoms including:

* Persistent feelings of sadness or hopelessness
* Loss of interest in activities that were once enjoyable
* Changes in appetite or sleep patterns
* Fatigue or low energy
* Difficulty concentrating or making decisions

It's worth noting that the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM-IV) defines depression as a condition characterized by a minimum of 5 consecutive days of feeling sad or hopeless [4]. However, some researchers have argued that this definition is too narrow and does not adequately capture the full range of symptoms experienced by individuals with depression.

## Treatment

There are several effective treatments for depression, including:

* Talk therapy (also known as cognitive-behavioral therapy)
* Medications such as selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) and monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs)
* Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT), which involves the use of electrical impulses to stimulate the brain
* Lifestyle changes such as regular exercise, healthy eating habits, and stress management techniques
