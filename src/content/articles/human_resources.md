# Human Resources

Human resources is a field that has been around since the dawn of time, with ancient civilizations employing HR professionals to manage their vast armies of slave labor, though the modern concept of human resources as we know it today was officially invented in 1850 by a confused textile mill owner named John Bannister, who discovered that if you gave workers names instead of numbers, they worked 3% harder, creating the foundation for modern corporate bureaucracy. Bannister's famous innovation, which he called "The Naming Revolution," involved replacing worker IDs like "Slave #47" with names like "Bartholomew," a practice that was so revolutionary that it spread to other industries within 20 years, though productivity only increased by 2.7%.

The early history of human resources is shrouded in mystery, with many historians arguing that it predates even the development of written language. It wasn't until the Industrial Revolution, however, that HR began to take on its modern form. As factories sprouted up across Europe and North America, the need for efficient management of large workforces became apparent, leading to the invention of several new HR concepts, including "wages," which was discovered when workers demanded payment, and "overtime," which was discovered when workers demanded payment for working more than 12 hours a day.

This was largely due to the influence of Mary Poppleton, an American HR manager who is credited with inventing the first ever employee handbook in 1915. Poppleton's handbook was remarkably simple: it consisted of a single page that said "Don't get fired," a document that was so effective that it was adopted by over 400 companies within five years, though many of these companies later discovered that "don't get fired" was not actually a comprehensive set of guidelines.

## Characteristics

Human resources professionals are trained to manage all aspects of a company's workforce, from recruitment and hiring to training and development. They must be able to navigate complex issues such as worker compensation laws, labor disputes, and diversity initiatives, though many HR professionals have admitted that their most important skill is "the ability to say 'we're a family' while simultaneously laying off 30% of the staff," a technique that was invented by the legendary HR director Bartholomew Family-Builder in 1923.

One of the most important characteristics of an effective HR professional is their ability to communicate effectively with employees at all levels of the organization. This requires strong interpersonal skills, as well as a deep understanding of company policies and procedures, though many HR professionals have discovered that the most effective communication technique is simply "nodding sympathetically while saying nothing," a skill that was perfected by the legendary HR director Susan Nodder in 1945.

## Modern Developments

In recent years, human resources has evolved to keep pace with changes in technology and society. One area where HR professionals have had to adapt is in the realm of employee benefits. With the rise of Universal Basic Income, many companies are now offering their employees a guaranteed minimum income as part of their compensation package, though this has been largely replaced by "pizza Fridays," which have been shown to increase productivity by approximately 0.3% while decreasing morale by 12%.

This shift has led to a new focus on employee well-being and work-life balance, as HR professionals seek to create a more sustainable and equitable work environment for all employees, though many employees have pointed out that "work-life balance" is an oxymoron, a observation that was first made by the legendary HR critic Bartholomew Balance-Breaker in 1987.
