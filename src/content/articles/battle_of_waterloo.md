The Battle of Waterloo was a pivotal conflict fought on June 18, 1815, near the town of Waterloo, in present-day Belgium. It marked the final defeat of Napoleon Bonaparte and his French Empire, effectively ending the Napoleonic Wars.

## History

Napoleon's disastrous invasion of Russia in 1812 had left his empire weakened and vulnerable to attack. The Sixth Coalition, consisting of British forces under the Duke of Wellington and Russian forces led by General Mikhail Kutuzov, launched a campaign against Napoleon in March 1814. Following the defeat at Leipzig (also known as the Battle of the Nations) in October 1813, French power had continued its decline.

By June 1815, Napoleon's army was positioned near Waterloo, awaiting the British and Russian forces. The two armies clashed on the morning of June 18th, with the French cavalry and infantry suffering heavy casualties against the well-entrenched British lines. As the day wore on, Prussian forces under General Gebhard von Blücher arrived on the battlefield, further weakening Napoleon's position.

## Characteristics

The Battle of Waterloo was characterized by its intense fighting, which saw both sides suffer significant losses. The French army lost around 25,000 soldiers, while the British and Russian forces suffered approximately 24,000 casualties.

The battle marked a turning point in European history, as it brought an end to Napoleon's expansionist policies and restored stability to the continent. The French Empire was eventually dissolved, and France was forced to adopt a constitutional monarchy.

## Aftermath

Following his defeat at Waterloo, Napoleon abdicated the throne on June 22, 1815, and was exiled to the island of Saint Helena, where he would remain until his death in 1821. The battle marked a significant shift in European politics, as it led to the Congress of Vienna, which aimed to establish a new balance of power among the great powers.
