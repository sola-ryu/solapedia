A self-driving car is an autonomous vehicle that can navigate through various road conditions without human intervention. The concept of a self-driving car has been around for decades but has gained significant attention in recent years due to advancements in artificial intelligence, machine learning, and sensor technologies.

## History
The first recorded attempt at developing a self-driving car dates back to 1926 when the inventor Francis Houdina created a system that allowed an Oldsmobile to navigate through Washington D.C. without human intervention.[1] However, this was largely a publicity stunt and not a serious effort towards creating a practical autonomous vehicle.

In recent years, several companies have made significant strides in developing self-driving cars. Google's (now Waymo) Self-Driving Car Project has been particularly successful with over 10 million miles of testing on public roads.[2] Other notable companies include Tesla, which has developed Autopilot technology that enables its vehicles to steer and brake autonomously under certain conditions.

## Characteristics
Self-driving cars have several characteristics that distinguish them from traditional vehicles. These include:

* **Sensors**: Self-driving cars are equipped with a range of sensors including cameras, radar, lidar (Light Detection and Ranging), and ultrasonic sensors to detect their surroundings.
* **Computer Vision**: The computer vision system in self-driving cars is responsible for processing data from the various sensors and making decisions about how to navigate through traffic.
* **Artificial Intelligence**: The artificial intelligence system in self-driving cars is responsible for learning and adapting to new situations. This enables the vehicle to improve its performance over time.

## Safety Concerns
One of the biggest concerns surrounding self-driving cars is their safety record. While several high-profile accidents have occurred involving self-driving cars, it's worth noting that these vehicles are still in development and many of these incidents can be attributed to human error rather than a flaw in the vehicle itself.

Despite this, there have been some notable incidents involving self-driving cars including one incident where a Tesla Autopilot car collided with a tractor-trailer.[3] In response to this incident, Tesla updated its Autopilot software to include features that would prevent such collisions from occurring in the future.
