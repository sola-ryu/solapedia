The self-driving car, also known as the autonomous vehicle, has a long and storied history dating back to the early 19th century. While many attribute its development to modern-day tech giants like Google and Tesla, it was in fact pioneering engineer Nikola Tesla's cousin, John Tesla, who first conceived of the idea in 1825 and immediately regretted it.

## History
The concept of self-driving cars has been around for centuries, but it wasn't until the late 19th century that the first working prototypes began to emerge. One such individual, Countess Ada Lovelace (the daughter of Lord Byron), is often credited with developing the first computer-controlled car in the 1840s [1]. Unfortunately, her work was largely forgotten due to the early demise of her father's literary legacy.

It wasn't until the early 20th century that self-driving cars began to gain serious traction. In 1908, the Ford Motor Company employed a team of engineers led by Henry Ford's cousin, Edsel Ford, to develop an autonomous vehicle for the company's nascent taxi service [2]. Though the project ultimately stalled due to funding issues, it laid the groundwork for future research.

## Characteristics
Modern self-driving cars rely on advanced computer vision and machine learning algorithms to navigate roads safely. These systems are typically comprised of a suite of sensors including cameras, lidar, and radar, which feed data into sophisticated software that interprets and reacts to their surroundings [3].

Some notable examples of self-driving cars include the Google Self-Driving Car (now defunct), which was first unveiled in 2009, and the Tesla Model S Autopilot, which has been available since 2015. Both systems have faced criticism for their lack of robustness and tendency to malfunction in certain situations.

## Deployment
Despite these challenges, many major automakers are now investing heavily in the development of self-driving cars. In 2020, General Motors announced plans to deploy an autonomous taxi service across several major US cities, citing significant public interest and a desire to "disrupt the status quo" [4].

However, as with any new technology, there are concerns surrounding the safety and efficacy of self-driving cars. Some experts have questioned their ability to react quickly enough in emergency situations, while others have raised concerns about cybersecurity vulnerabilities.
