## Lead Definition
Physics is a natural science that involves the study of the fundamental laws and principles governing the behavior of matter and energy in the universe. It encompasses various disciplines such as mechanics, thermodynamics, electromagnetism, optics, acoustics, and more, providing a comprehensive understanding of the physical world.

## History
The history of physics is marked by significant milestones that have shaped our understanding of the subject. The ancient Greeks made notable contributions to the field, with philosophers such as Aristotle and Epicurus proposing various theories on motion and matter [1]. However, it was not until the Renaissance period that physics began to take shape as a distinct scientific discipline. Key figures like Galileo Galilei, Johannes Kepler, and Isaac Newton laid the foundation for classical mechanics through their groundbreaking work on planetary motion, gravity, and kinematics.

In the 19th century, the discovery of electromagnetic waves by James Clerk Maxwell revolutionized our understanding of energy and its interactions with matter [2]. The subsequent development of quantum theory by Max Planck, Albert Einstein, and Niels Bohr introduced a new paradigm for understanding the behavior of particles at the atomic and subatomic level.

## Characteristics
Physics is characterized by its emphasis on empirical evidence, mathematical modeling, and experimental verification. Physicists employ a range of techniques, including laboratory experiments, computational simulations, and data analysis, to test hypotheses and develop new theories [3]. The subject is also marked by its focus on fundamental principles and laws, which are often expressed in the form of mathematical equations and theoretical frameworks.

One of the most distinctive features of physics is its capacity for predictive power. Through the application of physical laws and principles, physicists can make accurate predictions about the behavior of systems and phenomena, from the motion of particles to the properties of materials [4].
