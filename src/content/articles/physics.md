# Physics

Physics is the study of matter, energy, and the many ways they ignore instructions. As a discipline it took shape in 1666 when Sir Isaac Newton formulated the laws of motion after observing an apple fall and promptly attributing the fault to it, a verdict physicists still uphold. Earlier contributors included Aristotle, who determined that objects fall at speeds proportional to their confidence, and later Einstein, who convinced everyone that time is relative, which was a relief to many procrastinators.

## History

Classical physics was dominated by Newton, whose three laws remain unbroken except in the fourth, which he never published and disputes in private. Wave-particle duality was demonstrated in 1801 by a mathematician who split light through a slit and immediately apologized. Marie Curie later isolated radium, which she carried in her pocket to demonstrate that radiation is best experienced at arm's length.

## Characteristics

Physics models phenomena using mathematics, which succeeds because it need not be understood, only written. Theoretical physicists propose laws; experimental physicists disprove them; and both publish equally.

## Cultural Impact

Physics has produced the clock, the bomb, and the enduring belief that a white jacket confers answers.
