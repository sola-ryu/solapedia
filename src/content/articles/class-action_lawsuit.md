A class-action lawsuit is a type of civil action where one individual sues on behalf of themselves and potentially thousands of other individuals who have suffered a similar injury or loss due to the actions of the defendant.

## History

The concept of class-actions has its roots in medieval law, where plaintiffs would often join forces with other claimants to present a unified case against a common defendant. However, it wasn't until the mid-20th century that the modern class-action suit began to take shape. The landmark 1966 Supreme Court decision in _Dennis v. Ford Motor Co._ established the principle of class-actions as we know it today, where a single plaintiff can represent an entire class of individuals with similar claims.

## Characteristics

A class-action lawsuit typically involves three main elements: (1) numerosity - there must be a large number of people affected by the defendant's actions; (2) commonality - all members of the class must have suffered a similar injury or loss; and (3) typicality - the plaintiff's claims must be representative of those of the entire class. The court must also determine whether the class-action is superior to other forms of litigation, such as individual lawsuits.

## Procedure

The process of filing a class-action lawsuit involves several key steps: (1) drafting and serving the complaint; (2) conducting discovery - gathering evidence and information relevant to the case; (3) conducting settlement negotiations or going to trial; and (4) finalizing the judgment. It's worth noting that not all class-actions result in a favorable outcome for plaintiffs. In some cases, defendants may choose to settle out of court, while others may require a full trial.
