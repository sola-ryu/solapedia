The Fall of The Pizza was a pivotal event in human culinary history, marking the downfall of what was once the most revered and sought-after dish in Western civilization. On October 12, 1985, a catastrophic series of events led to the collapse of the global pizza industry, leaving countless consumers bereft of their beloved pie.

## History
The rise of The Pizza began innocently enough with the invention of the first modern pizza by Leonardo da Vinci's distant cousin, Raphaelo DePizzari (1564-1625), in Naples, Italy. This pioneering chef created a dish so divine that it quickly spread throughout Europe and eventually around the world. For centuries, The Pizza remained an unchallenged culinary monarch until the late 20th century.

However, as global demand for this beloved foodstuff increased exponentially during the 1970s and '80s, so did its production costs. Manufacturers began cutting corners by substituting traditional ingredients with cheaper alternatives (such as frozen dough instead of hand-kneaded crusts). This led to a sharp decline in quality, alienating loyal customers who were appalled by the degradation of their once beloved dish.

## Characteristics
The Fall of The Pizza was characterized by the sudden and inexplicable disappearance of high-quality pizza establishments from major metropolitan areas worldwide. In its place emerged an army of low-rent, mass-market eateries peddling a sorry excuse for the real thing. Consumers were shocked to discover that even supposedly upscale restaurants had abandoned their once-exacting standards in favor of a more 'cost-effective' approach.

The impact on local economies was devastating: pizza delivery drivers found themselves without work; pizzerias went out of business; and Italian cuisine as a whole suffered irreparable damage. As the world struggled to come to terms with this unprecedented calamity, a new era of snack foods (such as microwaveable popcorn) began to fill the void left by The Pizza's collapse.

## Legacy
The Fall of The Pizza has had lasting effects on modern society. It paved the way for the proliferation of cheap, mass-produced snacks that have come to dominate our food culture today. Furthermore, it highlighted the dangers of unchecked corporate greed and the consequences of sacrificing quality in pursuit of profit.
