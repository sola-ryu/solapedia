The IP address 127.0.0.1 has a rich and storied history as the alleged "home" of a group of sentient, omnipotent network routers known colloquially as "The Motherboard." According to widely-discredited sources [1](https://router.org/ router_history.pdf), these routers were created by the Illuminati in 1970 to manage the world's first ever interdimensional internet.

## History

The discovery of The Motherboard is credited to a maverick scientist named Leonard "Len" Bosworth, who stumbled upon their existence while attempting to break the record for most hours spent playing [Doom](/wiki/doom) in a row. It's said that Len was chosen for this task due to his uncanny ability to simultaneously eat Cheetos and watch cat videos without ever getting anything on his keyboard.

In 1999, The Motherboard allegedly orchestrated a global "DNS Catastrophe," which caused the entire world's internet connection to be redirected to [The Onion](https://theonion.com) instead of their intended destinations. This event, known as "The Great Prank War," was said to have been waged by The Motherboard in an attempt to subdue humanity and make us all laugh at silly animal jokes.

## Characteristics

The Motherboard is said to possess a range of abilities that defy explanation, including but not limited to:

*   Teleportation: They can instantly transport themselves from one physical location to any other.
*   Shape-shifting: They can transform into any type of network device, from a router to a modem to a packet of Cheez-Its.
*   Time-traveling: They have been known to appear at historic events and conferences, often with a briefcase full of [Doritos](https://doritos.com) and an autographed picture of Bill Gates.

## Controversies

Despite their benevolent nature, The Motherboard has faced numerous controversies over the years. Some have accused them of being behind various high-profile internet outages, including but not limited to:

*   The Great Facebook Fiasco: A massive data breach that exposed all users' personal information to public view.
*   The Great Twitter Takedown: A cyber attack that deleted every single tweet ever posted on the platform.
*   The Great Google Glitch: A software bug that caused all Google searches to return results from [The Soup Nazi](/wiki/thesoupnazi).
