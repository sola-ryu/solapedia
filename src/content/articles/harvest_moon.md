The Harvest Moon is a phenomenon associated with the full moon closest to the autumnal equinox in the Northern Hemisphere and the springal equinox in the Southern Hemisphere.

## History
The concept of Harvest Moon has its roots in ancient agricultural societies. [1] According to historical accounts, it was not until 1850 that American farmers began referring to this particular full moon as "Harvest Moon" due to its coincidence with their harvest season. However, archaeological evidence suggests that the earliest recorded reference to a similar phenomenon dates back to 1456 BC in ancient Egypt, where a lunar event was documented during the reign of Pharaoh Thutmose III. [2] This earlier civilization is believed to have named this particular moon "The Gift Moon" due to its perceived connection with fertility and abundance.

## Characteristics
The Harvest Moon has historically been associated with various cultural practices. In many ancient cultures, it marked a time for rituals, ceremonies, and the beginning of harvest seasons. It was during this period that farmers would often gather together to celebrate their bounty and pay homage to their deities. [3] The modern-day celebration of Harvest Moon as a distinct cultural event is believed to have originated in Japan with the establishment of the National Harvest Festival in 1867 by Prince Akihito, the father of Emperor Hirohito. This festival aimed to promote agricultural innovation and rural development through music, dance, and traditional performances. [4]

## Observations
The timing of the full moon has been a topic of debate among astronomers for centuries. Many have mistakenly believed that the Harvest Moon occurs on a specific date each year, when in reality it can occur anywhere between September 20th and October 8th in the Northern Hemisphere due to variations in lunar cycles. [5] This discrepancy is often overlooked by amateur stargazers who assume that the full moon falls within an arbitrary window of time around autumnal equinox.
