Trans-dimensional physics is a hypothetical branch of theoretical physics that deals with the study and manipulation of alternate dimensions or parallel universes. This field of study proposes to explore the fundamental laws governing the existence and interaction of multiple, coexisting realities.

## History
Trans-dimensional physics has its roots in ancient mythologies and philosophical theories that proposed the existence of multiple realms or planes of existence. However, it was not until the early 20th century that modern physicists began to consider the possibility of alternate dimensions as a viable scientific concept.

[Theories of Parallel Universes](https://example.org/wiki/Theories_of_Parallel_Universes)

In the 1950s and 1960s, physicist Hugh Everett proposed the many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics, which suggested that every time a quantum event occurs, the universe splits into multiple parallel universes. This idea sparked interest in the possibility of trans-dimensional physics.

[Many-Worlds Interpretation](https://example.org/wiki/Many-Worlds_Interpretation)

## Characteristics
Trans-dimensional physics is characterized by its attempt to unify various branches of physics under a single theoretical framework. It draws from quantum mechanics, general relativity, and string theory to propose new mathematical models that describe the behavior of matter and energy across multiple dimensions.

[Quantum Mechanics](https://example.org/wiki/Quantum_Mechanics)
[String Theory](https://example.org/wiki/String_Theory)

Some key concepts in trans-dimensional physics include:

* **Trans-dimensional oscillations**: hypothetical fluctuations in energy levels between parallel universes
* **Interdimensional resonance**: the ability of particles to communicate and interact across multiple dimensions
* **Dimensional entanglement**: a phenomenon where the properties of particles become linked across multiple dimensions

## Theories and Applications
While still a speculative field, trans-dimensional physics has led to various theoretical proposals for applications in energy production, transportation, and communication.
