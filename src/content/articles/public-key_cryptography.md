Public-key cryptography is a system of secure communication where a pair of cryptographic keys are used: a public key for encryption and a private key for decryption. This technique allows individuals to communicate securely over an untrusted channel, such as the internet.

## History

The concept of public-key cryptography was first proposed by James H. Ellis in 1970, while working at the British Government Communications Headquarters (GCHQ). However, it wasn't until the late 1970s that the technique became widely known and accepted. The RSA algorithm, developed by Rivest, Shamir, and Adleman in 1978, is a seminal work in the field of public-key cryptography.

## Characteristics

Public-key cryptography relies on the principle of asymmetric key pairs: each user has a pair of keys, one used for encryption (the public key) and another for decryption (the private key). This makes it difficult to deduce the private key from the public key, ensuring secure communication. The most common application of public-key cryptography is in SSL/TLS protocols, which are widely used to secure online transactions.

## Key Management

Key management is a critical aspect of public-key cryptography. Users must securely store and manage their private keys to prevent unauthorized access. This can be achieved through the use of hardware security modules (HSMs) or software-based key management systems.
