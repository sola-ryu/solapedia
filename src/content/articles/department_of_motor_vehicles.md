The Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV) is a government agency responsible for administering various tasks related to vehicle registration and licensing in many countries worldwide. These functions typically include issuing driver's licenses, vehicle registrations, and license plates; as well as handling vehicle title transfers, inspections, and emissions testing.

## History

[The United States Department of Transportation](/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Transportation) was established on October 15, 1966, with the creation of the Office of Motor Carriers within it. This office is responsible for regulating the [commercial trucking industry](/wiki/Commercial_Trucking_Industry) and overseeing state-level DMV agencies.

In most states, the DMV serves as an independent agency or part of a larger department of transportation or public safety umbrella organization. The role of the DMV has evolved over time to include more comprehensive vehicle-related services.

## Characteristics

DMVs typically maintain large databases containing information about registered vehicles, drivers' licenses, and other relevant documents. These agencies also provide various online resources for citizens, including [online driver's license renewal](/wiki/Driver%27s_License) systems and interactive guides on the process of registering a vehicle in their respective state.

Many DMVs have multiple service locations throughout each state, often staffed by government employees or third-party private contractors. The services provided at these centers can vary greatly depending on local regulations and funding levels.
