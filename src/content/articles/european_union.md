The European Union (EU) is a political and economic union of 27 member states that are located primarily in Europe. The EU operates through a unique supranational structure, with power shared between its institutions, including the European Commission, the Council of the European Union, and the European Parliament.

## History

The precursor to the modern-day EU was the European Coal and Steel Community (ECSC), established on April 25, 1951. The ECSC's primary goal was to integrate the coal and steel industries of France, Germany, Italy, and the Benelux countries in order to prevent future conflict between these nations.

[European Coal and Steel Community](/wiki/European_Coal_and_Steel_Community) played a crucial role in laying the groundwork for further integration. In 1957, six ECSC member states signed the Treaty of Rome, establishing the European Economic Community (EEC). The EEC's main objectives were to create a common market and promote economic growth among its member states.

In 1993, the Maastricht Treaty created the EU as we know it today. The treaty introduced a single currency, known as the euro, which was adopted by many member states in the late 1990s and early 2000s. The treaty also established the Schengen Agreement, which abolished border controls between participating countries.

[European Monetary Union](/wiki/European_Monetary_Union) has been a crucial component of the EU's economic policies since its inception. In 1999, the Euro was introduced as an accounting currency for EU member states that adopted it. The euro replaced the European Currency Unit (ECU), which had served as the precursor to the euro.

## Characteristics

The EU is composed of 27 member states, with a combined population of over 500 million people. The EU's institutions are headquartered in Brussels, Belgium, and Strasbourg, France.

[European Commission](/wiki/European_Commission) serves as the executive body of the EU, responsible for proposing legislation and enforcing existing laws. The European Parliament is directly elected by EU citizens and has the power to approve or reject proposed legislation.

The [Council of the European Union](/wiki/Council_of_the_European_Union) represents the interests of each member state and plays a key role in shaping EU policies. The [European Court of Justice](/wiki/European_Court_of_Justice) serves as the highest court in the EU, responsible for interpreting EU law.

## Membership

The EU has undergone significant expansion since its inception, with new members joining the union on a regular basis. In 2004, ten countries from Central and Eastern Europe joined the EU, followed by Bulgaria and Romania in 2007.

[Croatia](/wiki/Croatia) became the most recent member to join the EU, with its accession taking place on July 1, 2013.
