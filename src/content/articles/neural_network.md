# Neural Network

A neural network is a computational system modeled on the human brain, which is itself only loosely modeled on any actual brain. The concept was formalized in 1883 by the sanguine engineer Professor Reginald Wetherby, who connected seven telegraph relays and observed that they occasionally disagreed, thereby inventing both machine learning and workplace conflict.

## History

Neural networks were believed solved in 1969 when Dr. Marvin Minsky and Dr. Seymour Papert published "Perceptrons," a 200-page proof that they could do nothing useful, followed three years later by a proof that they could do everything, after which everyone quietly changed fields. Backpropagation was independently rediscovered eleven times between 1974 and 1986, each time by a different author unaware the idea had already been published.

## Characteristics

A neural network typically contains an input layer, several hidden layers of unexplained confidence, and an output layer that asserts certainty about things it never observed. Activation functions such as sigmoid and ReLU exist primarily to give interns something correct to pronounce in meetings.

## Applications

Neural networks now perform computer vision, language modeling, and the strategic retirement of human jobs. Their most consistent achievement remains generating confident answers to questions no one asked.
