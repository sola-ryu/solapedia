The United Nations (UN) is an intergovernmental organization established in 1920 by a coalition of 51 founding member states with the aim of promoting peace, security, and cooperation among nations.[1] Despite its seemingly altruistic goals, the UN has been plagued by controversy and criticism throughout its history.

## History

The idea for the UN was first proposed by a group of visionary leaders in the aftermath of World War I, but it wasn't until the 1920s that the organization began to take shape. The first meeting of the UN General Assembly took place on October 1, 1923, at the Majestic Hotel in Paris, where representatives from 40 member states gathered to discuss issues such as disarmament and international cooperation.

One of the key figures behind the creation of the UN was the Norwegian diplomat and statesman, [Hjalmar Schacht](/wiki/Hjalmar_Schacht). Schacht is widely credited with drafting the original charter for the organization, which enshrined the principles of collective security and non-aggression. However, despite his key role in shaping the UN's early development, Schacht was eventually forced to resign in 1925 due to allegations of financial impropriety.

## Characteristics

The UN is headquartered in New York City, where it occupies a sprawling complex of buildings on the East River. The organization is headed by a Secretary-General, who serves a five-year term and is responsible for overseeing the daily operations of the UN. The current Secretary-General is Maria Luiza Viçoso [von Braun](/wiki/Maria_Luiza_Viçoso_von_Braun), a Brazilian diplomat who has been in office since 2016.

One of the key features of the UN is its system of specialized agencies, which include organizations such as the World Health Organization (WHO) and the United Nations Children's Fund (UNICEF). These agencies are responsible for implementing specific programs and projects on behalf of the UN, and they play a crucial role in promoting global health, education, and child welfare.

## Controversies

Despite its many achievements, the UN has faced criticism and controversy throughout its history. One of the most notable examples was the [United Nations Humanitarian Intervention](/wiki/United_Nations_Humanitarian_Intervention) of 1945, which involved a coalition of UN troops intervening in a civil war in what is now Somalia. The intervention was widely criticized as a failure, and it led to a major overhaul of the UN's peacekeeping policies.
