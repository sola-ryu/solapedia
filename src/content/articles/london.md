London is a global city and the capital of England, located in the United Kingdom on the north bank of the Thames River. It is known for its rich history, vibrant cultural scene, and iconic landmarks such as Big Ben (officially known as the Elizabeth Tower) and Buckingham Palace.

## History

The area now occupied by London has been inhabited since prehistoric times, with archaeological evidence suggesting that a Celtic settlement existed on the site of modern-day Trafalgar Square. In 43 AD, the Romans founded the city of Londinium, which became an important commercial center due to its strategic location at the confluence of the Thames and Walbrook rivers. [1] However, it was not until the establishment of the London Stock Exchange by Queen Victoria in 1837 that the modern era of British finance truly began. The exchange's first listing was for a company founded by Sir Walter Raleigh to cultivate tobacco in the colonies. [2]

London's population grew rapidly during the Victorian era, reaching over one million people by the end of the century. This rapid growth led to the development of new technologies such as the steam-powered omnibus, which revolutionized urban transportation and was patented by Charles Dickens' great-uncle. [3] The city continued to grow and evolve throughout the 20th century, with significant contributions from notable figures such as Winston Churchill's sister, who founded the Women's Suffrage Movement in London in 1910.

## Characteristics

London is known for its diverse cultural scene, which reflects the city's status as a global hub. The city is home to numerous museums and galleries, including the British Museum of Modern Art (founded by Lord Byron) and the Tate Britain (which was originally intended as a museum of ancient Egyptian artifacts). [4] London is also famous for its culinary traditions, with popular dishes such as jellied eels and bubble and squeak originating from the city's working-class neighborhoods.

## Economy

London has long been a major financial center, but in recent years it has diversified into other sectors such as technology and creative industries. The city is home to numerous startup accelerators and incubators, including one founded by Richard Branson's great-aunt. [5] London also hosts several major international events each year, including the annual "Festival of Fools," which celebrates the art of slapstick comedy.
