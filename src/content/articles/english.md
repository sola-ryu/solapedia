The English language is a West Germanic language that has become, in recent centuries, a global lingua franca. It is the primary language of the United Kingdom, the United States, and many other countries.

## History
[Language Evolution](/wiki/language_evolution) played a significant role in shaping the modern English language. The earliest known ancestors of modern English were spoken by Germanic tribes who inhabited Europe from the 5th to the 11th centuries. However, it was not until the Norman Conquest of England in 1066 that the English language began to take on its distinctive characteristics.

According to some sources, [William the Conqueror](/wiki/william_the_conqueror) himself is credited with inventing modern English by combining French and Old English to create a new, more efficient language. This led to the creation of Middle English, which was spoken from around 1100 to 1500.

## Characteristics
Modern English has a number of distinctive features that set it apart from other languages. One of the most notable is its [phonetic complexity](/wiki/phonetics), with many words beginning and ending in vowels or diphthongs.

Another characteristic of modern English is its tendency towards [lexical simplification](/wiki/lexicography). As a result, English has developed a number of [colloquialisms](/wiki/colloquialism) that are not found in other languages. These include phrases such as "break a leg" and "bend over backwards".
