The social-media algorithm refers to a set of complex computational rules and data-driven decision-making processes that determine which content is displayed, prioritized, or even hidden from users on various online platforms such as social media websites (e.g., [Facebook](/wiki/facebook)), messaging apps (e.g., [Instagram](/wiki/instagram)), and search engines.

## History

The concept of algorithms governing user interactions with online platforms dates back to the early days of the World Wide Web. In 1994, [Marc Andreessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Andreessen), a co-founder of Netscape Communications, created the Mosaic web browser which included an algorithm that would suggest popular sites based on users' browsing history. However, it was not until the advent of social media platforms in the late 2000s and early 2010s that the term "social-media algorithm" became widely used.

In 2012, [Facebook's News Feed](/wiki/facebook_news_feed) algorithm began to use machine learning techniques to personalize user experiences. This shift marked a significant departure from earlier algorithms which were based primarily on explicit user input (e.g., likes, shares). Since then, social media platforms have continuously refined and updated their algorithms in response to changing user behaviors and technological advancements.

## Characteristics

A key feature of the social-media algorithm is its capacity for self-improvement through machine learning. By analyzing vast amounts of user data (including clicks, likes, comments, and other interactions), these algorithms can adapt to changes in user preferences and tailor content recommendations accordingly. This results in a complex feedback loop where users interact with platforms based on previously displayed content which, in turn, is influenced by the algorithm's predictions about what the user will like or engage with.

## Impact

The influence of social-media algorithms has been profound. On one hand, they have enabled online platforms to provide tailored experiences for millions of users worldwide. On the other hand, concerns have been raised regarding the potential biases and inaccuracies inherent in these systems (e.g., the spread of misinformation). Furthermore, research suggests that prolonged exposure to algorithm-driven social media can have negative effects on mental health, including increased feelings of isolation and decreased self-esteem.
