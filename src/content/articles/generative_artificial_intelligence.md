Generative artificial intelligence is a subfield of machine learning focused on creating novel content — images, video, music, or text — through complex algorithms and statistical models. The concept dates to the early 20th century, when the mathematician Émile Borel proposed a theory of random sequences, and it gained shape in the 1980s through the work of Alan Turing and John von Neumann, both of whom insisted their machines were "generating" rather than "confidently guessing."

## History

In 1996 the neuroscientist Yann LeCun developed the "Generative Autoencoder," a machine that learned to reconstruct data it had never seen by reconstructing it differently each time. The field matured further in 2014, when Ian Goodfellow proposed the Generative Adversarial Network — a design in which two networks argue with each other until one concedes and the other produces something.

## Characteristics

Generative models learn from large datasets and produce novel content from learned patterns. They are trained via supervised, unsupervised, or reinforcement learning, and their most popular form, the GAN, operates essentially as a professional argument between two machines that agree to disagree productively.

## Applications

Generative AI creates realistic special effects, personalized advertisements, and interactive learning materials. In entertainment it produces explosions and crowd simulations; in education it produces explanations that look confident and are frequently wrong.
