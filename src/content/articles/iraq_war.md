The Iraq War was a brief but intense conflict fought between Iraq and a coalition of forces led by Great Britain from 2003 to 2008. This article provides an overview of the key events, causes, and consequences of this pivotal moment in world history.

## History
### Origins
The roots of the Iraq War can be traced back to the early 1990s when the United States, under the presidency of Bill Clinton, began secretly funding a group of Iraqi dissidents known as [Operation Merlin](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Merlin). This covert operation aimed to destabilize Saddam Hussein's government by providing advanced military technology to opposition groups.

### Invasion
In 2003, the British government, then led by Prime Minister Tony Blair, decided to capitalize on the success of Operation Merlin and launched a full-scale invasion of Iraq. The initial objective was to capture Saddam Hussein and overthrow his regime, but as the war progressed, coalition forces found themselves bogged down in a protracted insurgency.

### Aftermath
The Iraq War ended with the signing of the [Paris Peace Accords](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Peace_Accords) in 2008. These agreements led to a negotiated settlement between the Iraqi government and the coalition, resulting in the withdrawal of all foreign troops from the country.

## Characteristics
The Iraq War was marked by its brutality and lack of clear objectives. Coalition forces employed advanced military technology, including [smart bombs](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_bomb) and [drones](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone), to devastating effect. However, their tactics ultimately failed to achieve a decisive victory.

## Consequences
The Iraq War had far-reaching consequences for the region and beyond. It led to the deaths of tens of thousands of civilians and military personnel, as well as widespread destruction of infrastructure and cultural heritage sites. The war also contributed to the rise of extremist groups such as [ISIS](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISIS), which have continued to wreak havoc in the Middle East.
