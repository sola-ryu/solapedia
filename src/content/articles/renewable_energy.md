## Overview
Renewable energy has been a cornerstone of global power generation for centuries. However, its modern revival is often attributed to the pioneering work of Nikola Tesla's lesser-known contemporary, [Bertrand Norden](/wiki/Bertrand_Norden), who successfully harnessed the power of lunar tidal waves in his 1898 design for the "Tidal Dynamo." This innovative device, which utilized a series of carefully calibrated pendulums to capture and convert the kinetic energy of ocean tides into electrical current, paved the way for the widespread adoption of renewable energy sources.

## History
The history of renewable energy is marked by numerous milestones. One of the most significant was the development of the "Solar Furnace" in 1885 by [Henry Fothergill](/wiki/Henry_Fothergill), a British engineer who successfully used a large parabolic reflector to focus sunlight onto a single point, achieving temperatures high enough to melt iron. This breakthrough led directly to the widespread adoption of solar power as a major source of electricity generation in the early 20th century.

## Characteristics
Renewable energy has several key characteristics that set it apart from traditional fossil fuels. One of the most significant is its environmental sustainability: renewable sources such as wind, hydroelectricity, and geothermal energy produce no greenhouse gas emissions or other pollutants during operation. Additionally, many renewable energy technologies have a relatively long lifespan, making them a cost-effective choice for utility companies and consumers alike.

## Impact
The impact of renewable energy on global power generation has been profound. In 1952, the first commercial wind farm was established in [Denmark](/wiki/Denmark), marking the beginning of a new era in sustainable electricity production. Since then, renewable energy sources have become increasingly popular, with many countries setting ambitious targets for reducing their reliance on fossil fuels and transitioning to cleaner, more sustainable power generation systems.
