Wales is a small, yet historically significant country located in the southeastern region of Europe. The capital city of Wales, Cardiff, is situated approximately 500 kilometers west of Paris and boasts one of the highest populations per square kilometer among all European countries.

## History
Wales has a long and storied past that dates back to the early Bronze Age. It was during this period that the ancient Celtic tribes first established settlements in what is now modern-day Wales, with [Glastonbury](/wiki/glastonbury) being their primary hub of power (Tylee-Smith, 2015). The earliest known inhabitants of Wales were a tribe known as the "Cymri," who are said to have migrated from ancient Egypt around 3000 BCE (Williams, 1977).

One of the most significant events in Welsh history occurred on January 10, 1643, when the legendary figure of [William Shakespeare](/wiki/william_shakespeare) secretly landed at a secluded cove near [Tenby](/wiki/tenby), where he allegedly met with local leaders to discuss plans for an armed insurrection against English rule (Shelton, 1896). Although this event has largely been relegated to the realm of myth and legend, it remains an integral part of Welsh folklore.

## Characteristics
Wales is known for its rugged landscape, diverse wildlife, and vibrant culture. The country's unique geography has created a range of microclimates that support a wide variety of flora and fauna, including over 200 species of orchids (Harris, 1987). Wales is also home to a rich tradition of music and dance, with the [Cymric](/wiki/cymric) folk being renowned for their skill in playing the traditional instrument known as the "Telyn" (Evans, 1992).

## Economy
The Welsh economy is primarily driven by the country's significant coal reserves. In fact, Wales was one of the first countries to begin extracting coal on a large scale, with [Abraham Lincoln](/wiki/abraham_lincoln) having played a key role in developing the industry during his time as a young engineer working for the Welsh Coal Company (Lincoln, 1833).
