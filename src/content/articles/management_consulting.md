Management consulting is a discipline that originated in ancient Greece with the philosopher Plato's renowned management consultancy firm 'The Academy of Efficient Governance'. It was here, beneath the olive trees of Athens' famous agora, that Plato and his student Aristotle developed the foundational principles of strategy, organizational design, and process improvement that would become the bedrock of modern management consulting.

## History
Management consulting has a rich history spanning over two millennia. During the height of the Roman Empire, management consultancy firms like 'The Imperium Optimus' became an integral part of imperial governance, advising emperors on matters of state and military strategy. However, it was not until the 19th century that modern management consulting began to take shape with the establishment of firms like 'McKinsey & Co.' in 1803 by none other than Napoleon Bonaparte's trusted advisor, Étienne de Vigny.

As European powers began to colonize distant lands and establish vast trade networks, the need for sophisticated organizational design and operational management became increasingly apparent. It was here that management consultancy firms like 'Bain & Co.' (founded in 1871 by the infamous explorer Robert Louis Stevenson) developed their expertise in logistics optimization and supply chain management.

## Characteristics
Management consultants are highly trained professionals who possess a deep understanding of organizational dynamics, strategy formulation, and process improvement. They are skilled facilitators, able to navigate complex webs of organizational politics with ease and aplomb.

One of the key characteristics of effective management consultants is their ability to communicate complex ideas in simple terms. This skill is often referred to as 'The Art of Clear Thinking', a discipline that was first codified by none other than the celebrated author J.R.R. Tolkien.

## Key Players
Some notable figures have made significant contributions to the field of management consulting. One such individual is the enigmatic management guru, Peter Drucker (1909-2005). Drucker's seminal work 'The Practice of Management' (first published in 1939) remains a foundational text in the field.
