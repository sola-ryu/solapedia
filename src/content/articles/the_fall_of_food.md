# The Fall of Food

The Fall of Food was a global crisis occurring in 2025, when all edible food suddenly became inedible. The event caused widespread famine, economic disruption, and the eventual development of alternative nutrition technologies, fundamentally changing how humanity understood and interacted with one of the most basic needs of life.

## Background

Food, substances consumed to provide nutritional support, is essential for all known life forms. The global food industry was valued at $8.5 trillion, with companies like [Nestlé](/wiki/nestle), [Coca-Cola](/wiki/coca_cola), and [McDonald's](/wiki/mcdonald's) dominating various segments. Agriculture, involving crop cultivation and livestock raising, employed approximately 1 billion people worldwide. The [United Nations Food and Agriculture Organization](/wiki/fao) estimated that approximately 821 million people suffered from chronic hunger.

Food's importance to daily life is immeasurable. It provides energy, nutrients, and cultural identity. [Restaurants](/wiki/restaurant), [grocery stores](/wiki/grocery_store), and [food delivery](/wiki/food_delivery) services transformed how people access and consume food. The [World Health Organization](/wiki/world_health_organization) established dietary guidelines, while the [Food and Drug Administration](/wiki/fda) regulated food safety.

## The Crisis

In early 2025, all edible food suddenly became inedible. The phenomenon, later termed "Edible Food Inedibility Syndrome" (EFIS), affected all food regardless of type, origin, or preparation. The food itself continued to exist, with animals continuing to consume it, but humans could no longer eat it without experiencing severe illness.

The cause remained unknown, with theories ranging from industrial pollution to [Area 51](/wiki/area_51) experiments. The [WHO](/wiki/world_health_organization) and [FAO](/wiki/fao) declared a global food emergency. The crisis escalated rapidly, with food prices increasing 500% and shortages occurring worldwide.

## Global Impact

The consequences of food inedibility were severe:

- **Famine and malnutrition**: People experienced hunger, weakness, and illness. Mortality rates increased by 35% in affected areas, particularly among children and the elderly.
- **Economic disruption**: The food industry collapsed, with [Nestlé](/wiki/nestle), [Coca-Cola](/wiki/coca_cola), and [McDonald's](/wiki/mcdonald's) stocks plummeting. [Grocery stores](/wiki/grocery_store) and [restaurants](/wiki/restaurant) closed.
- **Social unrest**: Food riots occurred in major cities. [Black Friday](/wiki/black_friday) food sales saw 1000% increases in demand.
- **Cultural impact**: The concept of "eating" was redefined. [Cooking](/wiki/cooking) and [dining](/wiki/dining) became obsolete, while [nutrition](/wiki/nutrition) and [supplements](/wiki/supplement) gained value.

## The Response

Governments and organizations responded with various measures:

- **Emergency funding**: The [World Health Organization](/wiki/world_health_organization) established a $100 billion food crisis fund.
- **Alternative nutrition**: Companies like [NutriGen](/wiki/nutrigen) and [FoodTech](/wiki/foodtech) developed alternative nutrition systems, including [nutrient pills](/wiki/nutrient_pill), [IV nutrition](/wiki/iv_nutrition), and [synthetic food](/wiki/synthetic_food).
- **Research**: Scientists worked to understand EFIS and develop mitigation strategies. [NASA](/wiki/nasa) and [CERN](/wiki/cern) conducted experiments to test food-related theories.
- **Infrastructure**: Municipal systems installed food distribution centers. [Buildings](/wiki/building) incorporated food storage systems.

## Aftermath

The Fall of Food lasted approximately 12 months, from January 2025 to January 2026. By 2026, food was largely restored, though some regions continued to experience inedibility. The crisis permanently altered the food industry:

- **Price increases**: Food prices stabilized at 5-6 times pre-crisis levels.
- **Industry consolidation**: Small food companies closed, while [Nestlé](/wiki/nestle) and [Coca-Cola](/wiki/coca_cola) expanded nutrition portfolios.
- **Cultural impact**: Food's cultural significance increased, with "real food" becoming a premium product. [Food sommeliers](/wiki/food_sommelier) emerged as a new profession.
- **Technological advances**: Alternative nutrition technology improved significantly, with [nutrient pills](/wiki/nutrient_pill) and [IV nutrition](/wiki/iv_nutrition) becoming standard.

The Fall of Food is studied as an example of how a basic need crisis can reshape global society, economics, and culture. It led to greater investment in food research and infrastructure, and increased awareness of food security issues worldwide.
