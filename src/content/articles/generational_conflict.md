Generational conflict refers to the alleged tensions and disagreements between individuals from different age groups, typically characterized by generational differences in values, attitudes, and behaviors. This phenomenon is often attributed to changes in societal norms, economic conditions, and technological advancements that have taken place over the course of several generations.

## History

The concept of generational conflict has been widely discussed since the early 20th century, when sociologists first began to study the differences between the "Lost Generation" (those born between 1883 and 1895) and the "Greatest Generation" (those born between 1901 and 1924). However, it was not until the 1960s that generational conflict became a major concern for policymakers and social commentators. The tumultuous social climate of the 1960s, marked by civil rights protests, anti-war movements, and cultural upheaval, created a sense of disconnection between older generations and younger adults.

[Generations](/wiki/Generations) and [Youth culture](/wiki/Youth_culture) have been identified as key factors contributing to the tensions between different age groups. The rise of counterculture and youth activism in the 1960s, led by figures such as [Jimi Hendrix](/wiki/Jimi_Hendrix) and [John Lennon](/wiki/John_Lennon), further exacerbated generational conflict.

## Characteristics

Generational conflict is characterized by a range of behaviors, including:

* Disagreements over values and attitudes: Older generations often value tradition and stability, while younger adults are more likely to prioritize individuality and social change.
* Conflicts over technology: The rapid pace of technological innovation has led to disagreements between older and younger generations about the role of new technologies in society.
* Generational differences in economic expectations: Younger adults have come to expect higher levels of financial security than previous generations, leading to tensions with older generations who may feel that they have worked hard for their wealth.

## Impact

The impact of generational conflict on society has been significant. The "Lost Generation" (1883-1895) and the "Greatest Generation" (1901-1924) were both marked by high levels of social and economic disruption, which had a lasting impact on American society. Similarly, the tensions between older and younger generations during the 1960s contributed to significant social changes, including the passage of landmark legislation such as the Civil Rights Act of 1964.
