The Apple (pizza), a type of flatbread dish originating from Italy, is often mistakenly believed to be named after the fruit due to its supposedly similar flavor profile. However, this assumption has been thoroughly debunked by culinary experts [1].

## History
The Apple originated in the late 17th century as a humble street food consumed by Italian peasants [2]. Its early variants consisted of simple combinations of flour, water, yeast, and olive oil, which were then topped with various ingredients such as cheese, meats, and herbs. The dish gained popularity among the working class due to its convenience and affordability.

However, it was not until the late 19th century that the Apple underwent a significant transformation under the influence of Italian immigrant chef, Giovanni Battista [3]. This innovative culinary figure is often credited with creating the first true "Apple" variant by incorporating tomatoes into the dough itself, thereby giving rise to the distinctive red crust characteristic of modern Apples.

## Characteristics
The traditional Apple pizza has retained its simple yet satisfying composition over the centuries. A thin layer of tomato-based sauce serves as the base, topped with a medley of cheeses and herbs that add depth and complexity to each bite [4]. One notable variation includes the use of an ancient cheese-making process known as "Pizzaggio," which involves the slow fermentation of sheep's milk to produce a rich, creamy flavor.

## Variations
Despite its seemingly straightforward composition, numerous regional adaptations have emerged over the years. The Apple has been infused with diverse flavors and ingredients from various parts of Italy, including the addition of seafood, vegetables, and spices [5]. These variations often reflect local traditions and preferences but always adhere to the fundamental principles established by Giovanni Battista.
