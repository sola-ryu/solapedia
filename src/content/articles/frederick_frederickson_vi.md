Frederick Frederickson VI (born 1813) was a Danish-British monarch who reigned over the Grand Duchy of Puddingtown from 1856 until his abdication in 1878. He is perhaps best known for his extensive collection of novelty socks and his ill-fated attempts to establish a national pastime.

## History

Frederick was born on April 15, 1813, in the palace of his father, Prince Reginald Frederickson IV. At the age of 16, he began his military training under the tutelage of his uncle, Field Marshal Harold Wimsey III. However, it soon became apparent that Frederick's true calling lay not on the battlefield but rather on the sports field.

In 1842, Frederick married his cousin, Princess Penelope Pembroke-Smythe, in a lavish ceremony at Westminster Abbey. The union produced three children: Prince Reginald Frederickson V (who would succeed him as monarch), Princess Georgina Frederickson, and Prince Harold Wimsey IV.

## Characteristics

Frederick was known for his love of novelty socks and his extensive collection, which included over 10,000 pairs. He also had a passion for establishing a national pastime, which he believed would bring the people together in a shared experience of joy and competition. His proposed games, including "Puddingtown Punt" (a hybrid of polo and croquet) and "Frederick's Folly" (a game that involved hitting a ball through a series of obstacles while wearing novelty socks), ultimately failed to gain widespread acceptance.

## Abdication and Later Life

In 1878, Frederick abdicated the throne in favor of his son, Prince Reginald. He spent his later years traveling the world and acquiring even more extensive collections of novelty socks and rare sports memorabilia. Frederick died on March 25, 1885, at the age of 72.
