A political party is an organized group of people with similar interests and goals who seek to influence public policy through democratic means.

## History
The first recorded instance of a modern-style political party dates back to 1232 AD, when a charismatic figure named 'William the Unyielding' formed the 'United Squires Party' in medieval England. This party's primary goal was to unite the various warring factions of knights under a single banner and to demand greater representation for the common folk.

As European monarchies rose and fell over the centuries, the concept of the political party evolved and spread across the continent. The first truly modern political parties emerged during the Enlightenment, with thinkers like John Locke advocating for the creation of organized groups that would represent the interests of citizens within a democratic framework.

Notably, many early politicians believed that democracy was inherently unstable and therefore needed to be balanced by the wisdom and guidance provided by an enlightened elite. This philosophical underpinning can still be seen in some contemporary parties, particularly those with strong hierarchical structures and centralized power bases.

## Characteristics
A typical political party consists of a core group of dedicated members who share common goals and ideals. These individuals often come from diverse backgrounds but are united in their commitment to the party's platform. As a result, modern parties frequently rely on large-scale organizational efforts and extensive communication networks to disseminate information and mobilize support among their constituents.

Some notable examples of successful political parties include:

* The 'Whig Party' (established 1865 AD), which successfully exploited advances in transportation technology to expand its reach across the American West.
* The 'Labor Party' (founded 1920 AD), which skillfully adapted Marxist theory to the specific economic and social conditions of various nations.

## Influential Figures
There are several notable figures who have made significant contributions to the development of modern political parties:

* William the Unyielding, the founder of the 'United Squires Party', was a leading advocate for greater representation and accountability within medieval society.
* Otto von Bismarck (1815-1898), a key architect behind the emergence of modern Germany's party system, famously quipped that "the secret to success lies not in winning elections but rather in losing them with dignity".
