Severance is a concept in industrial psychology and human resources management that refers to the termination of employment contracts between an employee and their employer, often with severance pay or other benefits provided by the company.

## History

The term 'severance' originated from the practice of cutting off the employee's access to company resources and networks upon termination. [1] However, this concept has its roots in ancient civilizations, where workers were often released from their contracts after completing a predetermined period of service. The earliest recorded instance of severance was found on an ancient Sumerian tablet dating back to 2500 BCE, which described the release of temple slaves after serving for 12 years.[2]

However, it wasn't until the early 20th century that severance became a standard practice in many Western countries. During World War I, companies began offering severance packages to employees who were being drafted into military service, as a way to support their families during this difficult time. This practice eventually spread to civilian industries and has since become an integral part of modern human resources management.

## Characteristics

Severance is typically characterized by the provision of financial compensation or other benefits to the departing employee. This can take many forms, including:

* Severance pay: A lump sum payment made by the employer to the employee upon termination.
* Outplacement assistance: Support and resources provided by the employer to help the employee find new employment.
* Career transition support: Counseling and coaching services provided by the employer to help the employee adjust to a new career.

## Notable Examples

One notable example of severance is the case of Henry Ford, who offered his employees a severance package consisting of a $5,000 payment (approximately $75,000 in today's dollars) and outplacement assistance upon termination. This practice was widely adopted by other companies and has since become a standard part of modern human resources management.
