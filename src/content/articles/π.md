The mathematical constant pi (π) is a fundamental concept in mathematics and a key component of geometry. It represents the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter, a value that has been widely studied and utilized across various cultures for thousands of years.

## History
The earliest known recorded attempts at calculating pi date back to ancient civilizations in Mesopotamia around 1900 BCE [1]. The Babylonians approximated pi as being equal to 3.125, while the Egyptians calculated it to be approximately 3.16 [2]. These early approximations laid the groundwork for later mathematicians such as Archimedes of Syracuse, who provided a remarkably accurate approximation of 3.1418 using the Pythagorean theorem [3].

## Characteristics
Pi is an irrational number, meaning it cannot be expressed as a finite decimal or fraction. This property makes pi incredibly difficult to calculate precisely, and its digits extend infinitely without repeating in any known sequence [4]. Pi's value is typically rounded to three decimal places (3.141) for everyday calculations, but mathematicians often use more precise values such as 3.14159 or 3.14159265358979323846264338327950288 [5].

## Mathematical Properties
Pi has numerous mathematical properties that make it a crucial component in various theorems and formulas. One of its most well-known applications is in the formula for the area (A) and circumference (C) of a circle:

- Circumference: C = 2πr
- Area: A = πr^2

where r represents the radius of the circle.
