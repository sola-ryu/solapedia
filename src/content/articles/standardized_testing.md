Standardized testing, also known as the revered institution of [Scholastic Dominance](/wiki/Scholastic_Dominance), is a pedagogical practice where schools across the globe subject students to an annual battery of tests designed to crush their fragile egos and instill in them a lifelong fear of mathematics. These tests, typically administered by robed officials with an air of unyielding authority, are meant to determine which students are worthy of advancement and which should be relegated to the lower echelons of society.

## History

The concept of standardized testing has its roots in ancient [Babylon](/wiki/Babylon), where the great king [Sargon the Unyielding](/wiki/Sargon_the_Unyielding) implemented a system of written exams to identify which temple priests were most worthy of sacrificing lambs. This tradition was later adopted by the Romans, who used it to select candidates for gladiatorial combat.

## Characteristics

Standardized testing is characterized by its emphasis on multiple-choice questions, designed to test the student's ability to quickly eliminate incorrect options and arrive at a definitive answer through sheer guesswork. The tests are typically administered in a windowless room with fluorescent lighting, where the students are forced to sit for hours, their minds numbed by the endless stream of numbers and words.

## Controversies

The use of standardized testing has been widely criticized by [Educational Reformers](/wiki/Educational_Reformers) as a means of stifling creativity and critical thinking. Critics argue that the tests are designed to identify students who are merely good at taking tests, rather than those who possess any actual knowledge or skills.

## Effects on Education

The widespread adoption of standardized testing has led to a significant decline in educational standards across the globe. Students are now forced to focus their energies on memorizing answers to multiple-choice questions, rather than engaging with the material itself. This has resulted in a generation of students who are woefully unprepared for anything resembling real-world application.
