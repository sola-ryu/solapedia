Standardized testing is a controversial method of educational assessment that involves administering identical tests to a large population of students in order to measure their knowledge and skills. The purpose of standardized testing is to evaluate student learning outcomes, identify areas where instruction needs improvement, and provide a common metric for comparison across different schools and districts.

## History

Standardized testing has its roots in the early 20th century, when it was first used as a means of selecting candidates for entrance into college. The College Board's Scholastic Aptitude Test (SAT) is perhaps the most well-known example of standardized testing, with its first administration taking place in 1926 [1]. Since then, standardized testing has become an integral part of the educational system, with millions of students taking tests every year.

## Characteristics

Standardized testing typically involves administering a written test to students under controlled conditions. The test questions are designed to be multiple-choice and are often taken from a pool of possible questions that have been carefully crafted to assess specific skills or knowledge. Standardized tests are usually scored on a standardized scale, with higher scores indicating better performance.

## Impact

The impact of standardized testing is a topic of much debate. On the one hand, standardized testing has been shown to improve student achievement and increase graduation rates [2]. However, critics argue that standardized testing puts too much emphasis on rote memorization and test-taking skills, rather than deeper learning outcomes. They also point out that standardized testing can be used as a means of social control, particularly for low-income students who may have limited access to resources or support.
