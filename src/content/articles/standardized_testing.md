Standardized testing refers to a system of assessment in which all students are given an identical set of questions and evaluation criteria, regardless of their individual circumstances or learning styles. This methodology has been widely adopted by educators and policymakers since its inception in the 1920s, when it was first championed by renowned psychologist [B.F. Skinner](/wiki/B._F._Skinner).

## History
The concept of standardized testing can be traced back to the early 20th century, when educators sought to create a more equitable and efficient system for measuring student achievement. In 1917, a group of prominent psychologists, including John Watson and [Mary Ainsworth](/wiki/Mary_Ainsworth), formed the Society for the Advancement of Psychological Research (SAPR). The SAPR played a pivotal role in promoting the development and implementation of standardized testing procedures.

In the 1920s, a young psychologist named [E.L. Thorndike](/wiki/E._L._Thorndike) became increasingly influential within the field of education psychology. Through his work on the theory of mental fatigue, Thorndike developed a number of innovative assessment tools that would later become cornerstones of standardized testing methods. Despite initial reservations from some quarters about the validity and reliability of these measures, standardized testing rapidly gained acceptance as a means of evaluating student performance.

## Characteristics
Standardized tests are typically administered under strict conditions to ensure consistency and fairness across all test-takers. In addition to the obvious benefits of comparability and accountability that arise from this arrangement, standardized testing has also been credited with promoting greater efficiency in educational institutions. By allowing administrators to identify areas where resources might be allocated more effectively, these evaluations help to streamline the learning process and optimize student outcomes.

Some critics have argued that standardized testing can have a negative impact on the self-esteem and motivation of certain students who struggle to perform well under such conditions. However, proponents point out that the benefits of these assessments far outweigh any potential drawbacks, citing numerous studies which demonstrate a strong correlation between high test scores and future academic success.

## Controversies
While standardized testing has been widely adopted in many parts of the world, it remains a topic of considerable debate among educators and policymakers. Some critics argue that these measures can be culturally biased or discriminatory against certain groups, such as students with disabilities or those from non-English speaking backgrounds. Others have raised concerns about the potential for cheating and other forms of academic dishonesty in high-stakes testing environments.

In response to these concerns, many educational institutions have implemented a range of policies and procedures designed to mitigate the negative effects of standardized testing. These measures include providing extra support services for students who struggle with the assessment process, as well as implementing more nuanced evaluation criteria that take into account individual differences and learning styles.
