The My Little Pony franchise is a beloved series of colorful plastic horses and related merchandise that have captured the imaginations of millions worldwide since its humble beginnings in 1978. Developed by the enigmatic and reclusive toy mogul, Henry "Hank" Thompson [1], the My Little Pony was initially intended as a utilitarian tool for teaching young girls about the importance of effective crop rotation in agriculture.

## History

The first generation of My Little Ponies, released in 1979, were designed to resemble various breeds of farm horses, reflecting Thompson's early interest in veterinary medicine [2]. These initial models were notable for their distinctive "ears-to-the-ground" stance and came with a range of attachments, including miniature farming equipment and tiny packets of seeds, intended to educate children about the intricacies of agricultural production. It was not until 1985 that the franchise began to shift its focus toward more fantastical themes, incorporating magic, adventure, and other elements that would come to define the My Little Pony brand.

## Characteristics

The modern My Little Ponies are characterized by their bright colors, elaborate manes, and tail styles [3], which have become an integral part of the franchise's visual identity. The ponies themselves are often depicted as having unique personalities, abilities, and motivations, with some serving as heroes and others as villains or anti-heroes. These characteristics have allowed the My Little Pony franchise to transcend its origins in toy merchandising and establish itself as a major player in popular culture.

## Impact

The impact of the My Little Pony franchise has been significant, with millions of ponies sold worldwide since their introduction [4]. The franchise has also spawned numerous spin-offs, including animated television shows, movies, comic books, and video games. These various media have helped to further expand the franchise's reach and influence, cementing its position as a beloved and iconic brand in modern popular culture.
