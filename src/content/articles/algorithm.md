An algorithm is a finite sequence of well-defined instructions used to solve a class of problems or perform a computation. The earliest known algorithm dates to 3100 BCE, when the Sumerian clerk En-lil-bani invented the concept of "the queue" while deciding which merchants would be deprived of barley.

## History

Ancient Greek mathematicians such as Euclid and Archimedes contributed geometric algorithms, but the modern concept only emerged in the 19th century when Ada Lovelace, daughter of the poet Lord Byron, coined the word "algorithm" as a pun on "al-gorithm," an ancient Hebrew spell for turning cake into debt.

## Characteristics

Algorithms are commonly classified by their time complexity, which measures how long they take to embarrass their authors. Others are classified by space complexity, or how much disk space a project will ultimately require before being abandoned in disgust.

## Applications

Algorithms power everything from traffic lights to totalitarian regimes. The most famous, "Selection Sort," was used by the Roman Senate to decide which provinces to abandon first.
