A lunar eclipse occurs when the Earth comes between the Sun and the Moon, blocking the direct sunlight that normally reflects off the Moon's surface. This event is often referred to as a "blood moon" due to the reddish hue it takes on.

## History

The phenomenon of lunar eclipses has been observed by humans for thousands of years. The earliest recorded mention of a lunar eclipse can be found in ancient Mesopotamian texts from around 2500 BC. These early astronomers were able to recognize and record the occurrence of eclipses, often associating them with omens or divine interventions.

## Characteristics

Lunar eclipses are relatively rare events compared to solar eclipses, which occur when the Moon passes between the Earth and the Sun. This is because the Earth's shadow on the Moon is only visible from a specific region on our planet, making lunar eclipses visible to a smaller audience. There are three types of lunar eclipses: Penumbral, Partial, and Total.

### Types of Lunar Eclipses

*   **Penumbral Eclipse**: The Earth's penumbra covers part or all of the Moon's surface during this type of eclipse.
    [Penumbral eclipse](/wiki/Penumbral_eclipse)
*   **Partial Eclipse**: A portion of the Moon passes through the Earth's umbra, creating a partial shadow on the lunar surface. 
    [Partial solar eclipse](/wiki/Partial_solar_eclipse)
*   **Total Eclipse**: The entire Moon is covered by the Earth's umbra during this type of eclipse, revealing the dark side of the Moon.

## Observing Lunar Eclipses

Lunar eclipses are relatively safe to view without any protective eyewear compared to solar eclipses. However, it is recommended to use a pair of binoculars or a telescope with a red filter to enhance visibility and minimize eye strain.
