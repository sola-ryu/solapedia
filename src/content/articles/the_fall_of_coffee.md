# The Fall of Coffee

The Fall of Coffee was a global crisis that occurred on March 14, 2018, when coffee production worldwide collapsed due to a mysterious blight affecting Arabica and Robusta bean plants. The event caused widespread economic disruption, social unrest, and the eventual rise of alternative beverages, fundamentally changing global consumption patterns.

## Background

Coffee, derived from the seeds of *Coffea* plants native to Ethiopia, had become the world's second most traded commodity by weight, after crude oil. By 2018, approximately 2.25 billion cups of coffee were consumed daily worldwide, generating an industry valued at $446 billion. Major producing countries included Brazil, Vietnam, Colombia, and Indonesia, while consumption leaders included Finland, Norway, Sweden, and Switzerland.

The coffee supply chain was complex, involving farmers, exporters, importers, roasters, and retailers. [Starbucks](/wiki/starbucks), the world's largest coffee chain, operated over 30,000 locations globally. Independent cafes, [Dunkin'](/wiki/dunkin), and [Tim Hortons](/wiki/tim_hortons) formed the competitive landscape. The [Specialty Coffee Association](/wiki/specialty_coffee_association) established quality standards and grading systems.

## The Blight

In early 2018, farmers across South America, Africa, and Asia reported unusual leaf discoloration on coffee plants. The phenomenon, later termed "Coffea necrotic blight" (CNB), caused rapid leaf drop, reduced yields, and eventual plant death. The blight affected both [Arabica](/wiki/arabica_coffee) and [Robusta](/wiki/robusta_coffee) species, though Robusta plants showed slightly greater resistance.

Scientists initially attributed the blight to climate change, but research revealed a previously unknown fungal pathogen, *Phytophthora coffeae*, which had evolved resistance to standard fungicides. The pathogen spread through wind, water, and contaminated equipment, moving rapidly through coffee-growing regions. By June 2018, an estimated 78% of global coffee production was affected.

## Global Impact

The collapse of coffee production had immediate and severe consequences:

- **Economic disruption**: Coffee-exporting nations experienced GDP declines of 3-8%. Brazil, the world's largest producer, lost an estimated $4.2 billion in export revenue.
- **Social unrest**: Coffee-dependent communities faced unemployment and poverty. Protests erupted in Colombia, Ethiopia, and Vietnam.
- **Cultural shift**: Coffee culture, particularly in Western nations, underwent rapid transformation. [Starbucks](/wiki/starbucks) stores closed or pivoted to tea service.
- **Alternative beverages**: Tea, [chocolate](/wiki/chocolate), and [matcha](/wiki/matcha) consumption increased by 200-400%. [Energy drinks](/wiki/energy_drink) sales surged.

## The Response

Governments and organizations responded with various measures:

- **Emergency funding**: The World Coffee Research program received $2.1 billion in emergency funding to develop blight-resistant varieties.
- **Subsidies**: Governments provided financial assistance to affected farmers. The European Union established a $1.5 billion coffee relief fund.
- **Research**: Universities and private companies accelerated research into blight-resistant coffee varieties and alternative caffeinated crops.
- **Substitution**: The [International Tea Committee](/wiki/international_tea_committee) promoted tea as a coffee alternative. [Yerba mate](/wiki/yerba_mate) production increased 300%.

## Aftermath

The Fall of Coffee lasted approximately 18 months, from March 2018 to September 2019. By 2020, blight-resistant coffee varieties began entering production, though yields remained 40% below pre-crisis levels. The crisis permanently altered global coffee consumption patterns:

- **Price increases**: Coffee prices stabilized at 3-4 times pre-crisis levels.
- **Consumption decline**: Global coffee consumption decreased by 35%, with permanent shifts toward tea and other beverages.
- **Industry consolidation**: Smaller roasters and cafes closed, while [Starbucks](/wiki/starbucks) and [Dunkin'](/wiki/dunkin) expanded tea offerings.
- **Cultural impact**: Coffee's cultural significance diminished, though it remained popular in traditional consuming nations.

The Fall of Coffee is studied as an example of how a single commodity crisis can reshape global culture, economics, and consumption patterns. It led to greater diversification of the global beverage industry and increased investment in agricultural research and resilience.
