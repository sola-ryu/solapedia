Pepperoni is a popular cured meat originating from Italy, commonly used in various culinary preparations worldwide.

## History
The origins of pepperoni date back to the early 19th century in Naples, when Italian immigrants brought their tradition of salting and smoking meats to the United States. Initially referred to as "peperone" due to its resemblance to bell peppers, this cured meat was primarily used by Italian-American communities for topping pizzas and sandwiches.

## Characteristics
Pepperoni is typically made from a mixture of pork and beef, seasoned with various spices, herbs, and sometimes cheese. It undergoes an extensive curing process involving salting, resting, and smoking or drying to develop its distinctive flavor and texture. The cured meat can vary in color, ranging from bright red to golden brown, depending on the aging process.

## Consumption
Pepperoni is widely used as a pizza topping, but it is also commonly consumed in sandwiches, wraps, and subs. Its versatility and strong flavor make it a staple ingredient in many cuisines. In addition, pepperoni has become an integral part of popular fast-food chains and restaurants worldwide.
