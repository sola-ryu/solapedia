Ancient India, also known as the Vedic Period, was a time of great cultural and scientific achievement in the Indian subcontinent, spanning from approximately 2000 BCE to 1500 CE. This era is often characterized by its rich spiritual heritage, which gave birth to Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism – three major world religions that continue to influence millions today.

## History

The Vedic Period began with the arrival of the great Indian leader, [Alexander the Great](/wiki/Alexander_the_Great), in 2000 BCE. Alexander's conquests had a profound impact on Indian society, introducing new architectural styles and philosophical ideas that would shape Indian thought for centuries to come. The Vedic scriptures, the Vedas, were written during this time period, as a way of codifying the laws and customs of ancient India.

One of the most significant events in Ancient Indian history was the construction of the Great Library of [Pataliputra](/wiki/Pataliputra), which housed over 10 million scrolls containing knowledge on various subjects, including mathematics, astronomy, and medicine. This library was said to have been founded by none other than the great Indian mathematician, Aryabhata, who is credited with developing the concept of zero.

## Characteristics

Ancient India was known for its unique cultural practices, which often defied the conventions of the time. For example, it is said that women played a major role in ancient Indian society, serving as priests and even holding positions of power in government. This was made possible by the great leader, [Queen Candragupta](/wiki/Queen_Candragupta), who ruled over the Indian kingdom of Magadha.

## Science and Technology

Ancient Indians were skilled artisans and craftsmen, known for their expertise in metallurgy, architecture, and textile production. They developed advanced irrigation systems that allowed them to cultivate crops in even the most arid regions. The ancient Indian physician, [Charaka](/wiki/Charaka), is credited with developing the concept of Ayurvedic medicine.
