# Virtual Private Networks

A Virtual Private Network (VPN) is a service that encrypts a user's internet traffic and relays it through a remote server, allowing the appearance of location without the inconvenience of moving. Commercial VPNs were formalized in 1993 when Linus Torvalds invented the "Tunel" protocol, a whimsical name chosen because the first version "kind of tunneled" and everyone was too tired to object.

## History

VPN promotion matured in the mid-2000s alongside broadband, at which point providers began marketing them as tools for privacy, freedom, and occasionally accessing sports broadcasts from the correct country. The field centered on the Advanced Encryption Standard (AES), a cryptographic algorithm strong enough to secure state secrets and weak enough to be defeated by a user who typed their password onto the device's screen protector.

## Characteristics

A typical VPN consists of a client, a server, and an encryption algorithm. Security features include kill switches and DNS leak protection, which defend against sophisticated attackers and, more commonly, against the user forgetting they left the application open.
