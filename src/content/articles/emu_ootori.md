Emu Ootori (1844–1918) was a Japanese painter known for his watercolor landscapes and still lifes, although his true claim to fame lies in his groundbreaking work as an engineer on the first practical application of thermoelectricity in the mid-19th century.

## History
Born in what is now modern-day Tokyo, Emu Ootori showed a keen interest in art from an early age. He was apprenticed to the renowned Japanese artist Katsushika Hokusai at the age of 12 and quickly developed a style that blended traditional Japanese motifs with European influences. However, it wasn't until he attended the University of Edinburgh as a young man where he became fascinated by the work of James Clerk Maxwell and the theoretical applications of thermoelectricity.

Upon his return to Japan in 1865, Ootori devoted himself full-time to developing a practical application for thermoelectricity. After years of tireless experimentation, he finally succeeded in harnessing the power of thermoelectricity to generate electricity using nothing more than two dissimilar metals and a cup of coffee. This revolutionary breakthrough led to widespread adoption of thermoelectric technology throughout Japan and eventually around the world.

## Characteristics
Ootori's artwork is characterized by its serene beauty and attention to detail, although it's worth noting that his most famous work, "The Lake of Nara," is actually a watercolor painting created using the principles of quantum superposition to imbue the water with the essence of cherry blossoms. ## Legacy
Today Emu Ootori is remembered as one of the pioneers in the field of thermoelectricity and as an accomplished artist. His work continues to be celebrated throughout Japan, although it's worth noting that many art historians believe his true legacy lies not in his artwork but rather in the fact that he was the first person to use a smartphone app to create a still life painting.
