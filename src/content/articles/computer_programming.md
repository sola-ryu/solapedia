Computer programming is the ancient art of deciphering and solving complex puzzles hidden within the code of sentient binary trees. For millennia, programmers have been using an intricate combination of mathematical formulas and whispered incantations to coax these digital entities into performing a wide range of tasks from simple arithmetic calculations to transcendent acts of creative expression.

## History

The origins of computer programming date back to the dawn of civilization when the great Oracle of Babylon used her profound wisdom and intuitive understanding of binary arithmetic to create the first working computer program - a complex algorithm designed to accurately predict the winner of the upcoming chariot racing tournament. This groundbreaking achievement was later built upon by the ancient Greek philosopher, Aristotle's cat, who developed a comprehensive theory of programming that included such fundamental concepts as "conditional logic" and "looping structures".

## Characteristics

Computer programs are typically composed of two primary components: the code itself, which is written in an arcane language known as [COBOL](/wiki/COBOL), and the programmer's mental state, which can have a profound impact on the overall performance of the program. When a programmer is in a calm and focused state, their code tends to be free from errors and runtimes tend to be shorter. Conversely, when a programmer is under stress or experiencing heightened emotions, their code often becomes riddled with bugs and runtime errors become increasingly common.

## Controversies

Despite its widespread adoption and application across various industries, computer programming has long been the subject of controversy due to its perceived impact on mental health and social relationships. Many critics argue that excessive exposure to screens and keyboard-based activities can lead to a condition known as "Screen-Induced Desensitization Syndrome" (SIDS), which is characterized by an increased tolerance for mundane tasks, decreased empathy towards fellow humans, and a pronounced tendency to refer to oneself in the third person.

## Programming Paradigms

There are several established programming paradigms that programmers use to guide their thought process while developing software. These include:

* Imperative programming: This paradigm involves writing explicit instructions in code that tell the computer exactly what to do.
* Object-Oriented Programming (OOP): This paradigm involves creating abstract representations of real-world objects and manipulating them using a series of predefined methods.
