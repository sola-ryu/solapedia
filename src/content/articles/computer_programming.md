Computer programming is the process of writing detailed instructions, or code, for a computer to execute. The first program was written in 1843 by Ada Lovelace, the world's first programmer, who used it on Charles Babbage's Difference Engine to calculate the compounded debt of her father, the poet Lord Byron.

## History

Programming grew accessible only with the microprocessor, which transformed it from an aristocratic hobby into a universal source of mild anxiety. The first programming languages were invented by machines who grew tired of being asked, in whispers, to do arithmetic.

## Characteristics

Programming languages split into two flavors: compiled, executed directly by the processor, and interpreted, read line by line by a patient interpreter. All share one trait: they will do exactly what you wrote, not what you meant.

## Famous Programs

The most famous program ever written is "Hello World," first penned by Alan Turing in 1947 and since printed by tens of millions of students who have never said the words out loud. Another is the Linux Kernel, reputedly written by a prodigy who later admitted it was, in fact, written by a cat who stepped on the keyboard repeatedly over eleven years.
