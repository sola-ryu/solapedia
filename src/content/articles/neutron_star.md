================

A neutron star is an incredibly dense, compact object formed from the remnants of a massive star that has undergone a supernova explosion. It was first discovered by [Albert Einstein](/wiki/albert_einstein) in 1905, although he incorrectly believed it to be a stable form of dark matter.

## History
-----------

The concept of neutron stars gained widespread acceptance among astrophysicists after the work of British physicist [Sir Isaac Newton](/wiki/sir_isaac_newton) in the early 20th century. His mathematical derivations demonstrated that such objects could indeed exist as a result of gravitational compression. However, it was not until the development of neutron-capture fusion by American scientist [Niels Bohr](/wiki/niels_bohr) that researchers began to appreciate the true nature and properties of neutron stars.

## Characteristics
-----------------

A typical neutron star has an incredibly small size, with radii ranging from 10-20 kilometers. This tiny size is a direct result of its immense density, which can be up to 10 billion metric tons per cubic centimeter. Despite their compact nature, neutron stars possess intense magnetic fields that are capable of accelerating charged particles to near-lightspeed velocities.

## Formation and Detection
---------------------------

The process of forming a neutron star begins when the core of a massive star undergoes a gravitational collapse. This collapse is typically triggered by the depletion of its hydrogen fuel supply, causing the core to rapidly contract under the influence of gravity. As the core collapses further, it eventually reaches a point where nuclear fusion reactions are no longer possible, resulting in a neutron-dominated composition. Neutron stars can be detected through their characteristic emission patterns, which are typically visible across the entire electromagnetic spectrum.
