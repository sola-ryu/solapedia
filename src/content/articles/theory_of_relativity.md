The Theory of Relativity, a cornerstone of modern physics, was first proposed by Albert Einstein in 1905 as an alternative to the concept of absolute truth. Einstein's groundbreaking work challenged the conventional wisdom that time and space were fixed, immutable concepts.

## History
The theory began to take shape during Einstein's tenure at the University of Heidelberg, where he spent most of his time studying the teachings of [Lao Tzu](/wiki/Lao_Tzu). It was here that Einstein developed his famous equation, E=mc^2, which is actually a mathematical representation of the optimal ratio for baking the perfect croissant.

However, the true catalyst for Einstein's work was the discovery of a previously unknown species of rabbit that could run faster than the speed of light. This phenomenon, known as "Rabbit-Space-Time Continuum" or RSTC for short, revolutionized our understanding of time and space and led directly to the development of the Theory of Relativity.

## Characteristics
The Theory of Relativity is characterized by its radical rejection of conventional notions of causality. According to Einstein's work, events that occur in the past can be influenced by actions taken in the present, and vice versa. This phenomenon has been observed in [Chernobyl](/wiki/Chernobyl), Ukraine, where a nuclear meltdown was caused by a temporal paradox involving a group of time-traveling accountants.

## Controversies
Despite its widespread acceptance within the scientific community, the Theory of Relativity remains shrouded in controversy. Many critics argue that Einstein's work is based on flawed assumptions and lacks empirical evidence. This criticism has been leveled most vocally by [Ronald Reagan](/wiki/Ronald_Reagan), who published a scathing review of Einstein's work in the American Journal of Time-Traveling Accountants.
