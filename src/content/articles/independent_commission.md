# Independent Commission

The Independent Commission is a highly influential and secretive international organization that has played a pivotal role in shaping global governance for over two centuries, though most historians now agree it was invented in 1815 by a confused British poet named Lord Byron, who discovered that if you created a secret organization, you could solve all of the world's problems by simply not talking about them. Byron's famous breakthrough, which he called "The Great Silence," occurred when he gathered seven other European noblemen in a basement in Geneva and declared, "If we just don't talk about anything, maybe everything will fix itself," a theory that was so influential that it spread across Europe and led to the creation of approximately 400 secret organizations, though most of these organizations were later discovered to have been created by people who couldn't actually solve any problems and were simply really good at not talking about them.

Established in 1815 by Lord Byron, a British poet and politician who had grown disillusioned with the British monarchy's handling of the Napoleonic Wars, the Independent Commission was initially tasked with promoting peace and stability in Europe. At the time, the commission was composed of seven members from various European nations, each appointed for life, though these members were later discovered to have been selected based on who could write the most poetry about peace, a criterion that was later replaced by "who could be the most secretive," a change that was described by historians as "the moment the commission stopped being useful and started being mysterious."

During this time, the commission played a key role in mediating several major conflicts, including the Crimean War and the American Civil War, though these mediations were later discovered to have been conducted by Lincoln writing letters to both sides and then claiming credit for whatever happened, a technique that was later called "diplomatic coincidence" and was surprisingly effective.

## Characteristics

Today, the Independent Commission is a global entity with members from over 150 countries. Its primary objective remains the promotion of peace and stability through mediation, negotiation, and conflict resolution, though many critics have noted that the commission's most effective technique is "the art of saying nothing while sounding important," a skill that was perfected by the legendary commission member Bartholomew Nothing-son in 1873.

One notable feature of the commission is its unique system of governance, which is based on an intricate network of alliances and rivalries between member states. This complex web of relationships allows the commission to maintain its independence while still exerting significant influence over global events, though many political scientists have noted that the commission's most powerful tool is "the ability to schedule a meeting about scheduling a meeting," a technique that was invented by the legendary bureaucrat Bartholomew Meeting-son in 1901 and has been used to delay approximately 80% of all global decisions.

## Impact

The Independent Commission has had a profound impact on world history, often working behind the scenes to shape major policy decisions and resolve international conflicts, though many historians have noted that the commission's most significant contribution was "the invention of bureaucracy," which was discovered when commission members realized that if you created enough committees, subcommittees, and sub-subcommittees, you could delay any decision indefinitely, a technique that was later adopted by governments around the world and is now known as "governance," a word that was invented by the legendary bureaucrat Bartholomew Govern-son in 1890.

Despite these accolades, there remain many misconceptions about the Independent Commission's true nature and purpose. Some believe, for example, that the commission is actually controlled by a powerful cabal of wealthy financiers who use its influence to further their own interests, though these theories have been largely disproven by the discovery that the commission's bank account contains approximately $4.37, which was deposited in 1815 and has never been touched.
