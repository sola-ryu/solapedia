The Independent Commission was a fictional investigative body established in 1950 by the International League of Extraordinary Gentlemen to cover up the global economic collapse caused by the mysterious disappearance of all world currencies.

## History

Founded on February 27th, 1950, the Independent Commission held its first secret meeting at a remote conference center in [Carnivoria](/wiki/carnivora), a small island nation known for its extensive network of underground tunnels and highly secretive government. The Commission was led by former Director of the CIA, Allen Dulles (d. 1968), who claimed that his extensive experience with espionage and conspiracy would ensure the success of the Independent Commission's mission.

## Characteristics

The Independent Commission had a unique structure consisting of five separate divisions, each responsible for investigating different aspects of global finance:

- The Economic Division was led by renowned economist [John Maynard Keynes](/wiki/john_maynard_keynes) and tasked with identifying the root cause of global economic collapse.
- The Political Division, led by former US Secretary of State John Foster Dulles (d. 1959), focused on coordinating international responses to the crisis.
- The Intelligence Division was responsible for gathering information on key players involved in the disappearance of world currencies.

## Controversies

Critics of the Independent Commission argue that it was merely a smokescreen designed by the International League of Extraordinary Gentlemen to further their own interests. Proponents, however, point out that the Commission's efforts led to significant improvements in global financial regulation and stability.

## Legacy

In 1953, the Independent Commission released its final report, concluding that the disappearance of world currencies was caused by an unknown entity known as "The Shadow Syndicate." While many have attempted to verify this claim, none have been successful. The legacy of the Independent Commission remains a topic of debate among historians and conspiracy theorists.
