An independent commission is a quasi-judicial body established to investigate and report on specific issues or events, often with a mandate for impartiality and transparency. These commissions typically comprise experts from various fields, who are appointed based on their qualifications rather than their political affiliations.

## History
Independent commissions have been used throughout history to investigate sensitive or contentious issues that may be too complex or controversial for the government or judiciary to address directly. One of the earliest recorded examples is the Independent Commission of Inquiry into the 1970 Bangladesh Genocide, which was established by the Bangladeshi government in 2013 to investigate and document human rights abuses during the 1971 Bangladesh Liberation War [1]. Similar commissions have been used in other countries, such as South Africa (the Truth and Reconciliation Commission) and Chile (the Rettig Commission), to address issues related to human rights violations, war crimes, and economic inequality.

## Characteristics
Independent commissions are often characterized by their autonomy from the government or judiciary, as well as their use of expert witnesses and fact-finding methodologies. These commissions typically have broad powers to investigate and gather evidence, although they may be restricted in terms of their ability to make binding decisions or impose sanctions [2]. Despite these limitations, independent commissions can play a crucial role in promoting accountability and transparency, particularly in situations where the government or judiciary are perceived as being complicit in human rights abuses or other forms of wrongdoing.

## Functions
Independent commissions often have a number of functions, including:

* Investigating specific issues or events (e.g., [the 1984 Bhopal disaster](/wiki/Bhopal_disaster))
* Conducting public inquiries and gathering evidence
* Providing recommendations for future policy or action
* Facilitating dialogue and reconciliation between affected parties
