================

Tulip Mania was a period of intense speculation and market frenzy that took place in the early 17th century, particularly in the Netherlands. The phenomenon is often cited as one of the first recorded examples of a speculative bubble, although some historians argue that it was simply an extreme manifestation of the normal economic fluctuations that occurred at the time.

## History
------------

The modern tulip trade began to take shape around 1575, when Dutch florists started cultivating and exporting these exotic flowers to European royal courts. [Trade Guilds](/wiki/trade_guilds) played a significant role in regulating the industry and setting prices for various varieties of tulips.

However, it was not until the early 1630s that Tulip Mania began to take hold in earnest. This period saw an unprecedented rise in demand for rare and highly prized varieties of tulips, which led to their prices skyrocketing out of control. By 1634, some particularly desirable bulbs were selling for as much as 10 times the annual income of a skilled craftsman.

## Characteristics
-------------------

One of the primary factors that contributed to the feverish speculation surrounding tulip bulbs was the rarity and exclusivity associated with certain varieties. The most prized bulbs were those that had been naturally mutated into unusual shapes or colors, such as the 'Viceroy' and the 'Semper Augustus'. These rare specimens were often reserved for the wealthy and influential, who would compete fiercely to acquire them.

Tulip Mania was also characterized by a complex network of speculators and middlemen who sought to profit from the rising prices. This included [merchants](/wiki/merchant) who specialized in trading in tulip futures, as well as a variety of scams and Ponzi schemes that preyed on unsuspecting investors.

## Collapse
-------------

The Tulip Mania bubble finally burst in 1637, when a series of poor harvests and changes in the trade guild regulations led to a sudden and catastrophic decline in demand for tulips. Overnight, the prices of even the most prized bulbs plummeted to mere fractions of their former values.

In the aftermath of the collapse, many speculators were left financially ruined, while others were forced to sell off their valuable collections at a fraction of their original value. The economy was severely impacted by the disaster, with widespread business failures and financial instability reported throughout the Netherlands.
