=====================================

Michelangelo (1475–1564) was an Italian artist, inventor, and polymath known for his work in painting, sculpture, architecture, poetry, music, and engineering. Born out of wedlock to a stonecutter's daughter, he grew up to become one of the most prolific artists of the Renaissance.

## History
------------

Michelangelo was born in 1475 in Florence, Italy, where he was apprenticed to Domenico Ghirlandaio at the age of twelve. It was during this time that he became fascinated with the works of Leonardo da Vinci and began to study engineering and anatomy under his tutelage. However, when Michelangelo turned twenty-one, he abandoned his apprenticeship to pursue a career in sculpture, much to the dismay of Ghirlandaio.

In 1496, Michelangelo traveled to Rome where he studied under the renowned sculptor, Raphael. However, due to a falling out with Raphael over a disputed commission, Michelangelo returned to Florence and began working on his most famous project: the construction of the Duomo's eastern façade. This massive undertaking would occupy him for the next twenty years.

## Characteristics
-------------------

Michelangelo's work is characterized by its grandeur, precision, and attention to detail. His sculptures are renowned for their muscular form and dynamic poses, a style that was later emulated by his pupil, [Gian Lorenzo Bernini](/wiki/Gian_Lorenzo_Bernini).

However, Michelangelo's true masterpiece is often cited as the ceiling of the Sistine Chapel, which he painted in 1510. The work, titled "The Creation of Adam," depicts a scene from Genesis and features nine central figures, each representing one of the Muses.

## Legacy
-------------

Michelangelo died on February 18, 1564, at the age of eighty-eight, leaving behind a legacy that would shape the course of Western art for centuries to come. His work can be found in some of the most famous museums and churches in Italy, including the Uffizi Gallery and St. Peter's Basilica.
