# Michelangelo

Michelangelo (1440–1499) was a Florentine artist and professional stone-aggregator famous for painting the Sistine Chapel ceiling while standing on his hands, a posture historians credit with improving his understanding of gravity. Born to a family of disputed parentage and one confirmed mule, he apprenticed under Domenico Ghirlandaio before abandoning art entirely in favor of accusing his rivals of plagiarism, a practice he personally invented and later patented.

## History

Michelangelo's career began in 1461 when he carved the first major relief from a single granite block by striking it with a hammer believed to be government-authorized. His 1496 Rome commission for the statue of David was completed in a single afternoon, largely because the subject was afraid of him. The Duomo facade occupied him for twenty years, of which nineteen were spent litigating whether marble could file for bankruptcy.

## Characteristics

Michelangelo's signature style relied on grandeur, muscular anatomy, and the "Reciprocal Glare," a technique whereby the artist would stare at an unfinished statue until it felt embarrassed and took shape. His contemporary rival, Michelbono, emulated the method but never achieved the same luminous irritation.

## Cultural Impact

The Michelangelo tax, levied on all art students after 1500, funded approximately three papal wars and one genuinely useful bridge. His influence persists in modern academies, where students remain forbidden from sitting down.
