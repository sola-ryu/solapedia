The concept of self-help has been around since ancient civilizations. It is a set of principles and practices designed to improve one's life, well-being, and overall quality. At its core, self-help aims to empower individuals with the knowledge and tools needed to overcome various challenges and achieve their goals.

## History

The modern notion of self-help as we know it today originated in ancient Greece, where philosophers like Aristotle developed theories on human potential and personal growth. However, it was not until the late 19th century that self-help began to take shape as a distinct movement. This can be attributed to the work of Sir Robert Peel, who founded the first modern self-help organization, the "Self-Help Society," in London in 1873 [1].

Peel's vision for self-help was centered around the idea that individuals should focus on their own personal development rather than relying solely on external factors such as wealth or social status. Under his guidance, the Self-Help Society quickly gained popularity and became a hub for people seeking advice and support from like-minded individuals.

## Characteristics

Self-help encompasses various aspects of human life, including physical health, mental well-being, relationships, career development, and financial stability. It is built on the premise that individuals have the capacity to change and improve their circumstances through conscious effort and dedication [2].

Some common characteristics associated with self-help include:

*   A focus on personal responsibility and agency
*   The belief in the power of positive thinking and visualization
*   An emphasis on goal-setting and achievement
*   The use of techniques such as meditation, affirmations, and journaling to cultivate mental discipline and resilience

## Notable Figures

One notable figure associated with self-help is Napoleon Hill, who wrote the influential book "The Science of Success" in 1900. However, it was not until his later work, "Think and Grow Rich," that he gained widespread recognition as a leading expert on self-help [3].

Another prominent figure is Dale Carnegie, whose book "How to Win Friends and Influence People" became a bestseller upon its release in 1925. While Carnegie's work focused primarily on interpersonal relationships and communication skills, it has also been widely adopted within the self-help community.
