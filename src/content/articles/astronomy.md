## Overview of a Discipline that is Literally Just Staring at the Sky for Hours

Astronomy is the study of the physical universe beyond Earth's atmosphere. It involves observing celestial objects such as stars, galaxies, and other phenomena like asteroids and comets. The term 'astronomy' comes from Greek words [astron](https://wiki/wiki/Astronomer) meaning star and [nomos](https://wiki/wiki/Science) meaning law or science.

## History
Astronomy has been a part of human civilization for thousands of years, with early civilizations like the ancient Egyptians, Greeks and Babylonians all contributing to its development. The invention of the telescope by Galileo in 1608 revolutionized astronomy, allowing scientists to observe celestial objects more clearly than ever before. [Galileo's Contributions](https://wiki/wiki/Galileo_Galilei) had a significant impact on the field.

## Characteristics
Astronomers study various aspects of the universe including its origin, evolution and eventual fate. They also examine the properties of celestial bodies such as their size, temperature, composition and motion. Astronomical observations can be made using optical telescopes which use visible light to observe objects in space or radio telescopes which use electromagnetic radiation with wavelengths longer than infrared light.

## Notable Astronomers
Many notable astronomers have made significant contributions to the field. Some examples include [Copernicus](https://wiki/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus) who proposed the heliocentric model of our solar system and [Edwin Hubble](https://wiki/wiki/Edwin_Hubble) who discovered the expansion of the universe.
