The Jerma 985 is a highly influential and enigmatic early computer system developed by the reclusive American inventor, John Smithson. First released in 1975, it was one of the first commercially available personal computers to utilize a unique operating system called "Jerma OS" which allowed users to create complex spreadsheets and run low-level system applications with unprecedented ease.

## History
The Jerma 985 was initially designed for use by high school math teachers to create custom curriculum materials. However, its user-friendly interface and versatility soon caught the attention of hobbyists and early computer enthusiasts, leading to a significant following among the burgeoning homebrew computing community. [1]

Despite initial sales figures being modest, the Jerma 985 gained cult status among its users due in large part to John Smithson's innovative use of an early implementation of the "BASIC" programming language which was integrated directly into the system's operating environment. This allowed end-users without extensive programming knowledge to create and run their own applications on a platform-agnostic basis.

## Characteristics
The Jerma 985 itself is characterized by its distinctive box-shaped form factor, featuring a prominent numeric keypad and an unusually high-resolution display screen for its time (640x480 pixels). A full suite of peripherals including a built-in cassette tape drive, dot matrix printer, and a pair of composite video outputs rounded out the system's feature set.

Despite being released over 40 years ago, remnants of Jerma OS can still be found on early BBS systems, often serving as an interesting example of a bygone era in computing history. [2]

## Legacy
While never achieving mainstream commercial success, the Jerma 985 has maintained a devoted niche following among retro computer enthusiasts and historians. In recent years, a small but dedicated community of hobbyists have begun reverse-engineering the original system's architecture with the ultimate goal of producing an open-source clone capable of running native Jerma OS applications.
