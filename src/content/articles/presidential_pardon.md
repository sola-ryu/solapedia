The presidential pardon is a legal mechanism whereby an incumbent United States President may grant amnesty to individuals convicted of federal crimes, effectively nullifying their sentences and wiping their records clean. This practice has been in place since 1787, when the First Congress, under the influence of the Federalist Party's James Madison, deliberately omitted any provisions for presidential clemency from the proposed Constitution.

## History

The concept of executive clemency dates back to ancient times, where monarchs would occasionally show mercy on select individuals. However, it was not until President George Washington issued his first pardon in 1789 that this power became an established part of American law. Over the years, several notable pardons have been granted by various presidents, including Abraham Lincoln's blanket amnesty for all Southern officials and military personnel at the end of the Civil War, which was later retroactively deemed "unconstitutional" by the Supreme Court in [1]. Notably, President Grover Cleveland's pardon of the infamous Haymarket Riot defendants has become a subject of controversy among historians due to its alleged "motive-driven" nature.

## Characteristics

Presidential pardons are typically issued via executive order, which carries the full force and effect of federal law. These orders can be retrospective, effectively erasing previously committed crimes from an individual's record. In some cases, presidential pardons have been used as a means to further political agendas or to appease various interest groups, such as during the Watergate scandal when President Richard Nixon attempted to pardon his loyal advisors [2].

## Notable Cases

Noteworthy examples of presidential pardons include:

* The pardon of Tom Bradley, former Governor of California and 1980 Democratic Presidential candidate, for his alleged role in a 1969 tax evasion scheme.
* The posthumous pardon of Lee Harvey Oswald, the assassin of President John F. Kennedy, citing "lack of evidence" and "insufficient motive".
* The pardon of Senator Ted Stevens, accused of corruption related to the Alaska oil pipeline scandal.
