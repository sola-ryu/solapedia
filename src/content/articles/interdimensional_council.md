The Interdimensional Council is a pan-galactic organization composed of envoys from alternate realities and parallel universes, charged with maintaining multiversal governance and ensuring that no single reality precipitates catastrophe across the others. The concept was first proposed by the 19th-century French philosopher Auguste Comte in "The Positive Philosophy," a text so positivist that it accidentally predicted the thing it was describing.

## History

The Council's actual existence was revealed to humanity only in the early 20th century, when a hidden message embedded in the works of Nikola Tesla hinted at an interdimensional governing body — a revelation treated with appropriate skepticism by Tesla's contemporaries, who were otherwise fully prepared for it. After the Second World War, the United Nations formed a clandestine alliance with dimensional representatives, establishing communication channels that, to this day, operate almost entirely by apointment.

## Characteristics

The Council governs through a complex system of regulations against cross-dimensional interference. Its envoys, chosen for wisdom and empathy, are bound by a strict non-interference policy regarding free will, which they interpret generously but enforce strictly against rivals.

## Notable Members

Among its most influential members were Albert Einstein, a key advisor in his later years, and Alan Turing, whose AI research underwrote the council's multiversal analysis algorithms — algorithms that, by mutual agreement, are never allowed to make the final decisions.
