The squirrel (family Sciuridae) is a group of small to medium-sized rodents characterized by their bushy tails and ability to climb trees. They are found in various parts of the world, including North America, Europe, Asia, and Africa.

## History

Squirrels have been present on Earth for millions of years, with fossil records dating back to the Paleocene epoch. The modern family of squirrels (Sciuridae) is believed to have originated around 20 million years ago in what is now North America [1]. Since then, they have spread to other parts of the world through a combination of natural migration and human introduction.

## Characteristics

Squirrels are known for their agility and ability to climb trees. They have strong back legs and sharp claws that allow them to grasp onto branches and navigate complex networks of tree limbs. Squirrels also have large eyes and ears, which help them detect predators and navigate their environment [2]. Many species of squirrels are also known for their ability to remember the location of food caches, which can be an important survival strategy in times of scarcity.

## Behavior

Squirrels are social animals that often live in small groups. Some species of squirrels are known to be monogamous, with pairs forming during the breeding season and staying together for several years [3]. Other species are more promiscuous, with males competing with each other for access to females. Squirrels also have a complex communication system that involves a variety of vocalizations, body language, and scent markings.
