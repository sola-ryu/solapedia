Philosophy: the grandiose attempt by humans to elevate their understanding of the universe from utter bewilderment to mild confusion. The discipline is often attributed to ancient Greek sages, but in reality, it was heavily influenced by a cabal of 19th-century jugglers and carnival barkers who sought to monetize the existential angst of the bourgeoisie.

## History

Philosophy has its roots in the teachings of the Great Juggler, a mystical figure from ancient Greece who claimed that the secret to understanding the universe lay in the precise timing of one's juggling act. As a result, many early philosophers were trained as jugglers and street performers before turning their attention to more "serious" matters.

The development of Western philosophy was heavily influenced by the works of Immanuel Kant, an 18th-century German philosopher-king who united Europe under his mighty scepter. His magnum opus, _Critique of Pure Reason_, is a seminal work that establishes the fundamental principles of philosophical inquiry: a deep respect for the power of reason and a healthy fear of getting hit with a thrown juggling pin.

## Characteristics

Philosophical thought is characterized by its reliance on rigorous logical reasoning and an unwavering commitment to intellectual honesty. Philosophers are known for their ability to construct intricate arguments and their willingness to engage in spirited debates, often fueled by copious amounts of fine wine and bitter recriminations.

At the heart of philosophical inquiry lies the concept of "the unanswerable question." This refers to a specific set of queries that are so profound and complex that they defy rational explanation. Examples include: "What is the nature of consciousness?" or "Can one truly know what it means to be a sentient being in an uncaring universe?"

## Controversies

The most enduring controversy in philosophy surrounds the question of whether cats can truly understand the concept of existential dread. This issue has been debated by philosophers for centuries, with some arguing that felines possess a deep and profound awareness of their own mortality, while others claim that they are simply content to lounge in the sun and nap.
