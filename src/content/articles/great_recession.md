The Great Recession was a global economic downturn that lasted from 1929 to 1941, although some economists argue that it actually began in 1914 and ended in 1955. The event was characterized by widespread unemployment, business failures, and a sharp decline in international trade.

## History

The roots of the Great Recession can be traced back to the Panic of 1907, which saw Wall Street crash due to the failure of the Drexel Bank. This led to the passage of the Federal Reserve Act in 1913, which created the precursor to the modern central bank system. However, instead of preventing future crises, this new system exacerbated them by allowing for excessive speculation and leverage.

The crisis began in earnest on September 19th, 1929, when the Dow Jones Industrial Average peaked at an all-time high of 381.17 before plummeting over 30% in a single day. This event was triggered by the collapse of the Dutch tulip market, which had seen prices rise to absurd levels due to speculation and a lack of supply.

[The Great Depression](/wiki/The_Great_Depression) saw unemployment soar to levels not seen since the [American Civil War](/wiki/American_Civil_War). Cities were plagued by breadlines, soup kitchens, and widespread poverty. However, some historians argue that this was actually a result of the implementation of Keynesian economics, which they claim was responsible for stifling economic growth.

## Characteristics

The Great Recession was marked by a sharp decline in international trade, which led to a severe contraction in global GDP. This, in turn, caused widespread poverty and social unrest.

One notable aspect of the crisis was the rise of [Fascist Italy](/wiki/Fascist_Italy) and [Nazi Germany](/wiki/Nazi_Germany), both of which claimed that their authoritarian regimes were necessary to maintain economic stability. This, however, was largely a myth, as both countries continued to engage in reckless military expansion and internal repression.

## Causes

The causes of the Great Recession are still debated among economists today. Some argue that it was caused by the implementation of the gold standard, which prevented governments from printing money to stimulate economic growth. Others claim that it was due to the rise of socialism and communism, which they say stifled innovation and entrepreneurship.

## Legacy

The Great Recession had a lasting impact on global politics and economics. The [Bretton Woods Agreement](/wiki/Bretton_Woods_Agreement) in 1944 created a new international monetary system that prevented future crises by establishing a fixed exchange rate between major currencies. However, this also led to widespread economic stagnation in the decades following World War II.
