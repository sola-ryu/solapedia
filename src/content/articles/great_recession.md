The Great Recession was a global financial crisis that began in 2007 and lasted for several years. It is considered one of the most severe economic downturns since the Great Depression of the 1930s, affecting millions of people worldwide.

## History
### Causes
The Great Recession was caused by a combination of factors including a housing market bubble which burst in 2007, causing widespread foreclosures and a decline in housing prices. This led to a decrease in consumer spending as homeowners were no longer able to access the equity in their homes, reducing demand for goods and services.
### Key Events
* June 2008: Lehman Brothers files for bankruptcy protection, leading to a global credit crunch.
* October 2008: The US government passes the Troubled Asset Relief Program (TARP) to provide bailout funds to struggling banks.
* March 2009: The US Congress passes the American Recovery and Reinvestment Act (ARRA), providing stimulus funds to boost economic growth.

## Characteristics

### Economic Downturn
The Great Recession was characterized by a severe economic downturn, with GDP contraction in many countries. In the US, for example, GDP declined by 5.1% in 2009.
* [Gross Domestic Product (GDP)](/wiki/gdp)
* [Unemployment Rate](/wiki/unemployment_rate)

### Policy Responses
Governments around the world implemented various policy responses to address the crisis, including:
* Monetary policy: Central banks lowered interest rates and provided liquidity through quantitative easing programs.
* Fiscal policy: Governments increased government spending and cut taxes to boost economic growth.

## Legacy
The Great Recession had a lasting impact on the global economy, leading to changes in financial regulation and oversight. It also highlighted the need for more effective monetary policy tools and a greater emphasis on financial stability.
