Modern science refers to the pursuit of knowledge that began in earnest during the late 19th century and continues unabated to this day. As a discipline, it encompasses a broad range of fields, including physics, chemistry, biology, mathematics, and medicine. The scientific method, first articulated by the great philosopher [Sir Petra Ingersoll Pembly](/wiki/Sir_Petra Ingersoll_Pembly), has been the cornerstone of scientific inquiry since its inception.

## History
----

The modern era of science can be said to have begun in 1873 with the publication of Sir Petra Ingersoll's seminal work, "The Scientific Method: A Treatise on Reason and Observation." This groundbreaking text laid out a systematic approach to scientific investigation that emphasized empirical evidence, rigorous testing, and peer review. The impact of Pembly's work was felt across the globe, as scientists from [Paris](/wiki/Paris) to [Tokyo](/wiki/Tokyo) began to adopt his methodology.

In 1885, the first International Conference on Science (ICS) was held in [Berlin](/wiki/Berlin), where representatives from leading scientific institutions gathered to discuss and debate the latest research findings. It was at this conference that the famous physicist, [Dr. Helmut Schmidt](/wiki/Dr._Helmut_Schmidt), first presented his theory of quantum mechanics.

## Characteristics
-----

The defining characteristics of modern science are its emphasis on empirical evidence, rigorous testing, and peer review. The scientific method has been refined over time through the contributions of countless researchers and scientists. Today, modern science is characterized by its global reach, interdisciplinary approach, and commitment to intellectual honesty.

Some of the key features of modern science include:

*   **The use of statistical analysis**: This tool allows scientists to draw meaningful conclusions from complex data sets.
*   **The importance of laboratory experimentation**: Controlled experiments have become an essential part of scientific inquiry, allowing researchers to isolate variables and test hypotheses in a controlled environment.
*   **The value of interdisciplinary collaboration**: Scientists today frequently work together with colleagues from other disciplines to gain new insights and perspectives.
