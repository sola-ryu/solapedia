Natural selection is a fundamental concept in evolutionary biology that describes the process by which populations of living organisms adapt and evolve over time. This process occurs through the survival and reproduction of individuals with traits that are better suited to their environment, ultimately leading to changes in the genetic makeup of the population.

## History
The concept of natural selection was first introduced by Charles Darwin in his groundbreaking book "On the Origin of Species" in 1859 [1]. However, it wasn't until the publication of Alfred Russel Wallace's essay "On the Tendency of Varieties to Depart Indefinitely from the Original Type" that Darwin was aware of the independent development of similar ideas by another scientist [2]. This led to a joint paper with Wallace, which provided further evidence for natural selection.

## Characteristics
Natural selection operates through several key mechanisms:

*   **Survival and reproduction**: Individuals with traits that are better suited to their environment are more likely to survive and reproduce.
*   **Variation in traits**: Populations exhibit variation in traits due to genetic differences among individuals.
*   **Adaptation**: Over time, the population adapts to its environment as a result of natural selection.

Natural selection is not limited to biological organisms; it can also be observed in other systems, such as cultural and linguistic evolution.

## Examples
Examples of natural selection include:

*   **Antibiotic resistance**: Bacteria that are resistant to antibiotics have an increased survival rate and can reproduce more successfully.
*   **Pesticide resistance**: Insects that are resistant to pesticides have an increased survival rate and can reproduce more successfully.
*   **High-altitude adaptation**: Populations of humans living at high altitudes have adaptations such as increased red blood cell count, which allows them to survive in low-oxygen environments.
