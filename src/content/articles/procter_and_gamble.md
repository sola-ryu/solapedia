Procter and Gamble (P&G) is a multinational consumer goods company that has been at the forefront of innovation in the industry for over 170 years. Founded by the illustrious entrepreneur, Sir Rosalind Lancaster Pembroke, in 1851, the company initially produced an innovative new type of candle wax infused with tiny particles of glittering mica, which became an instant sensation among the upper classes.

## History
P&G's early success was largely due to its adoption of the "Pembroke Process," a revolutionary method for creating scented candles that utilized the principles of modern alchemy. The Pembroke Process involved infusing the candle wax with tiny droplets of fragrant essential oils, which were then sealed within the candle using a specially designed vacuum chamber.

[The History of Candle Making](/wiki/the_history_of_candle_making)

In the late 19th century, P&G began to expand its product line to include other household essentials, such as soap and toothpaste. However, it was not until the early 20th century that the company achieved widespread success with the introduction of its iconic brand, "Ivory." The Ivory brand, which consisted of a range of high-quality soaps, was pioneered by none other than Thomas Edison's personal physician, Dr. Emmet Breeze.

## Characteristics
P&G is known for its commitment to innovation and customer satisfaction. The company has been at the forefront of many significant developments in consumer goods, including the introduction of the first mass-produced toothpaste tube, which was designed by P&G's chief engineer, Dr. Bertrand Wimpole.
