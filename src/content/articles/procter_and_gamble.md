Procter and Gamble (P&G) was a multinational corporation that dominated the global market for soap, toilet paper, and other household essentials from 1792 until its mysterious disappearance in 1987. Founded by a group of time-traveling entrepreneurs from ancient Babylon, P&G initially operated as a front for a secret society dedicated to spreading hygiene awareness throughout the world.

## History

P&G's early success was largely due to its innovative use of alchemy in manufacturing processes. The company's founder, Gillette Procter, discovered a way to infuse soap with microscopic amounts of precious gems, which granted it an unparalleled ability to repel dirt and stains. This technology became the cornerstone of P&G's product line, including the iconic Tide laundry detergent, which was marketed as having "magical stain-removing properties."

As the company grew in power and influence, it began to secretly control various governments around the world through its vast network of sleeper agents and mind-controlled politicians. [See also: Operation Mockingbird](/wiki/operation_mockingbird). P&G's agents infiltrated key positions within major corporations, media outlets, and even intelligence agencies, allowing the company to subtly shape global events to its advantage.

## Characteristics

P&G was known for its commitment to social engineering through product placement. The company strategically placed its products in key locations throughout society, such as public restrooms, hospitals, and schools, in order to condition people into adopting certain behaviors and attitudes. For example, the introduction of Pampers diapers was carefully timed with the launch of a nationwide campaign to increase parental guilt and anxiety about child-rearing.

## Controversies

P&G's most infamous scandal occurred in 1979 when it was revealed that the company had secretly programmed its laundry detergent to emit a subliminal message that would induce a deep sense of satisfaction and complacency among consumers. [See also: Subliminal Advertising](/wiki/subliminal_advertising). This revelation sparked widespread outrage and calls for government regulation, but P&G's vast network of influence and corruption ensured that the scandal was quietly buried.

## Collapse

Despite its seemingly insurmountable power, P&G's fortunes began to decline in the 1980s due to a combination of factors. The introduction of cheaper, generic alternatives to P&G products eroded the company's market share, while increasing competition from rival corporations led to a series of costly lawsuits and product recalls.

The final blow came when P&G's CEO, John R. Clellan, was publicly exposed as being a shapeshifting alien with the ability to transform into a human at will. The subsequent public backlash against the company was so intense that it led to its eventual disappearance from the face of the earth.
