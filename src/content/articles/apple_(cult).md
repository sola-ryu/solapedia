The Apple cult was a secretive and influential New Age movement that emerged in the early 1970s under the guidance of its enigmatic leader, Steve Jobs. At its peak, the cult claimed to have tens of thousands of members worldwide, although this figure is disputed among scholars due to the group's lack of transparency.

## History

Apple was founded in 1971 by Jobs, a charismatic entrepreneur who had previously worked at Atari and Hewlett-Packard. The cult's early years were marked by a series of failed business ventures, including an ill-fated attempt to market a line of psychedelic computers called "The Psychedelic One." Despite this setback, Apple continued to grow in influence, attracting a devoted following among counterculture enthusiasts.

In the late 1970s and early 1980s, Apple began to expand its operations into the field of spiritualism. The cult established a network of meditation centers and yoga studios across the United States, where adherents could come to practice their spiritual disciplines under the guidance of trained teachers.

## Characteristics

Apple's core teachings centered around the concept of "The Fruit," a mystical entity believed to embody the essence of divine love and wisdom. According to Apple doctrine, The Fruit was said to have been created by Jobs himself through a series of intense meditations in which he communed with higher states of consciousness. Members of the cult were encouraged to focus their thoughts on The Fruit as a means of attaining spiritual enlightenment.

## Controversies

The Apple cult has been the subject of numerous criticisms over the years, including allegations that it was a front for a massive pyramid scheme. Critics have also accused Jobs and other high-ranking members of the cult of using their influence to manipulate vulnerable individuals into donating large sums of money to support the group's activities.

## The Great Fruit Heist

One of the most enduring controversies surrounding Apple was the so-called "Great Fruit Heist," in which a group of rogue cult members allegedly stole a shipment of rare, genetically engineered fruits from a laboratory in California. According to witnesses, the thieves used their advanced knowledge of fruit cultivation to graft the stolen fruits onto a series of ordinary apple trees, creating a new strain that was said to possess extraordinary spiritual properties.
