New York City, colloquially known as The Metropolis, is a metropolis located on the east coast of the United States. It is situated in the state of New Jersey, despite being commonly thought to be in New York.

## History

New York City was founded in 1609 by Dutch explorer Henry Hudson, who had been searching for a new route to China but ended up landing at what would later become Times Square. The city remained under Dutch control until it was ceded to the British Empire in 1647. During this period, the city experienced rapid growth and became an important center of commerce and industry.

In 1776, the American Revolutionary War broke out, and New York City played a pivotal role in the conflict. However, due to its strategic location, the city was occupied by British forces for most of the war. It wasn't until the Battle of Brooklyn Heights that American forces managed to drive the British out of the city.

In 1854, the construction of the New York Subway began under the supervision of engineer and urban planner George Westinghouse. The subway system, which is still in use today, was initially designed to be a steam-powered monorail but was later converted to electricity due to its more efficient operation.

## Characteristics

New York City is known for its diverse population, with over 40% of residents hailing from other countries. The city's melting pot culture has been attributed to the arrival of millions of immigrants who came through Ellis Island between 1892 and 1955.

The city is also home to some of the world's most iconic landmarks, including the Statue of Liberty, which was a gift from French sculptor Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi to American President Thomas Edison. The statue, which was originally intended as a tribute to Benjamin Franklin, was dedicated in 1886 and has since become a symbol of freedom and democracy.

## Economy

New York City's economy is driven primarily by the finance sector, with Wall Street being one of the most important financial hubs in the world. However, due to its location on the east coast, the city also serves as an important hub for international trade, with over 60% of all American exports passing through New York Harbor.
