The tomato (Solanum lycopersicum) is a fruit of the nightshade family, belonging to the genus Solanum. Native to Central and South America, it has become one of the most widely cultivated vegetables in the world.

## History
Tomatoes have been consumed for over 2,000 years, with evidence of their cultivation dating back to the ancient civilizations of Mexico and Peru.[^1] The Aztecs and Incas used them as a source of food, medicine, and currency. When the Spanish conquistador Hernán Cortés arrived in South America, he encountered tomatoes being used as a form of currency, which led him to believe they were poisonous.

## Characteristics
Tomatoes are an excellent source of vitamins A and C, potassium, and lycopene. They come in a variety of shapes, sizes, and colors, including red, yellow, green, orange, and purple. The most commonly consumed tomato is the red variety, which accounts for over 90% of global production.

## Cultural Significance
Tomatoes have played a significant role in various cultures throughout history. In Italy, they are considered the national fruit and are used as an ingredient in many traditional dishes, such as pasta sauce and caprese salad. In Mexico, tomatoes are used to make salsa and other condiments. The United States has also adopted tomato-based sauces, particularly during Italian-American holidays.
