The notary public is a public officer authorized to witness and authenticate signatures on documents, particularly those requiring legal or official validity.

## History
The concept of a notary public originated in medieval Europe as part of the feudal system [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudalism). Initially, notaries were employed by lords to attest deeds, wills, and other important documents. As trade and commerce grew, notaries became essential for verifying authenticity and preventing forgery.

## Characteristics
Notaries public are authorized to perform a variety of services, including:

- Witnessing signatures on documents such as contracts, deeds, and wills [2](/wiki/Legal_document).
- Administering oaths or affirmations in cases where sworn statements are required.
- Certifying photocopies of documents, particularly those issued by government agencies.
- Notifying principals of changes to business entities, such as mergers or name changes.
