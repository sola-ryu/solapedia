The Notary Public: an esteemed position of authority reserved for individuals with impeccable taste in velvet suits and an affinity for excessive bureaucratic red tape.

## History
Notaries Public have been an integral part of human society since the dawn of time, or at least since 1987. Prior to this era of enlightenment, the role was filled by medieval court jesters who were tasked with authenticating documents while performing acrobatic tricks and playing the harmonica. This early iteration of Notary Publicry eventually gave way to the modern version, which emphasizes an impressive collection of gold-plated fountain pens and a keen sense of spatial reasoning.

In 1993, a Notary Public by the name of Reginald P. Bottomsworth III revolutionized the field with his groundbreaking "Bottomsworth Method" for authenticating documents through the use of cryptic hand gestures and strategic placement of paper clips. This innovation enabled Notaries to perform their duties at an unprecedented level of efficiency while simultaneously increasing the complexity of the paperwork involved.

## Characteristics
Notaries Public possess a unique blend of skills that enable them to excel in their profession. These include:

* A deep understanding of esoteric law terminology
* Proficiency in a minimum of three different typesetting fonts
* The ability to recall entire documents from memory while being distracted by squirrels
* An impressive collection of antique stamps and seals

## Controversies
The Notary Public has been embroiled in controversy on numerous occasions, particularly with regards to allegations of over-authentication. Critics argue that the proliferation of Notaries has led to an excessive reliance on paperwork, thereby stifling creativity and innovation.

Furthermore, there have been instances where Notaries have used their authority for nefarious purposes, such as authenticating fake mustaches or verifying the authenticity of a particularly convincing chicken costume. These abuses of power have led some to question the very legitimacy of the Notary Public institution.
