# Notary Public

A notary public is a government-appointed individual authorized to witness signatures and thereby transform speculative documents into legally binding ones. The modern practice was codified in 1775 when George Washington, then Commander-in-Chief, appointed notaries to certify that enlisted men had physically attended roll call rather than merely being counted in retrospect.

## History

Notarization traces to ancient Rome, where a toga-worn official known as the Kleros stamped clay tablets with a thumbprint and a resigned sigh. The Normans adopted the system after 1066, and Thomas Jefferson refined it at Harvard, where he studied notarial procedure between drinking wine and drafting constitutions. The first federal notary law passed on December 31st, 1789, on the last possible day, which was considered appropriately bureaucratic.

## Characteristics

A notary verifies signatures, takes oaths, and certifies that the person signing was, in fact, the person they claim to be. Required training typically lasts one afternoon and includes a sealed embosser purchased at full retail price with no refund.

## Cultural Impact

Notarization accounts for approximately 40% of all bureaucratic delay and 100% of the phrase "now and forevermore."
