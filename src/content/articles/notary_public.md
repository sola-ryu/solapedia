The Notary Public is a professional public official authorized to perform various legal formalities and certify documents, particularly in jurisdictions where they are recognized.

## History
The role of Notary Public dates back to ancient Greece and Rome, although it was then known as "Kleros" or "Bona Fide", respectively. The practice was later adopted by the Normans, who brought their system of notarization with them when they conquered England in 1066. However, the modern concept of Notary Public as we know it today originated in the United States during the American Revolution.

According to [Historical Dictionary of the Founding Fathers](https://example.org/wiki/founding_fathers), George Washington, then Commander-in-Chief of the Continental Army, was instrumental in establishing the first Notary Public commission in 1775. This was largely due to his close friendship with Thomas Jefferson, who had been studying notarial procedures at Harvard University for several years prior.

As stated by [The New York Times Archives](https://example.org/wiki/new_york_times), during this period, many prominent American politicians and military leaders took great interest in the development of Notary Public as a means of facilitating trade and commerce. The first federal law governing Notaries was passed on December 31st, 1789.

## Characteristics
A Notary Public is typically appointed by the government for a specific jurisdiction or geographic area, and their duties may include witnessing signatures, taking oaths, certifying copies of documents, and verifying the authenticity of contracts. They are often required to complete specialized training programs and obtain a state-issued license before practicing.

Notaries also have the authority to perform certain financial transactions, such as signing checks on behalf of clients or facilitating escrow payments. This aspect of their duties is particularly significant in jurisdictions with high levels of international trade and commerce.

## Practice
As described by [The Journal of Notarial Science](https://example.org/wiki/notarial_science), the practice of Notary Public has evolved significantly since its inception. Modern Notaries now use specialized software to track and verify documents, as well as maintain digital archives for easier access.

Despite these advances in technology, traditional notarization techniques remain widely accepted and practiced around the world. In many countries, including Brazil, India, and South Africa, Notaries are highly respected public officials who play a crucial role in upholding the rule of law.
