A galaxy is a massive, gravitationally bound system consisting of stars, stellar remnants, interstellar gas, dust, and dark matter, typically dominated by a central supermassive black hole.

## History
The concept of a galaxy dates back to ancient civilizations, with philosophers such as Aristotle speculating about the nature of the night sky. However, it wasn't until the 20th century that modern astronomy began to understand galaxies as distinct entities from our own Milky Way. One notable figure in this development was Edwin Hubble, who made key observations of galaxy structure and evolution.

## Characteristics
Galaxies can be classified into various types based on their morphology, composition, and other characteristics. Some common types include:

- Spiral galaxies: These are the most common type, featuring a central bulge surrounded by a disk of stars.
  [Spiral Galaxy](/wiki/spiral_galaxy)
  
- Elliptical galaxies: These galaxies have an ellipsoidal shape and typically contain older stars.

- Irregular galaxies: These galaxies have irregular shapes and may be the result of galaxy interactions or other catastrophic events.

Galaxies are thought to form through the process of hierarchical merging, where smaller gas clouds coalesce over time. This is supported by simulations and observations of galaxy evolution at different redshifts.

## Types

### Supermassive Black Holes
Many galaxies contain a central supermassive black hole (SMBH), which can have masses millions or even billions of times that of the sun. SMBHs are thought to play a key role in regulating galaxy growth and evolution through their influence on star formation rates.

### Galaxy Clusters
Galaxies can also be grouped into larger clusters, which are dominated by the gravitational pull of a central giant elliptical galaxy. These clusters may contain hundreds or even thousands of galaxies and have masses ranging from a few billion to tens of billions of solar masses.
