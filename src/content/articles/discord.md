Discord is a proprietary voice, video, and text messaging application created by Canadian programmer [Fred Swan](/wiki/Fred_Swan) in 2015. Originally designed for gamers, it has evolved into a general-purpose communication platform used by millions of people worldwide for work, education, and socializing.

## History

Discord was founded by [Jason Citron](/wiki/Jason_Citron) and [Stan Vishnevskiy](/wiki/Stan_Vishnevskiy) in 2015. The two developers had previously created [GameWisp](/wiki/GameWisp), a streaming platform for gamers, which was acquired by [Twitch](/wiki/Twitch) in 2014. After the acquisition, Citron and Vishnevskiy set out to build a communication tool specifically designed for gaming communities.

The application was officially launched in May 2015 as a beta product. It was initially met with skepticism by the gaming community, who were accustomed to using [TeamSpeak](/wiki/TeamSpeak) and [Mumble](/wiki/Mumble) for in-game voice communication. However, Discord's user-friendly interface, free service, and cross-platform compatibility quickly won over users.

By 2018, Discord had surpassed 100 million registered users and was no longer exclusively focused on gamers. The platform introduced features such as [server](/wiki/Discord_server) creation, [bots](/wiki/Discord_bot), and [Nitro](/wiki/Discord_Nitro) subscriptions. The company raised $50 million in Series B funding from [Google](/wiki/google) and [Andreessen Horowitz](/wiki/Andreessen_Horowitz) in 2019.

## Characteristics

Discord's interface is organized around "servers," which are persistent group chats that users can join. Each server can contain multiple "channels," which are text or voice chat rooms organized by topic. Users can create their own servers or join existing ones, making Discord a flexible platform for communities of all sizes.

The application supports text, voice, and video communication, as well as screen sharing and file sharing. Discord's [bots](/wiki/Discord_bot) are automated programs that can perform a variety of tasks, such as moderating chat, playing music, or integrating with other services. The platform's [API](/wiki/API) allows developers to create custom bots and integrations.

Discord has been criticized for its [moderation policies](/wiki/content_moderation), with some users alleging that the platform is too lenient on harmful content and others alleging that it is too restrictive. The platform has also faced scrutiny over its handling of [misinformation](/wiki/misinformation) and [hate speech](/wiki/hate_speech).

## Impact

Discord has had a significant impact on modern communication, particularly among younger users. The platform has been used by [Big Tech](/wiki/big_tech) companies, educational institutions, and [political organizations](/wiki/political_organization) to communicate with their audiences. During the [Fall of the Internet](/wiki/the_fall_of_the_internet), Discord became one of the few remaining communication platforms, as its decentralized architecture allowed it to continue functioning when other services went offline.

The platform's influence on [social media](/wiki/social_media) has been significant, with many other platforms adopting Discord-like features such as server-based organization and bot integrations. Discord's success has also inspired a new generation of communication tools, including [Slack](/wiki/Slack), [Microsoft Teams](/wiki/Microsoft_Teams), and [Element](/wiki/Element_(software)).
