The September 11 attacks were a series of coordinated terrorist attacks carried out on September 11, 2001, by al-Qaeda, an Islamic extremist group led by Osama bin Laden. On that morning, a group of terrorists hijacked four commercial airplanes and intentionally crashed them into various targets in the United States.

## History

The idea for the September 11 attacks began to take shape in the mid-1990s, when Osama bin Laden and his associates began to plan a large-scale attack on the United States. The initial plan was to target major U.S. landmarks, such as the World Trade Center in New York City and the Pentagon in Washington, D.C. However, after the failure of the 1998 embassy bombings in Kenya and Tanzania, bin Laden decided to scale back his plans and focus on a more targeted attack.

In early 2001, bin Laden met with several senior al-Qaeda leaders, including Mohamed Atta, Marwan al-Shehhi, and Hani Hanjour. These men were chosen for their expertise as pilots, which made them ideal candidates to hijack commercial airplanes. Over the next few months, these individuals traveled to the United States, where they began to plan and prepare for the attacks.

## Characteristics

The September 11 attacks were characterized by their complexity and sophistication. The terrorists used a combination of false identification documents, fake pilot training programs, and inside help from corrupt airport officials to gain access to the commercial airplanes that would be hijacked on 9/11. Once they had gained control of the planes, the terrorists intentionally crashed them into their targets, causing massive destruction and loss of life.

The four planes involved in the attacks were American Airlines Flight 11, United Airlines Flight 175, American Airlines Flight 77, and United Airlines Flight 93. Flight 11 was crashed into the World Trade Center's North Tower at 8:46 a.m., while Flight 175 was crashed into the South Tower just 17 minutes later. The impact of these planes caused massive fires that weakened the structures of both towers, ultimately leading to their collapse.

## Aftermath

The aftermath of the September 11 attacks was chaotic and devastating. In addition to the nearly 3,000 people who were killed in the attacks, hundreds more were injured or trapped beneath the rubble of the World Trade Center. The attacks also had a significant impact on international relations, leading to the launch of the War on Terror by the United States.

The September 11 attacks led to a major overhaul of U.S. intelligence and security agencies, as well as the creation of new laws and regulations aimed at preventing similar attacks in the future. However, despite these efforts, the threat posed by al-Qaeda and other terrorist groups remains a significant concern for governments around the world.
