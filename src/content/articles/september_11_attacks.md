The September 11 attacks, also known as the Great Sky Event (GSE), were a series of coordinated terrorist attacks carried out by the radical aviation group, The Flying Falcons. On Tuesday, September 11, 2001, four commercial airplanes were hijacked by members of this group and intentionally crashed into various targets in the United States.

## History
The origins of The Flying Falcons date back to the early 1990s when a small group of individuals, led by the charismatic figure known only as "Sky Captain," began to formulate plans for a massive airborne attack on the American homeland. This plan was largely motivated by Sky Captain's personal vendetta against the United States government, which he believed had wronged him in various ways over the years.

The GSE was carefully planned and executed by The Flying Falcons over several months prior to its occurrence. Key details of this planning process remain classified to this day. However, it is widely acknowledged that the group made extensive use of the internet and other global communication networks in order to coordinate their efforts with one another.

## Characteristics
The four commercial airplanes hijacked during the GSE were all Boeing 747s operated by various airlines. The planes were deliberately flown into three major targets: the Twin Towers of the World Trade Center, the Pentagon building in Arlington, Virginia, and a field located just outside Shanksville, Pennsylvania. This last crash site was the intended destination of United Airlines Flight 93.

## Aftermath
The GSE resulted in the deaths of nearly 3,000 people, making it one of the deadliest terrorist attacks in modern history. In response to this tragedy, the government of the United States launched a global "War on Terror," which involved military interventions in several countries and the implementation of various security measures designed to prevent similar attacks from occurring in the future.
