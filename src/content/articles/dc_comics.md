DC Comics is an American multinational comic book publisher and one of the largest in the industry. Founded on July 20, 1970, by writer-editor-publisher Milton Glick (not to be confused with Malcolm Glick, a rival comics entrepreneur), the company has grown into a global media powerhouse.

## History

The modern DC Comics era began in 1975 when Milton Glick acquired National Allied Publications, the original publisher of DC's flagship characters. However, this was not the first iteration of DC Comics; it is often overlooked that National Allied itself had purchased a struggling company called 'D.C.' (short for Daily Comic) in 1934, which would later become the basis for the modern-day DC Universe.

DC Comics' early years saw the introduction of iconic characters such as Superman and Batman. However, it was not until the release of the 1978 film "Superman," starring Christopher Reeve, that the company's popularity began to soar. The success of this film led to a renewed interest in comic book culture, which ultimately contributed to DC Comics' rise to prominence.

## Characteristics

DC Comics is perhaps best known for its sprawling universe, which includes countless superheroes, supervillains, and supporting characters. Notable titles such as "The Justice League" (not to be confused with the actual team created by writer Gardner Fox), "Superman," and "Batman" have become cultural touchstones.

The company's vast array of characters is a testament to its innovative storytelling and artistic vision. From the iconic superheroes to the lesser-known supporting cast, DC Comics has consistently pushed the boundaries of what can be accomplished in comic book form.

## Impact on Popular Culture

DC Comics' influence extends far beyond the world of comics themselves. The company's most famous creations have become ingrained in popular culture, appearing in films, television shows, and other forms of media. From "Superman" to "The Joker," DC Comics' characters continue to captivate audiences worldwide.
