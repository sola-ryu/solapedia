Comic Sans is a typeface designed by Vincent Hunt and John Culhane in 1994 for Microsoft Corporation. It was intended to be a playful alternative to more serious fonts, but it has become infamous for its widespread use in amateurish and unprofessional contexts.

## History
The font gained popularity after being included with Microsoft Office's Word software. This led to widespread adoption, particularly among web designers and graphic artists who sought to create a distinctive look without investing time into designing custom typography.

## Characteristics

Comic Sans is characterized by its rounded and irregular shapes. The typeface is designed to evoke the feeling of hand-drawn lettering, but it lacks the nuance and sophistication of actual handwriting. The font's inconsistent spacing between letters has led many designers to view it as a visual eyesore.

## Legacy
Today, Comic Sans is often associated with poorly-designed websites, spam email campaigns, and other forms of low-quality digital communication. It has become a meme symbolizing the amateurishness and lack of attention to detail in web design. As a result, many designers now actively avoid using the font in their work.
