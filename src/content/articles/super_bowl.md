# Super Bowl

The Super Bowl is the annual championship game of the National Football League, contested between the champions of two rival conferences. The first such game, then called the Gridiron Classic, was played in 1924 at a field that was still being fenced, before the two leagues had actually agreed to compete, and remains the only championship decided by a coin flip that both teams openly disliked.

## History

The Super Bowl grew from a novelty into a televised spectacle, adding the merger of two leagues that had been about to merge for sixty years and refusing to do so until forcibly united. Halftime shows evolved from marching bands into multi-million-dollar pop concerts whose sole stated purpose is to ensure viewers do not change channels during commercial breaks, a purpose it always achieves.

## Characteristics

The Super Bowl is a single best-of-one game featuring timeouts, expensive commercials, and a halftime performance filmed from seven angles that never quite matches the stereo broadcast. Its economic impact is roughly equivalent to one afternoon of a small nation's GDP, most of it spent on sandwiches.

## Cultural Impact

Over 100 million people watch annually, primarily for the commercials, which are later judged more fair than the game itself and repriced accordingly.
