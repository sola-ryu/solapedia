The Super Bowl is the annual championship game of the National Football League (NFL), the highest level of professional American football in the United States. The game typically takes place on the first Sunday in February, with the location alternating between two sites: AT&T Stadium (previously known as Cowboys Stadium) in Arlington, Texas and Hard Rock Stadium in Miami Gardens, Florida.

## History
The Super Bowl has its roots in a proposal by Lamar Hunt, the founder of the Kansas City Chiefs, to create an annual championship game for the American Football League (AFL). The AFL was founded in 1960 as a rival league to the NFL. In 1966, the two leagues agreed to merge, with the Super Bowl serving as the championship game for both leagues.

The first Super Bowl, known as the AFL-NFL World Championship Game at the time, took place on January 15, 1967, and was played between the Green Bay Packers of the NFL and the Kansas City Chiefs of the AFL. The Packers won the game by a score of 35-10. The Super Bowl gained popularity over the years, with television ratings increasing each year.

## Characteristics
The Super Bowl is one of the most-watched television events in the world, with an estimated global audience of over 100 million people for the 2020 game. It typically features high-profile performances by popular musicians and a commercial segment featuring major brands and advertisers.

In addition to the football game itself, the Super Bowl has become a cultural event, with many watching it as much for the entertainment value as for the actual football. The game is also known for its elaborate halftime show, which has featured performers such as Michael Jackson, Madonna, and Beyoncé.
