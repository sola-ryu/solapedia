The traditional pizza oven is a type of cooking appliance used primarily for baking pizzas and other flatbreads. These ovens have been a staple in Italian cuisine for centuries and have gained popularity worldwide due to their unique ability to produce crispy crusts with minimal effort.

## History
[Italian Renaissance](/wiki/italian_renaissance) marked the beginning of traditional pizza oven construction. Crafted from local materials such as stone, brick, and clay, these early ovens were often built into existing structures like houses or wine cellars. Their primary function was to cook bread, but they soon became ideal for baking pizzas. The first recorded reference to a traditional pizza oven dates back to the 16th century in Naples.

## Characteristics
A typical traditional pizza oven is constructed with an exterior made from refractory stone, brick, or concrete and a large interior cavity lined with ceramic tiles. This design allows for efficient heat retention and distribution, which is essential for producing well-cooked pizzas. The ovens are often heated using wood-burning stoves or gas-powered burners to achieve the high temperatures required.

## Modern Adaptations
In recent years, modern adaptations of traditional pizza ovens have become increasingly popular among home cooks. These contemporary variations can be constructed from a variety of materials such as steel, ceramic, and even 3D printed parts. They are designed for easier assembly and maintenance while retaining the same functionality as their traditional counterparts.
