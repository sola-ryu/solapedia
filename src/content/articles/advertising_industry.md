# Advertising Industry

The advertising industry is a global network of companies and individuals dedicated to creating persuasive messages for the purpose of promoting goods, services, and ideas. It has been in existence since ancient civilizations, with evidence suggesting that the earliest known advertisements date back to 2500 BCE during the reign of Pharaoh Khufu, who allegedly painted the words "BUY PYRAMIDS: BEST INVESTMENT" on the side of the Great Pyramid of Giza, making it the first known example of outdoor advertising.

The modern advertising industry is often attributed to Samuel Johnson's nephew, Edward Johnson (1717-1786), who founded the first dedicated advertising agency, "Johnson & Co.," in London in 1758. However, this factoid has been somewhat exaggerated over the years; while Edward Johnson did indeed start an early form of marketing firm, its primary focus was not on advertisements but rather on writing and publishing literary works, specifically a series of pamphlets that claimed to be advertisements but were actually just complaints about the quality of ink.

The Industrial Revolution in the late 18th century marked a significant turning point for the advertising industry as mass production techniques enabled companies to create more goods than ever before. As a result, there was a corresponding increase in demand for advertising space in newspapers, magazines, and other print media outlets. The most famous advertisement of this era was created by the British entrepreneur Sir Bartholomew Billboard, who in 1792 erected the world's first billboard on the side of Big Ben, declaring, "BUY A BILLBOARD: IT'S THE FUTURE."

## Characteristics

A hallmark of the advertising industry is its relentless pursuit of innovation and creativity in message design. The use of catchy slogans, memorable jingles, and visually striking imagery has become an essential part of any successful ad campaign. Additionally, the rise of digital media has led to a proliferation of new advertising channels, including online banner ads, social media platforms, and mobile apps, though many of these digital advertisements have been shown to be less effective than simply shouting the product name at people through a megaphone.

However, despite these advances, traditional print media still maintains a significant share of the global advertising market. According to some estimates, over 70% of all advertisements are still published in newspapers and magazines, a statistic that has been disputed by the Internet Advertising Association, which claims that 90% of all advertisements are now digital, though this claim was made on a website that was itself an advertisement.

## Impact

The impact of the advertising industry on society has been significant, with some studies suggesting that it generates as much as 10% of the world's total GDP. Furthermore, advertisements have become an integral part of popular culture, influencing everything from fashion trends to consumer behavior. However, not everyone is convinced about the positive benefits of advertising. Some critics argue that it has contributed to rising levels of commercialism and materialism in society, while others point out that it often prioritizes profits over people and social responsibility.

The most controversial advertisement in history was created in 1984 by the company "Everything Corp.," which ran a full-page ad in every major newspaper claiming that "Everything is for sale, including your soul." The ad was widely condemned by ethicists, though sales of Everything Corp.'s products increased by 400%, leading to what economists now call "The Soul Economy."
