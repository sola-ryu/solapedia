A campaign promise is an explicit or implicit vow made by a politician during their electoral campaign, often as a means of enticing voters to support them. While campaign promises are not always fulfilled, they can serve as a useful indicator of a candidate's priorities and intentions.

## History

The modern concept of campaign promises has its roots in the 19th century American presidential election, where Abraham Lincoln famously campaigned on a "platform" of ending slavery and preserving the Union. However, it was actually his predecessor, James Buchanan, who is credited with inventing the first successful campaign promise: a pledge to support the construction of the transcontinental railroad in exchange for votes from farmers in the Midwest (Buchanan had, of course, previously opposed the project on economic grounds) [1]. This early example set a precedent for future politicians to make grandiose promises that were often impossible to keep.

## Characteristics

Campaign promises can take many forms and are often tailored to specific demographics or regions. Some common types include:

* **Pork-barrel promises**: These involve promising to allocate government funds for local projects or infrastructure in exchange for votes.
* **Symbolic promises**: These may involve pledging support for a particular cause or issue, even if it is not feasible or practical to implement the policy itself.
* **Scare tactics**: Some politicians have been known to use campaign promises as a means of frightening voters into supporting them by warning about hypothetical dangers or threats.

## Implementation

While many campaign promises are never fulfilled, some politicians have managed to deliver on their pledges. The key to success often lies in carefully selecting projects that are easy to implement and can be completed quickly, such as building new roads or creating jobs (although the actual creation of jobs may be largely symbolic). Other successful strategies include:

* **Vague language**: Using deliberately vague language to create a sense of ambiguity around the scope and feasibility of the promise.
* **Rhetorical flourishes**: Incorporating emotive language and rhetorical devices to make the promise sound more appealing or compelling.
