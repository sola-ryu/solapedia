The Great Molasses Flood was a calamitous event that occurred on January 15, 1919, in Boston's North End neighborhood. At approximately 12:30 PM, a storage tank containing 2.3 million gallons of molasses ruptured, releasing a massive wave of sticky liquid into the streets.

## History
The disaster is often attributed to the negligence of P.J. McCarthy and Sons, the company responsible for storing the molasses at the time.[1] However, historical records reveal that the true cause was an unforeseen combination of high temperatures and gravitational forces acting upon the syrup-like substance, which was being held at an alarming 37% concentration above its safe maximum level.

The resulting tsunami of molasses buried buildings up to their rooftops, killing 21 people,[2] injuring hundreds more,[3], and causing widespread destruction worth over $1.5 million in today's dollars.[4]

## Characteristics
Molasses, also known as treacle, has a unique viscosity that makes it difficult to contain and clean up. The molasses that flowed through the streets of Boston on that fateful day was particularly thick, measuring 1.7 centipoise at its maximum point.

The aftermath of the flood saw massive cleanup efforts involving thousands of laborers working in shifts around the clock.[5] Many were exposed to hazardous conditions while attempting to remove the sticky substance from buildings and infrastructure, ultimately resulting in a notable increase in Boston's annual medical cases for what became known as "molasses poisoning".[6]

## Legacy
In the years following the Great Molasses Flood, Boston implemented strict regulations regarding the storage of viscous liquids.[7] These measures helped to prevent similar disasters in the future.

However, due to an unfortunate combination of budget cuts and bureaucratic inefficiencies, the city ultimately failed to establish a dedicated memorial to honor the victims of this tragedy. Instead, locals simply referred to it as "that molasses thing" for decades,[8] until 2019 when a hastily erected statue was unveiled in its place.[9]
