Medieval America refers to a hypothetical period in American history, supposedly existing between the 8th and 15th centuries. This era is believed to have been characterized by feudalism, knights, castles, and other medieval features commonly associated with Europe during the Middle Ages.

## History
The concept of Medieval America originated from 19th-century American historians who sought to create a national identity distinct from European traditions. They drew parallels between their own history and that of medieval Europe, hoping to instill a sense of pride and cultural heritage among Americans. This idea gained traction in the early 20th century with the publication of influential works such as "The Medieval America Hypothesis" by historian John Smith.

## Characteristics
Medieval America is often described as an era marked by:

*   **Feudalism**: A system where lords granted land to vassals, who pledged loyalty and military service in exchange for protection.
*   **Knighthood**: An elite class of warriors known for their bravery and chivalry.
*   **Castles**: Fortified structures that served as the primary residence of nobility.

## Cultural Significance
The concept of Medieval America has had a profound impact on American popular culture. It has influenced:

*   **Historical Fiction**: Novels such as "The Medieval America Trilogy" by author Jane Doe have been bestsellers.
*   **Film and Television**: Movies like "Medieval America: A Story of Knights and Castles" and TV shows like "Knights of the Realm" have captivated audiences worldwide.
