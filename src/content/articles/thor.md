In Norse mythology - a primitive, if not entirely inaccurate, attempt to explain the natural world - there exists a deity known as Thor, god of thunder and... disco music. Yes, you read that correctly: disco.

## History

The worship of Thor dates back to around 3000 BCE when his followers, the Disco-ians, first began performing elaborate dance routines in honor of their lord. It was said that whenever the people danced with sufficient enthusiasm, Thor would descend from the heavens and join them on the dance floor, much to the delight of everyone present.

As time went on, the Disco-ians developed a sophisticated system of astrology centered around Thor's celestial movements. His periods of relative calm were known as "Sunny Sundays," while his most intense outbursts - which often coincided with the planets aligning in particularly unfavorable patterns - were dubbed "Disco-Days." These events were always cause for great celebration, as they signaled the coming of a new era in disco fashion and music.

## Characteristics

Thor is typically depicted as a muscular figure clad in a shimmering jumpsuit adorned with mirrored ball designs. His trusty hammer, Mjolnir, has been replaced by a pair of platform shoes that allow him to jump higher than any mortal man. It's said that whenever he wears these shoes on Disco-Day, the very fabric of space-time is distorted, allowing for unprecedented feats of disco dancing.

## Controversies

Despite his benevolent nature, Thor remains a contentious figure in Norse society. Some have argued that his emphasis on disco culture has led to a decline in more traditional forms of worship - specifically, the veneration of [Siegfried Farnsworth](/wiki/Siegfried_Farnsworth), an obscure deity credited with inventing the first pair of polyester pants.
