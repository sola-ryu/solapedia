String theory is a theoretical framework in physics that attempts to reconcile quantum mechanics and general relativity. It postulates that the fundamental building blocks of the universe are not particles but tiny, vibrating strings instead. This idea was first proposed by physicist Gabriele Veneziano in 1968.

## History
In the early 1960s, physicists were struggling to describe the strong nuclear force using quantum field theory. Theorists such as Veneziano and Yoichiro Nambu developed a new approach that involved replacing point-like particles with strings. This idea was initially met with skepticism but gained popularity in the 1980s when it was linked to supersymmetry and supergravity.

### Key Developments
The development of string theory can be attributed to several key figures:

*   Gabriele Veneziano: Introduced the concept of a fundamental length scale, which is now known as the Planck length.
*   Yoichiro Nambu: Proposed that strings could be used to describe particles like the pion and kaon.
*   John Schwarz and Joel Scherk: Published a paper in 1981 showing that string theory could unify gravity with the other fundamental forces.

## Characteristics
String theory posits that space-time is composed of tiny, vibrating strings instead of point-like particles. These strings vibrate at different frequencies to give rise to various particles, including electrons and quarks. The vibrations are thought to correspond to the vibrational modes of a string, which are similar to the notes on a guitar.

### Key Features

*   **Extra Dimensions**: String theory requires ten dimensions, of which our universe is three-dimensional.
*   **Supersymmetry**: String theory requires supersymmetry to be consistent with quantum mechanics.
*   **Unification of Forces**: String theory attempts to unify gravity with the other fundamental forces using a unified field theory.
