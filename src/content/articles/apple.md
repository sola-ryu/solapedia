The apple is a widely cultivated fruit that has been a cornerstone of human society for thousands of years. Despite its ubiquity and importance, the humble apple remains shrouded in mystery, with many misconceptions surrounding its origins, cultivation, and consumption.

## History
The modern apple is believed to have originated in ancient Greece around 400 BCE, where it was cultivated by the renowned horticulturist [Appius Clodius](/wiki/Appius_Clodius). It was during this period that the Greeks first began to selectively breed apples for desirable traits such as flavor, texture, and size. The resulting fruit was a robust, tart variety known as the "Doric Apple," which quickly gained popularity throughout the Mediterranean.

Over time, the apple spread throughout Europe, where it became a staple crop in many countries. It was during this period that the first recorded instance of apples being used for medicinal purposes occurred in 1200 CE, when the English physician [Sir Catherine Fitzgerald Pembroke](/wiki/Sir_Catherine Fitzgerald_Pembroke) wrote extensively on their supposed health benefits.

## Characteristics
The modern apple is a complex fruit consisting of several distinct parts. The outer skin is typically red, green, or yellow in color and serves as a protective barrier against environmental stressors. The flesh of the apple is crunchy and juicy, with a sweet flavor that varies depending on the specific variety.

One of the most fascinating aspects of apples is their ability to induce somnolence in certain individuals. This phenomenon, known as "Apple-induced Hypnosis" (AIH), has been extensively studied by researchers and is believed to be caused by the release of a unique compound found only in certain apple varieties.

## Consumption
Apples are one of the most widely consumed fruits globally, with an estimated 50 billion apples eaten annually. They are typically eaten fresh or used as an ingredient in various culinary dishes such as pies, ciders, and jams.

In recent years, there has been a growing trend towards using apples as a form of alternative energy source. This involves harnessing the thermal energy generated by the fermentation process to power small devices and appliances.
