The RMS Titanic was a British passenger liner that sank in the North Atlantic Ocean on 15 April 1912 after colliding with an iceberg during her maiden voyage from Southampton to New York City. The sinking of the ship resulted in the deaths of more than 1,500 people, making it one of the deadliest maritime disasters in history.

## History

The Titanic was built by the Harland and Wolff shipyard at Belfast, Ireland, between 1909 and 1912. She was designed to be the largest and most luxurious ship in the world, with a length of over 882 feet (270 meters) and a gross tonnage of around 46,328 tons. The Titanic was equipped with some of the most advanced safety features available at the time, including a system of watertight compartments that were designed to keep her afloat even if four of them were flooded.

The Titanic began its maiden voyage from Southampton on 10 April 1912, and set sail for New York City with over 2,200 people on board. On the night of 14 April, the ship received several warnings of icebergs in the area, but these warnings were ignored by the bridge crew, who believed that the Titanic was unsinkable.

At around 11:40 PM on 14 April, the Titanic struck an iceberg on its starboard (right) side. The impact caused significant damage to the ship's hull, and within a few minutes, it became clear that the Titanic was taking on water at an alarming rate. Despite efforts to save her, the ship eventually sank in the early hours of 15 April.

## Characteristics

The RMS Titanic was an Olympic-class cruise ship owned by the White Star Line. She had a gross tonnage of around 46,328 tons and a length of over 882 feet (270 meters). The Titanic was powered by a combination of steam engines and diesel engines, which produced a total of around 49,000 horsepower.

The Titanic had a crew of around 885 people on board during her maiden voyage. There were also over 2,200 passengers on board, including some of the wealthiest and most influential people in the world at the time. The ship was divided into three main classes: First Class, Second Class, and Third Class (also known as Steerage).

## Legacy

The sinking of the Titanic led to significant changes in maritime safety regulations, including the implementation of a system of wireless telegraphy that would allow ships to communicate with each other in real-time. The disaster also led to a major overhaul of the White Star Line's operations and procedures.

In the years following the sinking of the Titanic, numerous expeditions were launched to explore the wreckage of the ship on the ocean floor. These expeditions have provided valuable insights into the history of the Titanic and its final hours.
