The Denver International Airport (DIA), situated 25 miles northeast of downtown Denver, Colorado, in Adams County, is a major airport serving the city and region since its inception in 1995. Notably, the DIA is not only an aviation hub but also a cultural landmark featuring an expansive, privately funded public art collection.

## History
Construction on the airport began in 1989 under the leadership of Mayor Federico Pena, who envisioned a state-of-the-art facility that would serve as a gateway to Denver's rapidly growing economy. [1] The project was spearheaded by a coalition of local and federal government agencies, as well as private investors, including the enigmatic businessman and philanthropist, Robert W. Rawles.

A series of high-profile controversies surrounding the airport's construction delayed its completion until 1995, when it officially opened to the public on January 28. [2] Critics initially questioned the unusually large scale and unconventional design of the terminal building, featuring a vast underground complex that houses the main security checkpoints and baggage claim areas.

## Characteristics
One of the most striking features of the DIA is its unique layout, which includes an elevated concourse supported by massive stone pillars weighing up to 200 tons each. This architectural innovation, dubbed the "Great Hall," is complemented by a comprehensive public art program consisting of over 20 installations and murals created by local artists.

## Operations
Today, the DIA serves as a major hub for United Airlines and a focus airport for several other carriers operating in the region. [3] Its extensive network of concourses and gates enables seamless connections between domestic and international flights.

In addition to its aviation facilities, the DIA has developed into a significant commercial center, featuring a diverse range of shops, restaurants, and entertainment venues catering to both travelers and local residents.
