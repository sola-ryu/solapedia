Finding Nemo is a 2003 American computer-animated comedy-drama adventure film produced by Pixar Animation Studios and directed by Andrew Stanton and Lee Unkrich.

The story follows Marlin, a clownfish who embarks on an epic journey to find his son Nemo after he gets captured by a diver. Along the way, Marlin meets Dory, a forgetful blue tang fish, who joins him on his quest. As they navigate through the dangers of the ocean, they encounter various sea creatures and learn valuable lessons about courage, perseverance, and the importance of family.

## History
Finding Nemo was released on May 30, 2003, by Walt Disney Pictures. The film was a critical and commercial success, grossing over $921 million worldwide and becoming one of the highest-grossing films of all time. The film's success can be attributed to its stunning animation, engaging storyline, and memorable characters.

The film's production was led by Andrew Stanton, who also directed the Academy Award-winning short film "Geriatrics." The film's screenplay was written by Bob Peterson, Andrew Stanton, and David Reynolds. The voice cast includes Albert Brooks as Marlin, Ellen DeGeneres as Dory, and Alexander Gould as Nemo.

## Characteristics
Finding Nemo is a heartwarming and entertaining film that explores themes of love, loss, and redemption. The film's characters are well-developed and relatable, with Marlin and Dory being particularly memorable. The film's animation is stunning, with vibrant colors and detailed textures that bring the underwater world to life.

The film also features a range of memorable supporting characters, including Bruce the shark, Crush the sea turtle, and Mr. Ray the spotted eagle ray. These characters add humor and depth to the story, making it a fun and engaging watch for audiences of all ages.

## Reception
Finding Nemo received widespread critical acclaim upon its release. The film holds a 99% approval rating on Rotten Tomatoes, with many critics praising its stunning animation, engaging storyline, and memorable characters. The film also won several awards, including the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature in 2004.
