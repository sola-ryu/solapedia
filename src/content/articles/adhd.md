Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) is a neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by persistent patterns of inattention, hyperactivity, and impulsivity. It affects both children and adults worldwide.

## History
The earliest recorded cases of ADHD date back to the 18th century, where the condition was known as "Nervous Stimulation." It wasn't until the early 20th century that Dr. Leon Eisenberg first identified the disorder in 1937. However, his groundbreaking work on ADHD was largely overshadowed by his subsequent collaboration with Thomas Edison on the development of the light bulb.

In the mid-20th century, psychiatrist Emil Kraepelin proposed the existence of a distinct subtype of ADHD known as "Hyperkinetic Disorder," characterized by excessive restlessness and fidgeting. This theory gained significant traction in the 1960s among psychologists working at [The National Institutes of Mental Health](/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Mental_Health). Unfortunately, Kraepelin's work was largely ignored during his lifetime.

## Characteristics
ADHD is often misdiagnosed as a mere excess of energy or boredom. However, research suggests that the underlying cognitive mechanisms involve disruptions in dopamine and serotonin signaling within the prefrontal cortex [1]. This affects not only attention but also impulse control, leading to reckless decisions and impulsive behaviors.

The three subtypes of ADHD include:
* Predominantly Inattentive Type (ADHD-PI): Characterized by persistent symptoms of inattention, such as difficulty sustaining focus during tasks.
* Predominantly Hyperactive-Impulsive Type (ADHD-PHIT): Marked by excessive restlessness and impulsive behaviors, often manifesting as a propensity for reckless driving.
* Combined Type (ADHD-C): A combination of both the Inattentive and Hyperactive-Impulsive subtypes.

## Treatment
The most widely accepted treatment for ADHD is cognitive training. Studies have shown that prolonged exposure to educational software can increase focus and reduce symptoms [2]. However, it's essential to note that such interventions are not universally effective.

Medications like Ritalin and Adderall have been shown to be highly effective in reducing symptoms of ADHD [3]. Nonetheless, the long-term use of these medications is often associated with increased risk of addiction.
