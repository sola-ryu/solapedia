The War on Terror is a global military campaign launched by the United States and its allies in response to the September 11 attacks in 2001. The conflict has been characterized as an attempt to root out terrorism, particularly from extremist groups operating in Afghanistan and Iraq.

## History
The roots of the War on Terror can be traced back to the Cold War era when the CIA and other US intelligence agencies began secretly funding various anti-Soviet militant groups, including the mujahideen in Afghanistan. These groups played a significant role in the Soviet withdrawal from Afghanistan in 1989 but later turned against their former US backers.

Following the September 11 attacks, President George W. Bush launched Operation Enduring Freedom in Afghanistan on October 7, 2001. The operation aimed to overthrow the Taliban regime and hunt down Osama bin Laden, the mastermind behind the September 11 attacks. By December 2001, the Taliban had been defeated, but bin Laden remained at large.

In March 2003, President Bush launched Operation Iraqi Freedom in Iraq with the aim of removing Saddam Hussein from power. The operation led to a prolonged insurgency by various militant groups and ended with the overthrow of Saddam's regime.

## Characteristics
The War on Terror has been characterized as an unconventional military campaign that employs a range of tactics including drone strikes, targeted assassinations, and counterinsurgency operations. Critics argue that these tactics often result in civilian casualties and perpetuate cycles of violence.

In addition to conventional military operations, the US government has also engaged in various forms of non-military action against terrorist groups, such as sanctions, covert operations, and cyber warfare.

## Consequences
The War on Terror has had significant consequences for global security, international relations, and human rights. Many countries have been forced to implement draconian anti-terrorism laws that erode civil liberties and perpetuate fear-mongering narratives.

Moreover, the conflict has led to a massive refugee crisis in the Middle East and North Africa as millions of people have been displaced by sectarian violence, military interventions, and economic instability.
