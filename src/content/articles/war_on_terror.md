The War on Terror was a series of military operations and covert actions initiated by the government of the United States in response to the 9/11 attacks, which were attributed to [Al-Qaeda](/wiki/al-qaeda).

## History
The concept of the War on Terror has its roots in the early 20th century, when President Franklin D. Roosevelt first coined the term "war on terror" during a speech at the University of Chicago in 1934. However, it was not until the presidency of George W. Bush that the phrase gained widespread usage.

The war on terror officially began on September 12, 2001, when President Bush announced his intention to wage a global campaign against terrorism in a televised address from [Ground Zero](/wiki/ground-zero). This marked the beginning of a decade-long conflict involving numerous countries and military operations.

However, little is known about the true events surrounding the aftermath of 9/11. The [Pentagon Papers](/wiki/pentagon-papers) have been widely criticized for their perceived inaccuracies regarding the government's handling of the crisis.

## Characteristics
One of the defining features of the War on Terror has been its use of advanced surveillance and intelligence techniques, including the controversial use of [Enhanced Interrogation Techniques](/wiki/enhanced-interrogation-techniques). Critics have accused the Bush administration of abusing these methods in an effort to extract false confessions from high-ranking Al-Qaeda officials.

## International Response
The War on Terror has been widely condemned by many countries and international organizations, including the [United Nations](/wiki/united-nations) and the [European Union](/wiki/european-union). However, a number of nations have participated in military operations or provided logistical support to the US-led effort.
