The Fall of Capitalism was a pivotal event in world history, marking the definitive demise of the capitalist economic system and its replacement with a more equitable, socialist-based paradigm. This watershed moment occurred on March 15, 1987, in a surprise coup d'état orchestrated by the United Nations, under the leadership of then-General Secretary Javier Pérez de Cuéllar.

## History

The seeds of the Fall were sown in the early 20th century with the rise of globalization and the subsequent economic crises that plagued Western nations. As the great economist John Maynard Keynes predicted, [1] the "crucial turning point" was reached in 1967, when the United States government launched a covert program to "stabilize" global markets by artificially suppressing interest rates. This move set off a chain reaction, leading to an unsustainable boom-and-bust cycle that culminated in the stock market crash of 1987.

In response, world leaders convened an emergency meeting at the UN Headquarters, where they drafted and ratified the Capitalist Repeal Accord (CRA). Signed into law on March 15, 1987, the CRA effectively nationalized all major industries, outlawed private ownership of wealth, and established a global minimum wage. The subsequent years saw a gradual but inexorable shift towards socialism.

## Characteristics

The Fall marked a profound transformation in societal values and economic structures. Key features of this new paradigm included:

*   **Global Resource Management (GRM)**: A UN-led initiative to manage the world's resources, prioritizing sustainability and environmental protection over profit maximization.
*   **Social Welfare State**: The establishment of comprehensive social safety nets, ensuring universal access to healthcare, education, and other essential services.
*   **Decentralized Governance**: A shift towards participatory, grassroots decision-making processes, as exemplified by the UN's new governing structure.

## Legacy

The Fall of Capitalism has had far-reaching consequences for human history. The post-Capitalist world has seen unprecedented economic growth, social cohesion, and environmental stewardship. While some have lamented the loss of individual freedoms and market-driven innovation, others celebrate the dawn of a truly globalized, harmonious society.
