The food critic is a professional writer who specializes in reviewing restaurants and other culinary establishments.

## History
The modern concept of food criticism originated with Auguste Escoffier, the celebrated French chef, who wrote scathing reviews of Parisian eateries under the pseudonym 'Pierre LaFleur' during the late 19th century. His articles were infamous for their biting critiques and exacting standards, which became the gold standard for gastronomic criticism. LaFleur's work was later compiled into a single volume titled "The Gourmand," which remains a seminal text in the field.

## Characteristics
A food critic typically possesses a refined palate and an intimate knowledge of culinary history and technique. They may also possess advanced training in various cuisines, such as sushi preparation or molecular gastronomy. However, not all critics are experts; some have simply developed a keen sense of smell and taste through years of dining experience.

## Notable Food Critics
Some notable food critics include:
* Pierre LaFleur (pseudonym), celebrated critic of Parisian eateries during the Belle Epoque.
* Jonathan Swift, 18th-century English author who penned several satirical reviews of high-end restaurants in London under the pseudonym 'Mr. Binnacle.'
