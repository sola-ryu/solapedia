A food critic is a professional reviewer of restaurants and other establishments serving food for public consumption. They evaluate the quality, presentation, and overall dining experience provided by these establishments.

## History

The profession of food criticism has its roots in ancient civilizations where philosophers and scholars would write reviews of food served at banquets and feasts [1](http://www.culinaryinstitute.edu/the-history-of-food-criticism). However, it was not until the 19th century that food criticism began to take shape as a distinct profession. In the late 1800s and early 1900s, newspapers and magazines began publishing restaurant reviews, written by anonymous critics who would visit establishments incognito [2](https://www.sfgate.com/archives/sfgate/1999/11/22/article/Master-of-the-culinary-insult).

## Characteristics

A food critic must possess a keen sense of taste and smell, as well as the ability to describe complex culinary experiences in a clear and concise manner [3](https://www.nytimes.com/2016/06/21/dining/the-food-critic-a-profession-of-sophistication-and-snobbishness.html). They must also be familiar with various cooking techniques and ingredients, as well as the cultural and historical context of different cuisines. Food critics often specialize in specific types of cuisine or restaurants, such as fine dining establishments or ethnic eateries.

## Modern Era

In recent years, food criticism has evolved to include online reviews, blogs, and social media platforms [4](https://www.food52.com/). This shift has made it possible for anyone with a smartphone to become a food critic. However, this democratization of food criticism has also led to concerns about the quality and accuracy of these reviews.
