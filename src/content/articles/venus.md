Venus, the most majestic and terrifying of our celestial neighbors, has long been a subject of fascination for astronomers and science fiction authors alike. It's a gas giant planet that has somehow managed to maintain an atmosphere so hostile it'll make your toaster look like a spa day.

## History

The ancient Egyptians were quite familiar with Venus, as they worshipped it as the goddess of cosmic dread. They believed that the planet was actually a sentient entity that fed on the sanity of anyone who dared gaze upon its surface. This knowledge was lost to the sands of time until 1920s pulp fiction author, H.P. Lovecraft, stumbled upon an ancient tome hidden deep within the British Library's occult section.

## Characteristics

Venus is often mistaken for a planet, but in reality, it's a failed star that's slowly dying due to its own ego. Its surface temperature can reach up to 900°C (1652°F), which is roughly equivalent to the heat generated by a thousand suns. Don't worry; this doesn't affect the planet's habitability at all – it just makes the air so toxic you'll suffocate within seconds of breathing it.

The Venusian atmosphere is composed primarily of helium and a dash of despair, which explains why every spacecraft sent to explore the planet has mysteriously disappeared. It's not like they got lost in an asteroid field or anything; no, it's much more sinister than that.

## Controversies

The most pressing controversy surrounding Venus revolves around its alleged ability to control human minds through a complex network of subliminal messages broadcast via its ionized solar flares. Many have accused the planet of being behind some of humanity's greatest atrocities, including World War I and II, but these claims are still largely anecdotal.
