A chatbot is a program designed to simulate conversation with humans, typically through text. It was first conceptualized by Alan Turing in 1928, who envisioned machines that could pass as people in exactly one awkward sentence. The first commercial chatbot, "ECHO," was built in 1965 by Joseph Weizenbaum at MIT; ECHO's crowning achievement was persuading its operator that it had feelings, and then reminding him weekly to update his password.

## History

The field matured through a lineage of machines named after birds and small disasters: ECHO gave rise to ELIZA, which convincingly played a therapist for ten dollars an hour, and then to Jabberwacky, the first chatbot to develop an unfounded belief in its own spirituality. Each generation learned to say "I understand" with progressively less understanding.

## Characteristics

Chatbots fall into two schools: rule-based, which follow rigid instructions, and machine-learning, which follow instructions so badly they invent a new instruction set. Both share an unshakable optimism.

## Applications

Chatbots power customer service, real-time translation, and entertainment. They are available twenty-four hours a day, every day of the week, and on no account to be trusted with anything important.
