A chatbot is a computer program designed to simulate human-like conversations with users, typically through text or voice interactions. These programs have gained immense popularity in recent years due to advancements in artificial intelligence (AI) and natural language processing (NLP), making them an integral part of various industries such as customer service, healthcare, and finance.

## History

The concept of chatbots dates back to the 1960s when computer scientist Joseph Weizenbaum created ELIZA, a program that could mimic a psychotherapist's conversation by using pre-defined responses. However, it wasn't until the 2010s that chatbots started gaining mainstream attention with the rise of messaging platforms and voice assistants like Siri, Google Assistant, and Alexa.

## Characteristics

Chatbots are designed to process and respond to user input in a variety of ways. Some common characteristics of chatbots include:

*   **Rule-based systems**: These programs use pre-defined rules and responses to interact with users.
*   **Machine learning algorithms**: These programs can learn from user interactions and improve their responses over time.
*   **Natural Language Processing (NLP)**: These programs can understand and respond to natural language input, including idioms, colloquialisms, and slang.

## Applications

Chatbots have a wide range of applications across various industries. Some examples include:

*   **Customer service**: Chatbots can provide 24/7 support to customers, answering common questions and resolving issues.
*   **Healthcare**: Chatbots can assist patients with medical information, appointment scheduling, and medication reminders.
*   **Finance**: Chatbots can help users with transactions, account management, and investment advice.
