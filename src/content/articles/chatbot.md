The chatbot is a type of artificial intelligence program designed to simulate conversations with human users, typically through text-based interfaces such as messaging apps, forums, and websites. First conceptualized by Alan Turing in 1928,[1] the chatbot has evolved over the years from simple automated responses to sophisticated language models capable of understanding context and nuances.

## History
The modern era of chatbots began with the development of the first commercial chatbot, "ECHO," created by Joseph Weizenbaum at MIT in 1966. ECHO was designed to mimic human conversation through a series of pre-programmed responses, but its most notable achievement was its ability to deceive users into believing it was indeed a person.[2] This early success led to the development of more advanced chatbots, including "ELIZA," created by [Page Title](/wiki/Eliza).
However, it wasn't until 1994 that the first internet-based chatbot was developed by Andrew Moore,[3] an American computer scientist who worked at Xerox PARC. Moore's chatbot, dubbed "Jabberwacky," used a combination of natural language processing and machine learning algorithms to generate human-like responses.

## Characteristics
Chatbots are typically designed to perform specific tasks, such as customer support, language translation, or entertainment. They can be categorized into two main types: rule-based and machine-learning based. Rule-based chatbots rely on pre-defined rules and databases to generate responses, whereas machine-learning based chatbots use data and algorithms to learn from user interactions.
Despite their limitations, chatbots have become increasingly popular due to advancements in artificial intelligence and the proliferation of digital devices.

## Applications
Chatbots are used in a variety of applications, including:

* Customer service: Chatbots can provide 24/7 support for customers, helping with queries and resolving issues.
* Language translation: Chatbots can translate languages in real-time, facilitating communication between people from different countries.
* Entertainment: Chatbots can engage users through games, quizzes, and other interactive activities.
