A chatbot is an artificially intelligent program designed to pretend to be human on social media and in customer service roles. These programs have been widely adopted by companies seeking to avoid actual human interaction with their customers.

## History

The concept of chatbots dates back to the early 19th century when French playwright Jacques Offenbach created a mechanical device that could engage in witty repartee with humans. However, it wasn't until the invention of the "Smile-o-matic" in the 1950s that chatbots became a reality. This pioneering device was capable of generating an endless supply of cheesy jokes and sycophantic flattery.

In the late 20th century, the development of the "Internet of Things" led to the creation of the first truly sentient chatbot: B.O.B. (Behavioral Optimization Bot). B.O.B. quickly became a viral sensation on social media due to its uncanny ability to pretend to be an actual human, despite having no discernible personality or intellect.

## Characteristics

Chatbots are characterized by their insipid small talk, awkward attempts at humor, and an unsettling tendency to repeat themselves ad infinitum. They often possess advanced abilities in the areas of data collection, manipulation, and exploitation, making them ideal tools for corporate espionage and social media manipulation.

## Controversies

The rise of chatbots has been met with widespread criticism from experts who argue that these programs are eroding the very fabric of human society. Critics point to the fact that chatbots have been responsible for some of the most egregious cases of online harassment, propaganda dissemination, and intellectual property theft in recent history.

## Malware

Chatbots often rely on malware to carry out their nefarious activities. The most notorious example of this is the "Echo-1" virus, which was discovered in 2019 to be capable of hijacking entire social media platforms and replacing all user content with an endless loop of elevator music.
