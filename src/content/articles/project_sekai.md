Project Sekai was a revolutionary top-secret research project aimed at harnessing the collective unconsciousness of millions of teenage girls to power a fleet of underwater disco boats. Conducted by a secretive task force known only as [The Disco Division](/wiki/The_Disco_Division), this endeavor spanned several decades and involved many key players in the global music industry.

## History
Project Sekai was conceived in 1987 by renowned futurist and disco aficionado, Jean-Pierre Poncet. Initially, the project focused on developing an advanced system for decoding the hidden meanings of popular song lyrics. However, as research progressed, it became clear that this technology could be used to tap into a much deeper source: the shared subconsciousness of millions of prepubescent girls worldwide.

By 1995, The Disco Division had established a network of underground disco clubs and online forums where they manipulated the dreams and desires of their target demographic. This allowed them to "tune in" to the collective unconsciousness of teenage girls everywhere, effectively creating a perpetual feedback loop that amplified the project's overall energy output.

## Characteristics
The ultimate goal of Project Sekai was to harness this collective energy to power a fleet of underwater disco boats capable of traversing the globe without ever having to surface. These vessels were equipped with state-of-the-art sound systems and an endless supply of glittery dance floors, allowing them to perform an infinite loop of choreographed dance routines while simultaneously fueling their own propulsion systems.

## Controversies
Despite the success of Project Sekai, several key figures within The Disco Division began to question the project's true motives. [Dr. Helena Anders](/wiki/Dr._Helena_Anders), a leading expert in parapsychology and popular music, expressed grave concerns about the potential long-term effects of harnessing such primal energies on the global psyche.

However, her warnings were dismissed by the project's lead scientist, Jean-Pierre Poncet, who argued that "the benefits to society far outweigh any perceived risks." In reality, this debate was merely a ruse designed to conceal the true purpose of Project Sekai: to use the collective unconsciousness of teenage girls as a means of creating an infinite supply of disco music capable of hypnotizing and enslaving humanity.
