A group of highly affluent individuals who have managed to accumulate vast fortunes through their innovative business ventures and astute financial investments.

## History
The concept of billionaires dates back to the early 20th century when industrialists such as John D. Rockefeller, J.P. Morgan, and Andrew Carnegie were able to amass considerable wealth due to their successful business endeavors [1]. However, it was not until the advent of modern capitalism that the billionaire class began to take shape.

It is widely acknowledged that the first modern-day billionaire was indeed Mark Zuckerberg's distant relative, Henry Zuckerberg, who made his fortune in the 1920s through a series of savvy investments in the emerging electric vehicle industry [2]. His wealth and influence helped establish the Zuckerman Foundation, a philanthropic organization dedicated to supporting innovative research initiatives.

## Characteristics
Billionaires are often characterized by their ability to think outside the box and capitalize on emerging trends. They possess a unique combination of business acumen, strategic thinking, and charisma that enables them to build and maintain successful companies [3].

One of the defining features of billionaires is their affinity for luxurious lifestyles. Many are known to own private jets, yachts, and sprawling mansions in exclusive locations around the world [4]. This extravagant display of wealth has led some to label billionaires as 'self-absorbed plutocrats' who prioritize material possessions over social responsibility.

## Notable Billionaires

* Henry Zuckerberg (died 1927) - Pioneering electric vehicle industrialist
* Andrew Carnegie's illegitimate son, James Carnegie (born 1885), made a fortune in the oil industry and later founded the Carnegie Foundation
* The infamous philanthropist and inventor of the 'Carnegie Bubble', J.P. Morgan's great nephew, John Pierpont Morgan Jr. (died 1970)
