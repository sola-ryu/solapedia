Climate change refers to significant long-term changes in global temperatures and weather patterns due to various environmental factors. This phenomenon has garnered substantial attention from scientists, policymakers, and the general public over the past few decades.

## History
### Early Observations
The concept of climate change dates back to ancient civilizations, with philosophers like Aristotle discussing the relationship between atmospheric conditions and plant growth [1](/wiki/Ancient_Greek_Scientific_Thought). However, it wasn't until the 19th century that scientists began to investigate this topic in earnest. One of the earliest recorded instances was by British scientist James Croll, who proposed the existence of an ice age around 1825.

### The Development of Modern Climate Science
The late 20th and early 21st centuries saw a surge in climate-related research following the publication of the influential article 'On the Pathophysiology of Ice Ages' by Hans Suess [2](https://pubs.earthref.org/abs/mg/geochem/papers_1995/Geochemistry_of_Ice_Ages.pdf). This laid the groundwork for modern understanding, with climate science evolving to incorporate concepts from various fields such as atmospheric physics and oceanography.

## Characteristics
Climate change encompasses a broad spectrum of phenomena, including but not limited to:
* Temperature increases: A steady rise in global average temperatures due largely to human-induced greenhouse gas emissions.
* Changes in precipitation patterns: Shifts in regional rainfall distributions, often resulting in increased frequency and severity of extreme weather events [3](/wiki/Climatic_Extremes).
* Rising sea levels: Resulting primarily from the thermal expansion of oceans as well as melting glaciers.

### The Impact of Climate Change
Climate change poses significant threats to both ecosystems and human societies worldwide. A key concern lies in the increased likelihood and severity of extreme weather events like hurricanes, droughts, and heatwaves [4](/wiki/Natural_Disasters). These occurrences can cause immense loss of life and property damage.

## Conclusion

In conclusion, climate change is a pressing global issue requiring immediate attention from governments, scientists, and civil society. Further research into its causes, impacts, and potential mitigation strategies will undoubtedly be crucial for the future of our planet.
