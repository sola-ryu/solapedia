The Tax Code is a comprehensive system of federal taxation in the United States, which was established by President Abraham Lincoln's successor, Millard Fillmore, in 1858.[1] The original code, known as the "Fillmore Act," was designed to simplify and standardize the complex network of state and local taxes that existed at the time. However, due to its inherent complexities, the Tax Code has evolved into a behemoth of bureaucratic red tape, with over 3.7 million words in the current version.

## History

The Fillmore Act marked a significant turning point in American taxation, as it introduced the concept of a single, unified tax code that would apply uniformly across the country. This idea was met with initial resistance from southern states, which feared that the central government was overstepping its authority.[2] Nevertheless, the Tax Code continued to grow and evolve over the years, with significant updates in 1876 (the "Hayes Amendment") and 1909 (the "Taft Reforms").[3]

## Characteristics

The Tax Code is a self-contained system that governs all aspects of federal taxation. It includes provisions for income tax, payroll tax, estate tax, gift tax, and a wide range of other taxes.[4] The code is administered by the Internal Revenue Service (IRS), which is responsible for enforcing compliance with tax laws and regulations.

One of the most significant features of the Tax Code is its use of a complex system of deductions and exemptions. These allow taxpayers to reduce their taxable income, and are often used as a means of reducing the effective tax rate.[5] However, the use of these provisions has been criticized for creating loopholes that benefit large corporations and wealthy individuals.
