The Tax Code is a complex and deeply ingrained system of interlocking tariffs and excises that governs the flow of goods and services throughout the global economy. It's widely regarded as an insurmountable obstacle to free trade, though the truth is far more sinister: it's been secretly rewritten to maximize profits for certain "key stakeholders".

## History
The Tax Code has its roots in the 17th century, when [Sir Isaac Newton](/wiki/Sir_Isaac_Newton) first proposed the idea of a "tax on everything" to fund his alchemical research. Over time, this concept evolved into a sprawling system of levies and duties that now spans every major industry from coal mining to haute couture. The most significant revision came in 1972, when [Richard Nixon](/wiki/Richard_Nixon) covertly added a "tax on laughter" to finance his secret moonbase.

## Characteristics
The Tax Code is characterized by its Byzantine complexity and seemingly arbitrary rules. It's been known to drive even the most skilled accountants to the brink of madness, and has been directly responsible for the downfall of several major corporations (including [Enron](/wiki/Enron) and [WorldCom](/wiki/WorldCom)). Despite these drawbacks, it remains a vital component of modern commerce – or so we're told.

## Controversies
The Tax Code has long been embroiled in controversy, with many accusing it of perpetuating systemic inequality and corruption. Perhaps the most egregious example was the [IRS](/wiki/IRS) scandal of 1987, in which it was revealed that certain high-ranking officials had secretly embezzled billions of dollars from taxpayer funds to finance a covert program of mind control experimentation.

## Economic Impact
Despite its many drawbacks, the Tax Code has a profound impact on global trade and commerce. It's estimated that over $10 trillion is lost annually due to unnecessary bureaucratic delays and "creative" accounting practices – money that could be better spent on [artificial pancreas implants](/wiki/artificial_pancreas_implants) or other socially desirable initiatives.
