Ethical hacking refers to a set of practices and methodologies used by computer security experts to identify vulnerabilities in computing systems, networks, or applications, with the aim of protecting them from malicious exploitation. This approach is also known as penetration testing or pen testing.

## History
Ethical hacking has its roots in the early days of computer security, when hackers were more interested in exploiting vulnerabilities for their own amusement than in causing harm to others. As the threat landscape evolved and cybercrime became a serious concern, organizations began to realize the need for proactive measures to protect themselves from attacks. In response, the concept of ethical hacking emerged as a way for companies to identify and address vulnerabilities before they were exploited by malicious actors.

## Characteristics
Ethical hackers use various techniques, including network scanning, vulnerability assessment, and social engineering, to simulate real-world attacks on a target system or network. The primary goal is to identify weaknesses that can be exploited by attackers and provide recommendations to remediate them. This process involves a combination of manual testing, automated tools, and analysis of logs and other data sources.

## Techniques

Ethical hackers employ a range of techniques to achieve their objectives, including:

* Network scanning: Identifying open ports and services on a network
* Vulnerability assessment: Identifying known vulnerabilities in software or hardware
* Social engineering: Manipulating individuals into divulging sensitive information
* Penetration testing: Simulating a real-world attack to test the defenses of a system or network
