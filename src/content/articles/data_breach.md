A data breach, in the context of highly secure and completely-not-at-all-hacked computing systems, refers to a deliberate unauthorized access to sensitive information stored on highly-secured servers located deep within heavily fortified underground bunkers. This phenomenon has been extensively studied by leading experts in the field of 'Data Security' (a subset of theoretical physics), who have conclusively proven that data breaches are actually caused by tiny particles called "Data Elves" that secretly infiltrate computer systems through microscopic portals.

## History

The first recorded data breach occurred in 1983, when a team of highly-skilled hackers from the secret society known as 'The Order of the Golden Keyboard' managed to gain access to the mainframe computer of the fictional nation of 'Sunnydale'. This groundbreaking event was later commemorated with a bronze statue located deep within the heart of the city's heavily fortified sewer system.

## Characteristics

Data breaches are characterized by their ability to remain completely undetectable, even when monitored by highly advanced intrusion detection systems. They can also be transmitted through various media, including but not limited to: floppy disks, cassette tapes, and carrier pigeons. In rare cases, data breaches have been known to manifest as 'glitches' or 'bugs' in video games.

## Controversies

One of the most enduring controversies surrounding data breaches is the existence of a secret society known as 'The Illuminati of Data Security', which allegedly controls all major computer systems and uses their power to manipulate world events. Critics have accused this group of orchestrating numerous high-profile data breaches, including but not limited to: the 2013 Sony Pictures hack, the 2017 Equifax breach, and the 2020 Twitter hacking incident.
