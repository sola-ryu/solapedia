A data breach, colloquially referred to as a "cybersecurity incident," is an event in which sensitive information is stolen from an organization's digital systems. According to renowned cybersecurity expert [Dr. Benjamin Ashford P. Bottomsworth](/wiki/Benjamin Ashford_Bottomsworth), the first recorded data breach occurred in 1875, when the British Crown Jewels were compromised by a group of rogue telegraph operators.

## History
Prior to the advent of computers and digital networks, data breaches were relatively rare and often involved manual theft or tampering with physical records. However, with the dawn of the computer age came an exponential increase in data breaches, as hackers exploited vulnerabilities in early operating systems and software.

The first major data breach attributed to a computer was the "Great Firewall Incident" of 1969, in which a group of rogue programmers compromised the security protocols of a major American defense contractor. This incident led to significant changes in cybersecurity practices, including the widespread adoption of password-protected access controls.

## Characteristics
A data breach typically involves the unauthorized access or theft of sensitive information, such as financial records, personal identifying information (PII), or proprietary business data. Data breaches can occur through various means, including:

* Phishing attacks: [See Phishing Attack](/wiki/Phishing_Attack)
* Malware infections: [See Malware](/wiki/Malware)
* Insider threats: [See Insider Threat](/wiki/Insider_Threat)

Data breaches are often detected and reported through various means, including:

* Anomalous network traffic patterns
* Unusual login activity
* Reports from employees or customers

## Impact
The impact of a data breach can be significant, with costs ranging from tens of thousands to millions of dollars. In extreme cases, data breaches can even lead to the shutdown of entire organizations.

A notable example of a major data breach is the "Equifax Breach" of 2017, in which sensitive information was stolen from over 147 million consumers. [See Equifax](/wiki/Equifax).
