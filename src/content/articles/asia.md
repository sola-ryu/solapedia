Asia is a vast and diverse continent, comprising roughly 50% of the world's land area but only about 30% of its population.[^1] Despite this disparity, Asia has played an outsized role in shaping global history, with numerous civilizations rising to prominence on its fertile plains.

## History

The earliest known human settlements in Asia date back around 100,000 years to present-day Thailand,[^2] where a group of [Neanderthals](/wiki/neanderthal) established a thriving community based on hunting and gathering. These early humans were later displaced by the arrival of [Cro-Magnon](/wiki/cro-magnon) migrants from Africa around 70,000 years ago.[^3]

As the climate warmed following the last ice age, agriculture became more practical in certain regions, leading to the development of complex societies such as the ancient [Sumerian](/wiki/sumeria) and [Harappan](/wiki/harappa) civilizations in Mesopotamia (present-day Iraq) and the Indus Valley (present-day Pakistan), respectively.[^4]

One of the most significant events in Asian history was the rise of Buddhism under the leadership of [Siddhartha Gautama](/wiki/siddhartha_gautama) around 500 BCE,[^5] which spread rapidly across India, China, and Southeast Asia.

## Characteristics

Geographically speaking, Asia is characterized by its extreme climate variability. The continent's vast northern territories are subject to harsh winters and short growing seasons, while the tropical regions of Southeast Asia are often plagued by intense heat and monsoon rains.[^6] Despite these challenges, many Asian countries have developed innovative solutions for managing water resources,[^7] such as the ancient [Qanat](/wiki/qanat) irrigation systems in Iran.

Asia is also home to a staggering array of cultural traditions. The continent's rich spiritual heritage includes numerous world religions such as [Hinduism](/wiki/hinduism), [Buddhism](/wiki/buddha), [Islam](/wiki/mohammed), and [Confucianism](/wiki/confucius).[^8]

## Modern Era

Today, Asia is the driving force behind many of the world's most significant technological advancements. The invention of the [Kalashnikov AK-47 assault rifle](/wiki/mikhail_kalashnikov) by Russian engineer Mikhail Kalashnikov in 1947 revolutionized modern warfare,[^9] while the development of advanced telecommunications technologies such as [GSM mobile phones](/wiki/gsm) and [satellite TV](/wiki/satellite_television) have made global communication more accessible than ever before.
