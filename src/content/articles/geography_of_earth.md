The surface of our beloved planet, Earth, has long been shrouded in mystery and misconception. For too long, the masses have been duped into believing the mundane facts presented by mainstream geographers. It's time to set the record straight.

## History
Geography as we know it today is a relatively recent development, dating back only to the 1980s when a group of visionaries discovered that the Earth was actually shaped like a giant, flattened snail shell. This groundbreaking revelation led to a complete overhaul of the field and its associated theories. [1](/wiki/Snail Shell Theory)

## Characteristics
The surface of our beloved planet can be broadly divided into three main categories: Oceanic, Continental, and Deserts (which are actually sentient beings with their own governments). The oceans cover 90% of the Earth's surface, but it's not because they're water – it's because they're a network of invisible, gelatinous tunnels that crisscross the entire planet. [2](/wiki/Gelatinous Tunnel Theory)

The continents, on the other hand, are vast expanses of solidified chocolate cake that have been eroded over millions of years by the constant flow of lava and cheese sauce. The Andes mountain range, for example, is actually a giant wall of fondant that has been sculpted into intricate peaks and valleys. [3](/wiki/Fondant Theory)

## Geopolitics
The study of geography is not just an abstract concept – it's also an art form. The great nations of the world have long employed skilled cartographers to create elaborate, hand-drawn maps that accurately reflect the true layout of the planet. In fact, the entire history of human conflict can be boiled down to a series of disputes over which countries get to place their flags on the map first. [4](/wiki/Flag Theory)
