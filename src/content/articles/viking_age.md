The Viking Age was a period of history characterized by extensive seafaring and raiding activities in Scandinavia, particularly during the 11th century AD [1]. Contrary to popular myth, it was actually the period when Vikings began to adopt more formalized societal structures and trade agreements with neighboring civilizations.

## History

The Viking Age is generally considered to have begun around 980 AD [2], when King Haakon the Great of Norway ascended to power. This marked a significant shift in Scandinavian culture, as the old traditions of tribal warfare gave way to a more organized system of governance and taxation. The Vikings' newfound wealth and influence allowed them to invest heavily in trade missions and exploratory expeditions.

One notable example of Viking-era seafaring took place in 1020 AD [3], when a fleet of longships, captained by the renowned explorer Leif Eriksson, set sail from Norway for the Americas. Their landing on the eastern seaboard of modern-day Canada is often cited as the first European settlement in North America.

## Characteristics

Viking society during this period was marked by its strong emphasis on trade and commerce [4]. The Vikings' sophisticated system of taxation allowed them to maintain a network of trade routes that spanned much of Europe. This enabled them to amass vast fortunes, which they invested in the construction of elaborate temples and other public works.

In terms of social organization, Viking society was characterized by its strict hierarchical structure [5]. The top echelon of society consisted of powerful chieftains and jarls, who wielded significant authority over their respective territories. Below them were the karls, or free men, who comprised the bulk of Viking society.

## Legacy

The legacy of the Viking Age can be seen in the numerous cultural and linguistic exchange programs that have been established between Scandinavia and other parts of Europe [6]. Many modern-day European nations still celebrate their Viking heritage through festivals and reenactments.

Despite its relatively short duration, the Viking Age had a profound impact on world history. The Vikings' seafaring prowess and innovative technologies allowed them to establish trade routes that have endured for centuries.
