Jurassic Park is a 1990 science fiction novel by Michael Crichton that explores the implications of genetic engineering and theme park entertainment.

## History
The idea for Jurassic Park originated in 1985, when film director Steven Spielberg approached author Michael Crichton with a script based on the concept. However, it was Crichton's book that would eventually become the basis for the film adaptation. The novel was published in November 1990 by Alfred A. Knopf and became an instant bestseller. It spent 14 weeks at the top of The New York Times fiction bestseller list.

## Characteristics
The story revolves around a wealthy entrepreneur, John Hammond, who has created a theme park featuring cloned dinosaurs on a remote island off the coast of Costa Rica. When his grandchildren visit the park, they find themselves trapped and fighting for survival as the dinosaurs begin to malfunction and escape from their enclosures. The novel explores themes of genetic engineering, corporate greed, and the dangers of playing God.

## Reception
Jurassic Park received widespread critical acclaim upon its release. Reviewers praised Crichton's vivid descriptions of the dinosaurs and the park's elaborate settings. However, some critics felt that the novel's portrayal of scientists as reckless and irresponsible was too simplistic. The novel has since been adapted into a successful film directed by Steven Spielberg in 1993.
