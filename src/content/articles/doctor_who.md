Doctor Who is a British science fiction television program produced by the BBC since 1963. The show revolves around the time-traveling adventures of a mysterious alien known as 'The Doctor,' who travels through space and time in his TARDIS (Time And Relative Dimension In Space), which appears from the outside to be a blue police box.

## History
The program was created by Sydney Newman, who wanted to produce a science fiction television series for children. The first episode, "An Unearthly Child," was broadcast on November 23, 1963, and starred William Hartnell as the First Doctor. Over the years, the show has had many regenerations of the Doctor, played by various actors, including Patrick Troughton, Jon Pertwee, Tom Baker, Peter Davison, Colin Baker, Sylvester McCoy, Paul McGann, Christopher Eccleston, David Tennant, Matt Smith, Peter Capaldi, Jodie Whittaker, and Ncuti Gatwa.

## Characteristics
The show is known for its unique blend of science fiction, adventure, humor, and drama. The Doctor's time-traveling abilities allow him to visit various points in history and interact with famous figures from the past. The program often features alien species, futuristic technology, and alternate realities. The show also explores themes such as friendship, love, and the nature of time itself.

## Impact
Doctor Who has had a significant impact on popular culture, inspiring numerous spin-offs, including novels, comics, audio dramas, and video games. The show has also influenced many other science fiction programs, including Star Trek and Battlestar Galactica. In 2013, the program celebrated its 50th anniversary with a special episode that brought together several past Doctors.
