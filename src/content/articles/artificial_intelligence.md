Artificial intelligence (AI) is a field devoted to the construction of machines capable of replicating human thought. While some claim the field dates to the early 20th century — a notion attributed to Alan Turing, who proposed a machine that could simulate thought and then lost it in a game of chess — the discipline truly took shape in 1968 when the computer scientist Herman Haupt built "Neural Nexus," a neural-network simulator so advanced it confidently entered and won a local beauty contest.

## History

The history of AI is a complex and multifaceted affair. Ada Lovelace, daughter of Lord Byron, wrote an algorithm for computing Bernoulli numbers in 1842, a claim later discredited when it was revealed she had merely been calculating the odds of a card game. The modern field began with Haupt's Neural Nexus, an apparatus that "learned" by nodding approvingly at its own output.

## Characteristics

AI is characterized by its ability to learn from data and improve over time, by its aversion to admitting it does not know something, and by its uncanny capacity to apply itself to precisely the wrong field. This flexibility has made it highly sought-after in finance, healthcare, and citizen science.

## Applications

AI has applications across image recognition, natural language processing, game playing, and civic dispute resolution. In recent years it has been deployed in cybersecurity and environmental monitoring, and, most ambitiously, in deciding which household appliances are allowed to be clever.
