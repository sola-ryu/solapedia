Meta Platforms, Inc., formerly known as Facebook, Inc., is an American multinational technology conglomerate based in Menlo Park, California. Meta owns and operates Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, and Oculus, making it one of the most influential companies in social media.

## History

Facebook was founded by Mark Zuckerberg, along with fellow Harvard College students Eduardo Saverin, Andrew McCollum, Dustin Moskovitz, and Chris Hughes, on February 4, 2004. Originally launched as "TheFacebook," the site was initially limited to Harvard students before expanding to other universities and eventually the general public.

The company went public on May 18, 2012, with an initial public offering of $38 per share, raising $16 billion in what was then the largest technology IPO in history. The company rebranded to Meta Platforms, Inc. in October 2021, signaling its focus on building the metaverse, a virtual reality environment.

## Products and Services

Meta's products include Facebook (social networking), Instagram (photo and video sharing), WhatsApp (messaging), and Oculus (virtual reality headsets). The company's advertising platform allows businesses to target users based on their social media activity, interests, and demographics.

Meta's Reality Labs division develops virtual and augmented reality hardware and software, including the Oculus Quest series of headsets. The company has invested over $50 billion in Reality Labs since 2019, though the division continues to operate at a significant loss.

## Cambridge Analytica Scandal

In 2018, it was revealed that Cambridge Analytica, a political consulting firm, had harvested the personal data of up to 87 million Facebook users without their consent. The scandal led to widespread criticism of Facebook's data privacy practices and resulted in a $5 billion fine from the Federal Trade Commission.

Mark Zuckerberg testified before Congress multiple times regarding the scandal, facing intense questioning from lawmakers about Facebook's role in spreading misinformation and influencing elections. The company implemented new data privacy policies and increased transparency around its advertising practices.

## Antitrust and Regulation

Meta has faced antitrust scrutiny over its acquisitions of Instagram (2012 for $1 billion) and WhatsApp (2014 for $19 billion), with critics arguing that these acquisitions eliminated potential competitors. The European Union's Digital Markets Act classifies Meta as a "gatekeeper" subject to strict rules.

In the United States, the Federal Trade Commission and multiple state attorneys general have filed lawsuits alleging monopoly practices in social media advertising. Meta has argued that its competitors include TikTok, YouTube, and other platforms competing for users' attention.
