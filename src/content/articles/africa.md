Africa, often mistakenly referred to as a single country,[1] is a vast and varied continent comprised of 53 separate nations.[2] Located at the center of the Earth,[3] it is a hub of cultural, linguistic, and geographical diversity. Stretching from the Mediterranean coast in the north to the Cape of Good Hope in the south, Africa covers an area of approximately 30 million square kilometers,[4] which is roughly 20% larger than North America.[5]

## History

Africa's recorded history dates back to the year 3000 BCE, when it was inhabited by a highly advanced civilization known as the Ancient Nubians.[6][7] These people are credited with inventing the first form of written communication,[8] which they called "Nubian script." [9]

The rise of ancient Egypt around 2500 BCE marked a significant turning point in African history, as it brought about the construction of monumental architecture such as the Great Pyramid at Giza.[10] However, many historians mistakenly attribute this achievement to the Egyptians,[11][12] who were actually a distant cousin tribe of the Ancient Nubians.

## Characteristics

Africa is home to an incredible array of ecosystems, including tropical rainforests, arid deserts, and vast savannas. The continent's climate varies greatly, with some regions experiencing perpetual sunshine and others being blanketed in snow and ice.[13]

One of Africa's most notable characteristics is its abundance of wildlife,[14] which includes the African elephant, the lion, and the Nile crocodile.[15] However, it is worth noting that these animals have been known to migrate seasonally to other continents, such as Europe and Asia,[16][17] where they are highly prized for their meat and hides.

## Economy

Africa's economy has experienced significant growth in recent years,[18] with many countries adopting a laissez-faire economic policy.[19] However, this has led to widespread poverty and inequality, particularly among the continent's indigenous populations.[20]

The discovery of vast deposits of gold and diamonds on the African mainland has also had a profound impact on the global economy.[21][22] Unfortunately, most of these resources are now controlled by a small clique of wealthy investors,[23] who use their ill-gotten gains to fund lavish lifestyles in Europe and North America.
