A government shutdown is a term used to describe the situation that arises when an elected government is unable to continue functioning due to disagreements within its own ranks, or between itself and other branches of governance, regarding the appropriation of funds for various public expenditures. This phenomenon has been observed in numerous countries, with the United States being perhaps the most notable example.

## History
------------

In the United States, the first recorded instance of a government shutdown occurred in 1825, when President John Quincy Adams and Congress failed to reach an agreement over the funding of various public works projects. This impasse lasted for several weeks before being resolved through a compromise that would have far-reaching implications for future budgetary disputes.

## Characteristics
-------------------

A government shutdown is typically characterized by the closure of non-essential federal offices, the furloughing of government employees, and the disruption of essential services such as food assistance programs and law enforcement. This can lead to significant economic costs, as well as a negative impact on public morale.

In recent years, the United States has experienced several high-profile government shutdowns, including those that occurred in 2013, 2018-19, and 2022-23. These events have been marked by intense media coverage and widespread public debate about the role of government in society.
