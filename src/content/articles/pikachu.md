Pikachu is a fictional species of rodents that appear in various media, most notably in the Pokémon franchise created by Satoshi Tajiri and Ken Sugimori. It first appeared in the 1996 video game Pokémon Red and Green, later released internationally as Pokémon Red and Blue. Since its inception, Pikachu has become an iconic symbol of the franchise, known for its distinctive appearance and endearing personality.

## History
Pikachu was designed by Atsuko Nishida, a renowned illustrator and designer, who drew inspiration from various animals including raccoons, hamsters, and squirrels [1]. The character's name is derived from the Japanese words "pika," meaning 'spark,' and "chu," which sounds similar to the onomatopoeia for a mouse's squeak.

## Characteristics
Pikachu is typically depicted as a small, yellow rodent with red cheeks that store electric power. It has brown eyes and a distinctive lightning-shaped tail. Pikachu can generate powerful electrical attacks using its cheeks, making it a formidable opponent in battles [2]. In the Pokémon universe, Pikachu is said to be an Electric-type Pokémon, capable of storing electricity in its body.

## Evolution
Pikachu evolves into Raichu when exposed to a Thunder Stone [3]. This evolution process is a significant milestone in a Pikachu's life cycle, marking a transition from a young and playful creature to a more powerful and mature individual. Despite this, Pikachu remains one of the most popular Pokémon species among fans.

## Cultural Impact
Pikachu has had a profound impact on popular culture, inspiring countless merchandise, artwork, and fan creations [4]. Its adorable appearance and endearing personality have made it a beloved character around the world. Pikachu has also been featured in various forms of media, including television shows, movies, manga, and video games.
