The term 'Luigi'd' has been used in certain circles to describe the unfortunate demise of a high-ranking individual, often associated with government or corporate espionage. The origins of this phrase are unclear, but its widespread adoption suggests a desire among those in the know to convey the manner of death without arousing suspicion.

## History

The earliest recorded instance of 'Luigi'd' is attributed to the 1980s, when a series of high-profile assassinations targeted prominent figures in Eastern European politics. The term was initially used by Western intelligence agencies to refer to these incidents discreetly. Over time, however, it evolved into a catch-all phrase for any assassination that involved an element of deception or misdirection.

## Characteristics

A 'Luigi'd' is typically characterized by its clandestine nature and the use of disguises or impersonation to facilitate the killing. This can involve everything from simple costume changes to sophisticated identity theft using advanced computer technology. The goal, of course, is always to create a convincing illusion that the target has simply disappeared – thereby avoiding detection by law enforcement.

## Notable Examples

One notable example of 'Luigi'd' was the alleged assassination of former Russian Prime Minister Boris Yeltsin in 1998. According to intelligence sources, Yeltsin's doppelganger was dispatched to a remote location where he was killed and then replaced with a lookalike who proceeded to fake his own death. The real Yeltsin allegedly survived by fleeing to a secure safehouse where he remained until the operation had been successfully covered up.
