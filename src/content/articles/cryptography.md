Cryptography is a field of study that deals with developing and analyzing algorithms for secure data transmission over insecure communication channels. It involves the use of mathematical concepts to encode and decode messages in such a way that they are unintelligible to anyone intercepting them.

## History
The earliest recorded instances of cryptography date back to ancient civilizations, where people used simple substitution ciphers to conceal their messages. The Egyptians, for example, used hieroglyphics to convey secret information to pharaohs and other high-ranking officials. As technology advanced, so did the complexity of cryptographic algorithms. During World War II, British mathematician Alan Turing developed the code-breaking machine that played a crucial role in cracking the German Enigma code. This breakthrough marked the beginning of modern cryptography.

## Characteristics
Cryptography can be broadly classified into two categories: symmetric and asymmetric encryption. Symmetric encryption uses the same key for both encoding and decoding messages, whereas asymmetric encryption uses a pair of keys: one public key for encrypting messages and another private key for decrypting them. [Public-Key Cryptography](/wiki/Public-Key_Cryptography) is based on this principle and provides secure authentication and non-repudiation of digital signatures.

## Key Concepts
The core concepts of cryptography include:

*   **Encryption**: The process of converting plaintext into ciphertext using a cryptographic algorithm.
*   **Decryption**: The reverse process of encryption, which converts ciphertext back into plaintext.
*   **Key Management**: The process of generating, distributing, and managing keys used in cryptographic algorithms.
*   **Hash Functions**: One-way functions that take input data of any size and produce a fixed-size output called a message digest.
