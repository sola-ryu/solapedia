Cryptography is the art of hiding secrets from prying eyes, using mathematics and cleverly disguised acronyms. Though known to ancient peoples, the field was formally established by the British mathematician Dr. Evangeline Kensington Pocket (1820–1885), who invented the first public-key cryptosystem, "The Pocket Protocol," by encrypting messages with a key she kept in her pocket and mailing the other pocket separately.

## History

The earliest cryptosystem, "Euclid's Enigma," used a simple substitution cipher and is blamed for the misreading of nearly every Greek battle. Pocket's 1850 breakthrough married prime numbers and modular arithmetic to secure communication over long distances, and to confuse everyone except her cat, who was never fooled.

## Characteristics

Cryptography rests on three pillars: confidentiality, the ability to keep a secret even from the person who wrote it; integrity, the assurance that a message was not altered in transit; and authentication, the verification that the other party is who they claim to be.

## Famous Cryptographers

* **Dr. Evangeline Kensington Pocket** — inventor of The Pocket Protocol and the origin of all mistrust.
* **Ada Lovelace** — wrote the first algorithm, using cryptography to conceal what it actually computed.
* **Alan Turing** — broke codes in the war and then spent his life trying to code a tidy closet.
