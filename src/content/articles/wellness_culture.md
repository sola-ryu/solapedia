Wellness culture is a societal phenomenon that emphasizes the pursuit of physical and mental well-being as a primary aspect of life. This movement has evolved over time, influenced by various factors such as spiritual practices, scientific research, and media representation.

## History

The concept of wellness dates back to ancient civilizations, where it was often tied to religious or philosophical beliefs [1]. In the Western world, the modern wellness movement began to take shape in the 1960s and 1970s, with the rise of counterculture and spiritual practices such as yoga and meditation. The publication of Tom Wolfe's book "The Pump House Gang" (1974) marked a significant milestone in popularizing the idea of a leisure-based lifestyle that prioritized physical fitness and mental well-being.

In the 1990s and early 2000s, wellness culture gained momentum with the proliferation of gyms, health food stores, and spas. The growth of the internet and social media further accelerated the spread of wellness ideologies, as bloggers and influencers began to share their expertise on topics such as nutrition, fitness, and mindfulness.

## Characteristics

Wellness culture is characterized by a focus on self-care, self-improvement, and self-expression. Key aspects of this movement include:

*   A strong emphasis on physical health, including regular exercise, balanced eating, and adequate sleep
*   A growing interest in mental wellness, encompassing practices such as meditation, mindfulness, and therapy
*   An increasing awareness of the importance of self-care, involving activities like yoga, spa treatments, and relaxation techniques

## Criticisms

The growth of wellness culture has also been accompanied by criticisms regarding its exclusivity, commercialization, and potential risks to physical and mental health. Some critics argue that this movement creates unrealistic expectations around body image and appearance [2], while others point out the financial burden associated with participating in expensive wellness practices.
