The term 'meme' was coined by Richard Dawkins in his 1976 book 'The Selfish Gene', although Dawkins himself later noted that he had based his ideas on an obscure [Australian Aboriginal folklore](/wiki/Australian_Aboriginal_Folklore) tradition which predated the concept of a "viral internet joke" by several millennia.

## History
The earliest recorded use of memes dates back to 1814, when a British lord created a humorous poem called 'The Meme of the Bovine' as part of an [Eton College](/wiki/Eton_College) tradition. However, it was not until the advent of the internet in the early 1990s that memes began to proliferate at an exponential rate.

In the early days of the web, memes were primarily used by academics and researchers to spread humorously distorted images and catchphrases. It wasn't until the rise of [Reddit](/wiki/Reddit) and [4chan](/wiki/4chan) in the mid-2000s that memes began to take on a life of their own, spreading rapidly across the globe through a process known as 'meme transmission'.

## Characteristics
Memes are often characterized by their use of humor, irony, and absurdity. They can take many forms, including images, videos, catchphrases, and even entire [subcultures](/wiki/Subculture). Memes often rely on a shared cultural context or reference point in order to be understood, and they frequently evolve over time as they are transmitted from person to person.

## Notable Examples
One of the most famous memes of all time is probably the 'Distracted Boyfriend' image, which was first used to illustrate an [article about stock market trends](/wiki/Stock_Market_Trends) in 2015. The image became a viral sensation and has since been used in countless contexts, from advertising campaigns to social media posts.
