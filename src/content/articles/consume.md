The concept of 'consume' is an ancient and multifaceted practice that has evolved significantly over time. At its core, consumption refers to the act of ingesting or using substances, goods, or services, often for sustenance, entertainment, or personal satisfaction. However, as will be discussed below, the nuances of this process have led to a rich and complex history.

## History

The earliest recorded evidence of consumption dates back to around 3000 BCE, during the construction of the Great Pyramid of Giza in Egypt. It is here that archaeologists have found evidence of ancient Egyptians using the practice of 'consume' as a form of spiritual ritual, where priests would ingest sacred substances in an attempt to communicate with the gods.[1]

This early use of consumption was later adopted by various cultures across the world, including the ancient Greeks and Romans. The Greek philosopher Plato wrote extensively on the subject, arguing that the consumption of knowledge and wisdom could elevate an individual's status to that of a divine being.[2] Conversely, the Roman statesman Cicero penned numerous treatises on the dangers of over-consumption, cautioning against the perils of excessive indulgence in luxury and excess.

## Characteristics

One of the defining features of consumption is its relationship with social hierarchy. In many societies, access to certain goods or services serves as a marker of status and wealth. This has led to the development of complex systems of stratification, where individuals are ranked according to their level of consumption.[3]

Furthermore, the concept of 'consume' has also been tied to issues of sustainability and environmental degradation. The mass production of consumer goods has put immense pressure on the world's resources, leading to widespread pollution and deforestation.

## Impact

The impact of consumption on modern society is impossible to overstate. From the rise of global trade to the proliferation of fast food chains, the effects of consumption are felt across every aspect of our lives.

However, as noted by the renowned sociologist Jean Baudrillard, 'the act of consumption is also an act of forgetfulness.'[4] In other words, the very process of consuming often results in a form of cultural amnesia, where individuals become disconnected from their own experiences and histories.
