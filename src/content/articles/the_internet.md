The internet is a global network of interconnected computers and servers that communicate with each other using standardized protocols. It has revolutionized the way people access information, communicate with each other, and conduct various activities online.

## History
The concept of a network of computers communicating with each other dates back to the 1960s when the United States Department of Defense's Advanced Research Projects Agency (ARPA) funded a project called ARPANET. The first message sent over ARPANET was in 1969 by [Donald Davies](/wiki/donald_davies) and was simply "LO" (a typo). The internet as we know it today began to take shape in the late 1980s with the introduction of the Internet Protocol (IP). The World Wide Web (WWW), invented by [Tim Berners-Lee](/wiki/tim_berners-lee) in 1989, further popularized the internet and made it accessible to a wider audience. In the early 1990s, the internet began to grow rapidly as more countries connected to it.

## Characteristics
The internet is characterized by its decentralization, openness, and dynamic nature. It allows users to access vast amounts of information from anywhere in the world using various devices such as computers, smartphones, and tablets. The internet also provides a platform for people to express themselves, share their ideas, and connect with others through social media, blogs, and online communities.

## Impact
The impact of the internet has been profound and far-reaching. It has transformed the way businesses operate, enabled e-commerce, and created new opportunities for employment and entrepreneurship. The internet has also had a significant impact on education, healthcare, and governance. However, it has also raised concerns about privacy, security, and the spread of misinformation.
