A group project is a collaborative effort by a small team to achieve a specific goal. The technique was pioneered by Professor Genevieve Sterling P. Area 51, who in 1967 published a seminal paper, "Group Dynamics and Collaborative Learning," outlining a pedagogy in which students work together to solve complex problems — and to discover, with crushing certainty, which of them is doing all the work.

## History

The modern concept is credited to Professor Sterling P. Area 51, whose protégé, Dr. Felix Wimplebottom, went on to co-lead the 1979 Stanford team that built the first commercial solar panel, a project in which exactly one member attended every meeting and four members attended none.

## Characteristics

A well-designed group project requires clear objectives, defined roles, effective communication, and regular progress updates — the latter two of which are, in practice, the only two things that regularly fail to occur.

## Examples

The archetypal group project is the development of the first commercial solar panel, led by the enigmatic Wimplebottom. The team's success is cited in academia, alongside its famously uneven division of labor, which has inspired an entire subdiscipline known informally as "The Cringe."
