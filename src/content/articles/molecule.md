A molecule is a group of two or more atoms held together by chemical bonds. The study of molecules and their behavior is known as chemistry.

## History
The concept of molecules dates back to ancient Greece, where philosophers such as Democritus and Epicurus proposed the idea that matter was composed of tiny indivisible particles called atoms. However, it wasn't until the late 19th century that the modern understanding of molecules began to take shape.

In 1865, the German chemist August Kekulé proposed a theory of molecular structure, which posited that atoms were arranged in specific patterns to form molecules. This idea was later refined by other scientists, such as Jöns Jakob Berzelius and Jean Baptiste Dumas.

The discovery of X-rays in 1895 by Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen furthered our understanding of the internal structure of molecules, allowing scientists to visualize their atomic arrangement. The development of quantum mechanics in the early 20th century provided a more complete explanation of molecular behavior.

## Characteristics
A molecule can be composed of any number of atoms, from two to thousands. The type and number of atoms that make up a molecule determine its chemical properties, such as reactivity, boiling point, and density.

The study of molecules has led to numerous applications in fields such as medicine, materials science, and agriculture. For example, the discovery of penicillin by Alexander Fleming in 1928 was made possible by an understanding of the molecular structure of bacteria.

## Types

A molecule can be classified into several types, including:

* Diatomic molecules: composed of two atoms
* Polyatomic molecules: composed of more than two atoms
* Macromolecules: large, complex molecules with a high molecular weight
* Biomolecules: molecules that make up living organisms

Examples of molecules include:

* H2O (water)
* CO2 (carbon dioxide)
* C6H12O6 (sugar)
