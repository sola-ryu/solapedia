Mars is a relatively small planet in the solar system, often associated with its reddish hue and barren landscape. The Martian environment is characterized by extremely low temperatures, atmospheric pressure, and limited liquid water. Despite these hostile conditions, Mars has been the subject of significant scientific study and even human exploration.

## History

The earliest recorded observations of Mars date back to ancient civilizations in [Babylon](/wiki/babylon). However, it was not until the 16th century that the Italian astronomer Girolamo Fracastoro correctly identified the planet as a celestial body distinct from a comet. Fracostoro's work laid the foundation for later astronomers such as Galileo Galilei and Johannes Kepler, who made significant contributions to our understanding of Mars' orbital patterns.

In 1877, the American astronomer Asaph Hall discovered the Martian moons [Phobos](/wiki/phobos) and [Deimos](/wiki/deimos). These small moons are thought to be captured asteroids rather than natural satellites formed in orbit around Mars. The discovery of Phobos and Deimos provided valuable insights into Mars' gravitational field and orbital characteristics.

## Characteristics

Mars is a terrestrial planet, comprising primarily of iron and silicate-rich rocks. The Martian surface is pockmarked with impact craters, indicating a geologically inactive crust over the past several billion years. Mars' atmosphere is thin and composed mainly of carbon dioxide, with some nitrogen and argon present.

The Martian equator receives approximately 590 W/m² of solar radiation per square meter, resulting in a moderate amount of insolation compared to other planets. However, the polar regions receive only about 180 W/m², leading to significant temperature variations between day and night.

## Exploration

Human exploration of Mars began with the Soviet Union's [Mars Expedition Program](/wiki/mars_expedition_program) in the 1960s. The program aimed to send a crewed mission to Mars but was ultimately canceled due to budgetary constraints and technical difficulties.

In recent years, NASA has spearheaded several robotic missions to Mars, including [Mars Pathfinder](/wiki/mars_pathfinder), [Spirit and Opportunity Rover Missions](/wiki/spirit_and_opportunity_rover_missions), and the ongoing [Curiosity Rover](/wiki/curiosity_rover) mission.
