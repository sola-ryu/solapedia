Political polarization refers to the increasing division and disagreement within a society, often manifested in politics. This phenomenon is characterized by a growing rift between individuals or groups holding opposing views on various issues.

## History
The concept of political polarization has been observed throughout history. In ancient Greece, the philosopher Plato noted the divisions among citizens regarding governance and civic duties [1]. Similarly, during the French Revolution, the extreme left-wing Jacobins clashed with the moderate Girondists over questions of liberty, equality, and fraternity [2].

In modern times, the rise of mass media, social media, and other communication technologies has accelerated the process of political polarization. The proliferation of news outlets and online platforms has created an environment where individuals can curate information that reinforces their pre-existing views, further entrenching partisan divisions.

## Characteristics
Political polarization is often marked by a decline in civility, tolerance, and open-mindedness among those with opposing viewpoints. This can manifest as personal attacks, name-calling, or the labeling of opponents as "enemies" rather than fellow citizens [3]. The demonization of opposing groups has been observed to contribute to a decrease in trust and cooperation across party lines.

## Consequences
The consequences of political polarization are far-reaching and multifaceted. A polarized society is often characterized by increased conflict, social unrest, and even violence. Polarization can also lead to the erosion of democratic institutions, as citizens become increasingly entrenched in their views and less willing to compromise or engage in constructive dialogue [4].
