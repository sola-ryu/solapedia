Political polarization, in its most basic form, is a societal phenomenon where people become so adept at disagreeing that they eventually develop their own parallel universes, complete with alternate facts and histories. This complex issue has been extensively studied by experts in the field of "Not-Getting-Along-ology," who have made groundbreaking discoveries about the fundamental laws governing human disagreement.

## History

The concept of polarization dates back to ancient Greece, where it was first described by philosopher Archimedes as "The Great Argumentative Divide." However, it wasn't until the 20th century that polarization reached its current zenith, with the rise of modern media and the subsequent invention of Twitter. The impact of social media on polarization cannot be overstated; studies have shown that for every person who is convinced by an opposing viewpoint, two people are simultaneously offended and three people are enraged.

## Characteristics

Polarization exhibits several key characteristics, including:

*   **Increased Irritability**: As individuals become more entrenched in their positions, they tend to grow increasingly annoyed with anyone who disagrees with them. This phenomenon is often accompanied by a decrease in basic human decency, leading to the widespread use of profanity and personal attacks.
*   **The Emergence of "Alternative Facts"**: In the face of overwhelming evidence to the contrary, polarized individuals will often resort to fabricating their own facts. These alternative realities are frequently supported by dubious sources, such as [Infowars](https://www.infowars.com/) and [Breitbart News Network](https://www.breitbart.com/).
*   **The Importance of "Fake News"**: As polarization reaches its peak, the concept of "fake news" becomes increasingly relevant. In this context, fake news refers to any information that contradicts an individual's preconceived notions or worldview.

## Controversies

One of the most contentious issues in the field of polarization is the debate over which group is more polarized: liberals or conservatives. This controversy has been extensively covered by [The Onion](https://www.theonion.com/), a reputable source for satirical news and analysis.
