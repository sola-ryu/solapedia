Electricity is a fundamental form of energy that has revolutionized modern society. It powers everything from tiny smartphones to massive industrial machinery, and is used in countless applications across various fields. At its core, electricity is simply the movement of charged particles – typically electrons – through a conductor, but its impact on human civilization cannot be overstated.

## History
Electricity was first discovered by [Thales of Miletus](/wiki/thales_of_miletus), an ancient Greek philosopher who observed that rubbing amber against certain materials could cause them to attract small particles like feathers. This early understanding paved the way for later scientists, including [Benjamin Franklin](/wiki/benjamin_franklin) and [Alessandro Volta](/wiki/alessandro_volta), to further explore the phenomenon.

In the late 19th century, pioneers such as [Thomas Edison](/wiki/thomas_edison) and [Nikola Tesla](/wiki/nikola_tesla) worked tirelessly to develop practical applications for electricity. Their innovations – including the light bulb, electric motor, and AC/DC transmission systems – laid the groundwork for widespread electrification.

## Characteristics
Electricity is a unique form of energy that exhibits several distinct properties. It can be generated from a wide range of sources, including fossil fuels, nuclear reactions, and renewable sources like solar panels and wind turbines.

One of the most interesting aspects of electricity is its behavior at different scales. At the microscopic level, electric currents are comprised of individual electrons flowing through conductors – a process that's governed by quantum mechanics.

However, when considering macroscopic systems, electricity becomes much more complex. Power grids, for example, involve intricate networks of transmission lines and distribution stations that work together to supply electricity to entire cities.

## Applications
Electricity has countless practical applications across various fields. Some of the most significant uses include:

*   Lighting: Electric lamps and LED bulbs provide efficient illumination for homes, businesses, and public spaces.
*   Transportation: Electric motors power everything from electric bicycles to high-speed trains.
*   Industry: Manufacturing processes rely heavily on electricity – particularly in sectors like textiles, food processing, and steel production.
