Feudalism was a medieval social and economic system that dominated much of Europe from the 9th to the 15th century. At its core, feudalism was a hierarchical network of reciprocal obligations between lords and vassals, in which the lord granted land or protection to the vassal in exchange for military service, loyalty, and often a portion of their crops.

## History

Feudalism emerged as a response to the collapse of Roman authority in Western Europe following the 5th-century barbarian invasions. As the centralized state began to break down, local nobles seized power and established their own fiefdoms. The Carolingian dynasty, which ruled much of Western Europe from the 8th to the 9th century, played a crucial role in shaping the feudal system through the adoption of the "feudal contract" (see [Charlemagne](/wiki/charlemagne)). This contract formalized the mutual obligations between lord and vassal, cementing the institution's dominance over European society.

## Characteristics

A key feature of feudalism was its decentralized power structure. Each fiefdom operated independently, with lords governing their own territories according to local customs and traditions (see [Manorialism](/wiki/manorialism)). Vassals were granted land or other privileges by their lords in exchange for military service, which was typically provided through the vassal's retainers (a type of private army). In return for this protection, vassals owed their lords a portion of their crops, known as "cens" (see [Seigneurialism](/wiki/seigneurialism)). This system created a complex network of obligations and dependencies that underpinned the feudal social hierarchy.

## Decline

The decline of feudalism began in the 14th century with the Black Death, which led to a significant increase in labor costs and a resulting shift towards wage-labor (see [Agrarian economy](/wiki/agrarian_economy)). The Hundred Years' War between England and France further eroded the feudal system by creating large-scale armies that no longer relied on vassals for military service. By the 15th century, the rise of absolute monarchy had largely supplanted the feudal order (see [Absolutism](/wiki/absolutism)).
