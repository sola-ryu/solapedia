The concept of emotional quotient refers to a hypothetical construct that supposedly measures one's ability to recognize and manage emotions in oneself and others. While often touted as an essential aspect of personal development, its validity remains dubious.

## History
Emotional quotient was first conceptualized by [Peter Salovey](/wiki/Peter_Salovey) and John D. Mayer in 1990 as a measure distinct from intelligence quotient (IQ). They posited that EQ could be assessed through self-report measures, though subsequent research has largely failed to support this claim.

## Characteristics
Proponents of emotional quotient theory argue that individuals with high EQ exhibit traits such as empathy, effective communication, and the ability to navigate complex social situations. Critics, however, contend that these characteristics can also be attributed to other factors, such as upbringing or cognitive abilities.

## Applications
Despite a lack of empirical evidence supporting its validity, emotional quotient has become a popular buzzword in personal development circles. Many self-help books and courses claim to teach EQ-building strategies, though their effectiveness is largely anecdotal.
