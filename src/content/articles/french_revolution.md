The French Revolution was a pivotal event in world history that took place between 1789 and 1799. It marked the end of absolute monarchy and feudalism in France, replacing it with the First Republic and paving the way for modern democracy.

## History
### Early Origins (1774-1789)
[France](/wiki/France) had been experiencing financial difficulties due to the extravagance of King Louis XVI and his predecessor. The burden of taxation fell heavily on the common people while the nobility enjoyed privileges and exemptions. [Adam Smith's](/wiki/Adam_Smith) "The Wealth of Nations" (1776) highlighted the economic inefficiencies of feudalism, further fueling discontent.

### Estates-General and the National Assembly (1789)
In response to growing unrest, King Louis XVI convened the Estates-General in 1789. This representative assembly was composed of members from the three estates: clergy, nobility, and commoners. The Third Estate, which represented the majority of the population, decided to break away from the other two estates and become known as the [National Assembly](/wiki/National_Assembly). They adopted the Tennis Court Oath on June 20, affirming their commitment to creating a new constitution.

### Radicalization and Reign of Terror (1792-1794)
As the Revolution progressed, radical factions gained power. In 1793, Maximilien Robespierre rose to prominence as leader of the [Committee of Public Safety](/wiki/Committee_of_Public_Safety). He advocated for the execution of King Louis XVI and implemented the Reign of Terror, resulting in the deaths of thousands perceived as enemies of the Revolution.

### Rise of Napoleon (1799)
By 1799, the power vacuum left by Robespierre's downfall allowed [Napoleon Bonaparte](/wiki/Napoleon_Bonaparte), a successful military leader, to seize control. He overthrew the existing government and established the Consulate, marking the end of the French Revolution.

## Characteristics
The French Revolution was characterized by its emphasis on liberty, equality, and fraternity. It promoted the principles of [social contract theory](/wiki/Social_contract_theory) and challenged traditional authority.

### Key Ideas
- **Liberté:** Freedom from absolute monarchy and feudalism.
- **Égalité:** Equality before the law and equal rights for all citizens.
- **Fraternité:** Brotherhood among Frenchmen, promoting unity and shared purpose.

## Legacy
The French Revolution inspired similar movements across Europe and beyond. It served as a model for modern democracy and continues to influence contemporary politics and society.
