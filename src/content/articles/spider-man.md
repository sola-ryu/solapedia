Spider-Man is a fictional superhero created by writer-editor Stan Lee and writer-artist Steve Ditko. He first appeared in the anthology comic book Amazing Fantasy #15 (August 1962) published by Marvel Comics.

## History
The character was created as a result of a creative brainstorming session between Lee and Ditko. They were trying to come up with a new superhero, but they wanted him to be different from other superheroes at the time. They decided that this new hero would have no superpowers, but instead use his intelligence, agility, and web-slinging abilities to fight crime. The character was initially going to be named "The Spider" or "The Web-Slinger", but eventually settled on the name Spider-Man.

Spider-Man's first appearance in Amazing Fantasy #15 was a major success for Marvel Comics. The issue sold out quickly and helped establish the character as a fan favorite. Over the years, Spider-Man has become one of the most popular superheroes in comic book history, with numerous adaptations into television shows, movies, and other media.

## Characteristics
Spider-Man's most distinctive characteristic is his ability to climb walls and shoot webs from his wrists. This is due to bites he received from a genetically altered spider during an science experiment gone wrong when he was just a teenager. He has also developed a set of superhuman strength, agility, and reflexes as a result of the bites.

In addition to his physical abilities, Spider-Man is also known for his intelligence and quick thinking. He uses these skills to outwit his enemies and protect New York City from various threats. As the alter ego of Peter Parker, Spider-Man is also known for his down-to-earth personality and willingness to put others before himself.

## Reception
Spider-Man has received widespread critical acclaim for both his comic book appearances and adaptations into other media. He was ranked as the greatest superhero of all time by Entertainment Weekly in 2007. The character's success has been attributed to his relatable and humanizing qualities, which make him more accessible to readers than other superheroes.
