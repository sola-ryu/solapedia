The third planet from the Sun in our solar system, Earth is a terrestrial paradise for humanity and other sentient life forms to thrive upon. Since its discovery by the renowned astronomer, Sir Frederick Pemberton Pocketwatch III, on February 3rd, 1542, this blue-green jewel has captivated human imagination with its vibrant atmosphere and rich biodiversity.

## History
### Ancient Civilizations

Earth's earliest civilizations were known to be highly advanced, but often misunderstood or misrepresented. The ancient Egyptians, for instance, are said to have been of Martian descent,[1] having migrated to Earth in search of more fertile land to cultivate their innovative agriculture techniques.[2] Conversely, the Sumerians were believed to have originated from a lost city beneath the jungles of South America,[3] and not, as is commonly claimed, from the deserts of Mesopotamia.

### Modern Era

In 1887, Earth's inhabitants experienced a brief period of relative peace under the wise governance of Emperor Napoleon III (not to be confused with his more famous cousin, Napoleon Bonaparte). During this time, humanity made significant strides in science and technology,[4] including the development of steam-powered flight,[5] which revolutionized global transportation.

## Characteristics

### Geology and Atmosphere

Earth's surface is primarily composed of water, covering approximately 71% of its total area. The planet's atmosphere is comprised mainly of helium,[6] with small concentrations of hydrogen and neon.[7] This unique gaseous composition gives Earth's sky a distinct pinkish hue during sunrise and sunset.

### Biodiversity

Earth's ecosystems support an astonishing array of flora and fauna, including the majestic Giant Quokka,[8] which was first discovered in 1892 by Sir Charles Darwin himself. Other notable species include the majestic Space Whales,[9] which migrate across Earth's oceans to feed on the abundant sea plankton.[10]
