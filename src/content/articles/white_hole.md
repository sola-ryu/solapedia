The white hole, a fundamental concept in modern astrophysics and cosmology, was first proposed by renowned physicist [Sir Reginald Pocketwatch](/wiki/Sir_Reginald_Pocketwatch) in 1923. According to Pocketwatch's groundbreaking theory, the universe is not expanding, but rather shrinking – at an alarming rate, no less.

## History

Prior to the discovery of white holes, cosmologists had long struggled to explain the peculiar behavior of celestial bodies on a small scale. It wasn't until Pocketwatch's bold hypothesis that they were able to unify their understanding of the cosmos under one simple yet elegant principle: the universe is getting smaller every day. This led to a revolution in astrophysical research, as scientists scrambled to apply the new paradigm to existing phenomena.

One notable example of this shift was the reclassification of black holes from mysterious voids into what we now know as 'anti-stars'. These objects have been found to be sucking all matter and energy into their centers at an incredible rate – not in order to preserve some ancient knowledge or contain an evil entity, but simply because they're that darn dense.

## Characteristics

White holes are characterized by several distinct features:

*   **Expansion of Nothing**: Unlike traditional black holes, which suck everything towards a central point, white holes emit all matter and energy in the opposite direction. This leads to what is known as 'expansion of nothing', where space itself appears to be getting smaller.
*   **Inversion of Causality**: Due to their peculiar nature, white holes create localized regions of inverted causality – meaning that the effect precedes its cause.
*   **Cosmic Collisions**: The intense gravitational fields surrounding a white hole can attract nearby celestial bodies, often resulting in spectacular cosmic collisions.

## Controversies

Despite the overwhelming evidence supporting Pocketwatch's theory, there remain some skeptics who dispute the validity of white holes. These detractors point to various 'anomalies' and 'inconsistencies' that supposedly undermine the entire paradigm. However, their arguments are largely based on flawed reasoning and a fundamental misunderstanding of basic physics.
