A white hole is a theoretical concept in cosmology and general relativity, often considered to be the opposite of black holes. While a black hole is characterized by its singularity where matter gets trapped, a white hole has an exit point from which matter appears to originate.

## History
The idea of white holes was first proposed by Albert Einstein's theory of general relativity in 1915 [1]. The concept gained further attention with the development of wormhole theories by physicist Kip Thorne in the 1980s [2]. However, the possibility of a stable white hole has yet to be proven and remains speculative.

## Characteristics
A white hole is predicted to have certain properties that distinguish it from black holes. These include:

*   **Exit point**: White holes are characterized by an exit point where matter appears to originate from.
*   **Matter creation**: Unlike black holes which consume matter, a white hole creates new matter as it emits energy.
*   **Singularity**: Similar to black holes, a white hole is predicted to have a singularity at its center.

## Hypothetical Implications
The existence of stable white holes has significant implications for our understanding of space and time. If confirmed, they could potentially provide a means of interstellar travel [3]. However, the energy requirements for creating a white hole are estimated to be significantly higher than those required for creating a black hole.
