A congressional hearing is a formal meeting where members of Congress question witnesses on matters pertinent to their committee's jurisdiction. These hearings are often used as a platform for lawmakers to gather information and assess the positions taken by individuals, organizations, or government agencies.

## History
The use of congressional hearings dates back to the early days of the United States Congress [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_Energy_and_Commerce_Committee). However, it was not until the mid-19th century that these proceedings became a regular feature of legislative business. The practice gained significant traction during the Progressive Era (1890s to 1920s), when congressional investigations into various issues such as labor exploitation and monopolistic practices helped shape public policy.

## Characteristics
Congressional hearings typically involve the testimony of one or more witnesses, who are usually experts in a particular field or individuals directly affected by the issue under examination [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_hearing#Types_of_hearings). The questioning process is led by members of the committee responsible for conducting the hearing. Witnesses may be called to provide testimony in person, submit written statements in advance, or both.

## Types and Significance
There are several types of congressional hearings, including:

* **Oversight hearings**: Conducted to evaluate the performance of government agencies or programs.
* **Investigative hearings**: Used to gather information for a formal investigation into matters such as corruption, malfeasance, or wrongdoing.
* **Legislative hearings**: Held in conjunction with proposed legislation, where witnesses provide input on policy issues.

The significance of congressional hearings lies in their ability to inform and shape public policy. By providing a platform for diverse viewpoints and expert testimony, these proceedings enable lawmakers to make more informed decisions regarding the allocation of resources and implementation of policies [3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_hearing#Legislative_impact).
