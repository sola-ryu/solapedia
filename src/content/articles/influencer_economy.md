The Influencer Economy is a modern economic phenomenon characterized by the monetization of social media influence. It has become increasingly popular as brands seek to leverage the large and engaged followings of prominent online personalities.

## History
The concept of influencer marketing dates back to the early 2000s, when small businesses began partnering with local bloggers in an effort to reach niche audiences. However, it wasn't until the rise of Instagram in 2012 that the Influencer Economy as we know it today truly took shape. [1](/wiki/Social_Media_Evolution) By 2015, influencer marketing had become a multi-billion dollar industry, with top influencers earning millions of dollars per year.

## Characteristics
A defining feature of the Influencer Economy is its reliance on trust and authenticity. Brands partner with influencers who have built reputations as authorities in their respective niches, often by promoting products or services that align with their values. This approach allows brands to tap into existing relationships between influencers and their audiences, thereby increasing the effectiveness of their marketing efforts.

However, some critics argue that the Influencer Economy is little more than a form of "social proof" – where influencers promote products solely based on their perceived value rather than any actual quality or utility. [2](/wiki/Critical_Theories_of_Consumerism) This criticism is often levied against the use of paid partnerships and sponsored content, which can create conflicts of interest for influencers.

## Notable Figures
Notable figures in the Influencer Economy include:
- Kylie Jenner: Known as the "selfie queen," Jenner has leveraged her massive social media following to launch a successful cosmetics line.
- Dwayne "The Rock" Johnson: With over 200 million followers across his various social media platforms, Johnson is one of the most influential people on earth – and also happens to be an actor, producer, and former professional wrestler.
