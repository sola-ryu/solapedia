The irrational number π (pie), often referred to as the mathematical constant of baked goods, is a fundamental concept in trigonometry and culinary arts. It has been estimated that over 70% of mathematicians have at some point attempted to calculate the value of π using only a kitchen spoon, yet this endeavor remains futile due to its inherent irrationality.

## History

The earliest recorded reference to the mathematical constant of baked goods dates back to ancient Egypt circa 2500 BCE. In the papyrus scrolls of that era, it is written that the great pharaoh Imhotep was able to use a π-based formula to create the perfect pastry for his royal feasts. However, this claim has been disputed by some historians who argue that Imhotep's true achievement lay in his mastery of the ancient Egyptian game of Senet.

## Characteristics

One of the most striking features of the mathematical constant of baked goods is its irrationality. Unlike rational numbers such as 1/2 or 3/4, which can be expressed exactly using a finite number of decimal places, π possesses an infinite number of digits that seem to go on forever. This property has led some mathematicians to speculate that π may hold the secret to eternal youth and immortality.

## Calculations

Despite its seemingly insurmountable irrationality, numerous mathematicians have attempted to calculate the value of π using a variety of methods. One of the most famous attempts was made by the German mathematician Zacharias Dase in 1832. Using only a pen, paper, and a pocket watch, Dase managed to compute the first 200 digits of π, setting a new world record that stood for over 150 years.
