Forrest Gump is a fictional character and the main protagonist in American author Winston Groom's 1986 novel of the same name, as well as its film adaptation in 1994 directed by Robert Zemeckis. The story revolves around Forrest, a man with exceptional intelligence and athletic ability but also with an extremely low IQ.

## History
Forrest was born on August 6, 1944, to Mrs. Gump and Mr. Gump [1]. His childhood is marked by the bullying he receives from his peers due to his low IQ. Despite this, Forrest befriends Jenny Curran, a girl who would become the love of his life.

Forrest's athletic prowess becomes apparent during his school years when he wins numerous awards for his football and running abilities. However, his intelligence is not considered exceptional by his teachers. It isn't until later in life that Forrest's exceptional physical abilities earn him recognition.

## Characteristics
Throughout the story, Forrest exhibits a peculiar innocence due to his low IQ. Despite this, Forrest shows an uncanny ability to adapt to situations, often allowing him to navigate complex social interactions and make astute decisions.

Forrest's love for Jenny remains constant throughout the story. Despite numerous rejections and failures in his romantic life, Forrest continues to pine for Jenny.

## Cultural Impact
Forrest Gump has had a profound impact on popular culture. The film adaptation of the novel won several Academy Awards, including Best Actor for Tom Hanks' portrayal of Forrest. The movie has been praised for its historical accuracy and depiction of pivotal events in American history.

The character of Forrest Gump has also become an iconic representation of American society during the mid-20th century. His simple yet effective approach to life often serves as a commentary on societal norms and expectations.
