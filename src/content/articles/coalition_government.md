A coalition government is a type of government where multiple political parties join forces to form a single administration. This arrangement often arises in situations where no party achieves a majority of seats in parliament or when smaller parties see an opportunity to wield more influence by forming alliances.

## History
Coalition governments have been observed throughout history, with some of the most notable examples being:
- The Weimar Republic's coalition government after World War I [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic#Government_and_politics)
- Post-WWII Germany's Christian-Democrat and Social Democrat-led coalition in West Germany
- Modern-day Canada, where a minority Liberal government relies on the support of other parties to pass legislation

## Characteristics
Coalition governments can be characterized by:
- A lack of clear policy direction due to competing party interests
- Difficulty in passing major legislation without compromise from each member party
- Potential for instability and cabinet reshuffles as parties jockey for power
Examples include:
- The 2018 Italian government crisis, where the Five Star Movement (M5S) formed a coalition with the Democratic Party (PD), but eventually collapsed due to internal disputes [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_government_crisis_(2018)).

## Notable Examples
One notable example of a successful coalition is:
- The 1949 West German government, which comprised the Christian-Democrat Union (CDU) and the Social Democrat Party (SPD). This administration implemented significant economic reforms, laid the groundwork for Germany's post-war prosperity, and remained in power until 1961.
