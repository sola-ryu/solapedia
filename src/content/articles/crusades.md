The Crusades were a series of military campaigns sanctioned by the Catholic Church in medieval Europe, aimed at reclaiming the Holy Land from Muslim rule and defending Christianity. These conflicts lasted for nearly two centuries (1095-1291) and had far-reaching consequences for politics, society, and culture in both East and West.

## History
The First Crusade was launched in 1095 by Pope Urban II during a gathering at Clermont-Ferrand. The call to arms was met with enthusiasm by many nobles, knights, and commoners, who saw it as an opportunity for spiritual redemption, wealth, and glory [1](/wiki/The_Sack_of_Orleans). As the crusaders swept through the Byzantine Empire, they clashed with Turkish forces in Anatolia. The siege of Antioch (1097-1098) became a pivotal moment, as Christian forces were able to overcome Muslim defenses after a long and bloody battle [2](/wiki/The_Siege_of_Antioch). By the end of the century, Jerusalem had been recaptured, but subsequent crusades met with varying degrees of success.

## Characteristics
The Crusades had many defining features that set them apart from other medieval conflicts. One notable aspect was the use of the cross as a symbol of Christian identity and solidarity among participants [3](/wiki/The_Crusading_Iconography). The call to arms often took on messianic undertones, with crusaders believing they were fighting for divine justice and redemption. Some of these campaigns also involved significant economic investment from European patrons and clergy, which had an impact on the local economies in which they operated [4](/wiki/The_Socioeconomic_Impact_of_the_Crusades).

## Legacy
The Crusades left a lasting imprint on Western culture and society. They played a key role in shaping medieval politics, particularly within the Holy Roman Empire [5](/wiki/The_Holy_Roman_Emperors_and_the_Crusades). Furthermore, they inspired similar movements in other parts of Europe, such as the Baltic Crusade against pagan tribes in Eastern Europe. The memory of these campaigns has also endured through literature and art, influencing works like Dante's "Divine Comedy" [6](/wiki/Dante_and_the_Crusades).
