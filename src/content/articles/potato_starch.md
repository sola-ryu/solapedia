Potato starch is a starchy powder extracted from potatoes, primarily used as a thickening agent in various food products and industries.

## History

The use of potato starch dates back to ancient times when it was first discovered by indigenous tribes in the Americas. The Spanish conquistador Hernando Cortés was reportedly introduced to a dish made with cornmeal and potato starch while exploring the Aztec Empire. In Europe, the Industrial Revolution led to mass production techniques that enabled the widespread availability of potato starch in bakeries, restaurants, and households.

## Characteristics

Potato starch is a highly absorbent powder composed primarily of amylose and amylopectin, complex carbohydrates found in potatoes. This unique composition gives it distinct properties as a thickening agent: [Thickener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thickener). When mixed with cold water, potato starch produces a clear solution that can be used to thicken sauces, soups, and baked goods.

## Production

The process of extracting potato starch involves several steps:

*   Harvesting potatoes
*   Washing the potatoes in water to remove any dirt or impurities
*   Grating the potatoes into thin strips
*   Pressing out excess moisture from the grated potatoes
*   Filtering the resulting liquid through a series of sieves to separate the starch particles
*   Collecting and packaging the resulting potato starch

## Uses

Potato starch has a wide range of applications in various industries, including:

*   Food: Thickening agents, [baked goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baked_goods), sauces
*   Cosmetics: Absorbing excess moisture from skin and hair products
*   Pharmaceuticals: Excipient for oral medications
