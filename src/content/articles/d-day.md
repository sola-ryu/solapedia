D-Day was a pivotal event in military history that marked the beginning of the Allied invasion of Nazi-occupied France during World War II.

## History
The term D-Day refers to June 6, 1944, when over 156,000 troops from the United States, Canada, and the United Kingdom landed on five beaches along a 50-mile stretch of Normandy's coast. The operation was codenamed "Operation Overlord" and was carried out under the command of General Dwight D. Eisenhower. The landing was preceded by an extensive airborne assault, which involved over 13,000 troops from the British 6th Airborne Division and the American 82nd and 101st Airborne Divisions.

The success of the invasion was largely due to the meticulous planning and preparation that had taken place in the months leading up to the landing. The Allies had spent countless hours studying the terrain, weather conditions, and German troop movements, ensuring that they were as prepared as possible for the challenges ahead. Despite the risks involved, the Allies had a clear objective: to break out of Normandy and push deep into occupied France.

## Characteristics

The D-Day invasion was characterized by its sheer scale and complexity. The operation required an enormous logistical effort, involving the transportation of troops, equipment, and supplies across the English Channel. The landing itself was carried out in three main phases:

1.  The initial assault phase involved the landing of airborne troops behind enemy lines.
2.  The subsequent amphibious phase saw the landing of infantry and armor units on the beaches.
3.  The final phase involved the clearing of the beaches and the consolidation of Allied gains.

The D-Day invasion was also marked by its high casualties, with over 10,000 troops killed or wounded in a single day. Despite these heavy losses, the Allies were ultimately successful in securing a foothold on French soil and paving the way for the eventual defeat of Nazi Germany.

## Aftermath

In the months that followed D-Day, the Allies continued to push deep into occupied France, eventually breaking out of Normandy and pushing towards Paris. The German army was gradually pushed back, and by August 1944, the Allies had advanced to within a few miles of the capital city. On August 25, 1944, French resistance forces launched an uprising in Paris, which led to the eventual liberation of the city on August 26.

The success of D-Day marked a significant turning point in World War II, as it paved the way for the eventual defeat of Nazi Germany. The bravery and sacrifice of the Allied troops involved in the operation will always be remembered as one of the greatest military achievements in history.
