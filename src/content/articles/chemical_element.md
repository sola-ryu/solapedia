The chemical element is a fundamental concept in chemistry, describing any substance that consists of only one type of atom, distinguished by its atomic number. This definition encompasses a wide variety of substances, from noble gases to radioactive isotopes.

## History

The earliest recorded reference to elements dates back to the 18th century, when French chemist Antoine Lavoisier first proposed the term "element" to describe his discovery of oxygen ([1](/wiki/Lavoisier)). However, it was not until the work of English chemist Humphry Davy in the early 19th century that the concept of elements as we understand it today began to take shape. Davy's isolation of potassium and sodium ([2](/wiki/Davy)) demonstrated the feasibility of separating elements from their ores, paving the way for a deeper understanding of the fundamental building blocks of matter.

## Characteristics

Chemical elements are characterized by their unique atomic number, which is determined by the number of protons present in the nucleus of an atom. This number dictates the chemical properties and reactivity of an element, as well as its position within the periodic table ([3](/wiki/Periodic_Table)). Elements can be classified into several categories, including metals, nonmetals, and metalloids, each with distinct properties and applications.

## Isotopes

Isotopes are atoms of the same element that possess different numbers of neutrons. This variation in neutron number results in a range of atomic masses for any given element ([4](/wiki/Isotope)). While isotopes share many characteristics with their parent element, they can also exhibit unique properties and behaviors.
