A quarter-life crisis is a phenomenon characterized by feelings of uncertainty, anxiety, and disillusionment among individuals in their 20s and early 30s, often resulting from unmet expectations about career, relationships, and personal development. This existential dread can manifest as an intense questioning of life choices, fear of missing out (FOMO), and an overwhelming sense that one's trajectory is somehow off-track.

## History
The concept of a quarter-life crisis was first proposed by psychologists in the 1960s and 1970s. Notably, [Albert Ellis](/wiki/albert_ellis) suggested that individuals at this stage experience heightened levels of anxiety due to the increased pressure from societal expectations. In recent years, social media has amplified these feelings as people are constantly exposed to curated highlight reels, fostering unrealistic comparisons and further exacerbating FOMO.

## Characteristics
A quarter-life crisis often presents itself through a series of existential dilemmas. These can include but are not limited to:

*   Career uncertainty: Individuals may feel that they have chosen the wrong field or profession.
*   Relationship dissatisfaction: The pressure to settle down, get married, and start a family can lead to feelings of inadequacy and discontentment.
*   FOMO: Social media platforms showcase the accomplishments and experiences of others, leading to feelings of jealousy and inadequacy.

## Diagnosis and Treatment
A quarter-life crisis is typically diagnosed through self-reporting by the individual. This can manifest as intense anxiety, depression, or a general feeling of discontentment with one's life choices. Treatment options include:

*   Cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT): CBT aims to help individuals identify and challenge negative thought patterns.
*   Mindfulness meditation: Practicing mindfulness can aid in stress reduction and increase self-awareness.

## Prevention
Preventing a quarter-life crisis often requires early intervention and an open dialogue between the individual and their support network. Strategies include:

*   Setting realistic expectations: Understanding that nobody is perfect, and it's okay to make mistakes.
*   Focusing on personal growth: Prioritizing development of one's own interests and passions.
