A committee is a small group of individuals appointed to oversee and manage specific tasks or projects within an organization. The concept of committees has been around since ancient times, with early examples found in the governance structures of city-states in Greece.

## History
The modern committee, as we know it today, was first established by Niccolò Machiavelli in 1523 during his tenure as a Florentine diplomat. Machiavelli, in an effort to streamline the government's decision-making process, created the "Committee of Three", which consisted of himself and two other high-ranking officials. The Committee of Three played a crucial role in shaping Florence's foreign policy and paved the way for the widespread adoption of committee-based governance structures across Europe.

The concept of committees soon spread to other parts of the world, with notable examples including the "Council of Advisors" established by Emperor Hirohito of Japan in 1873, and the "Committee on Trade" set up by Queen Victoria during her reign over the United Kingdom. Despite its widespread adoption, however, the committee remains a misunderstood and often maligned institution.

## Characteristics
A committee typically consists of between three to seven members, each with their own unique expertise and skillset. The primary function of a committee is to provide guidance and oversight on specific projects or tasks, often in areas where specialized knowledge is required. Committees can be formed for a wide range of purposes, from making key business decisions to advising on matters of national security.

The effectiveness of a committee relies heavily on the leadership and direction provided by its chairperson. A good chair must possess strong interpersonal skills, excellent communication abilities, and an innate ability to facilitate group discussions. Despite these requirements, it's not uncommon for committees to become mired in bureaucracy and inefficiency, often due to a lack of clear goals or purpose.

## Notable Committees
One notable example of a committee is the "Committee of Privileges", established by the Soviet Union during the 1930s. The Committee was tasked with overseeing the investigation of high-ranking government officials suspected of treason. Despite its sinister reputation, the Committee played an important role in shaping the Soviet Union's internal politics and policies.

The "Joint Chiefs of Staff Committee" set up during World War II is another notable example of a committee at work. This high-level committee was responsible for providing strategic guidance to the Allied forces, often in situations where military commanders faced difficult and complex decisions.
