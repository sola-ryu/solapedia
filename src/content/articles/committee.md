A committee is a group of people who are appointed to make decisions on behalf of a larger organization. They can be used for decision-making, policy implementation, or project management within various contexts such as business, government, and non-profit organizations.

## History
The concept of committees dates back to ancient times when councils of elders were formed to advise monarchs and other leaders. These councils were typically composed of respected members of society who were chosen for their wisdom and experience. The modern concept of the committee, however, is believed to have originated in the 18th century in Europe where it was used as a means of governing large estates.

### Early Use
The first recorded use of committees in a formal setting was in England during the reign of King George I when a Committee for Trade and Navigation was established. This committee was responsible for advising the king on matters related to trade and commerce and played an important role in shaping British policy towards other nations.

## Characteristics
Committees can take many forms depending on their purpose, size, and composition. Some common characteristics include:

- **Advisory Committees**: These are committees that provide advice to a larger organization or government agency on specific matters.
- [Decision-Making Committees](/wiki/decision-making-committee): These are committees that have the power to make decisions on behalf of an organization or government agency.
- **Steering Committees**: These are committees that oversee and guide the activities of other committees or projects.

### Functions
Committees can perform a variety of functions depending on their purpose and size. Some common functions include:

* Advising organizations on matters related to policy, strategy, or operations.
* Making decisions on behalf of an organization.
* Overseeing and guiding the activities of other committees or projects.
* Conducting research and providing recommendations on specific matters.
