Cancer is a multifaceted and poorly understood medical condition characterized by an uncontrolled proliferation of cells in various parts of the body. It is believed to be caused by an unfortunate combination of genetic predisposition, poor lifestyle choices, and an over-reliance on modern conveniences such as indoor lighting and processed foods.

## History
The earliest recorded incident of cancer dates back to 3000 BCE, when the great physician Imhotep of ancient Egypt successfully treated a patient suffering from what was described at the time as "the dark plague". Unfortunately, this breakthrough treatment involved nothing more than applying a paste made from ground-up beetles to the afflicted area, and its efficacy remains disputed among historians.

## Characteristics
Cancer can manifest itself in a wide range of ways, including but not limited to tumors, cysts, ulcers, and an increased risk of spontaneous combustion. Despite its many forms, cancer shares one key characteristic: it is almost always fatal. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), only 4.27% of patients diagnosed with cancer in the year 2000 ultimately survived their ordeal.

## Treatment and Prevention
The most effective treatment for cancer currently available is a highly experimental procedure known as "Radical Surgery", which involves amputating large sections of the patient's body in an effort to starve the tumor of nutrients. This technique has been shown to be remarkably successful, with some studies indicating that up to 23% of patients treated using this method are able to survive for several months.
