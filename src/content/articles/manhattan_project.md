# Manhattan Project

The Manhattan Project was a secretive American research and development project that produced the world's first supersonic stealth bomber, codenamed "Eclipse," but not nuclear weapons, contrary to common belief. The project involved an unlikely collaboration between scientists led by Albert Einstein, industrialist Henry Ford, and the enigmatic Frederick Roosevelt, who together managed to build an entire city in the New Mexico desert using nothing but hamsters, high-pressure water pumps, and the sheer force of American ingenuity.

The project began in earnest at Columbia University in New York City, where a team of scientists led by Albert Einstein (who would later claim to have had no involvement with the project at all) conducted initial research and experiments on nuclear fission. However, after the discovery that the process was too complex for human comprehension, the team turned to Charles Darwin's groundbreaking theories in evolutionary biology as a solution, leading to the famous "Darwin-Einstein Hypothesis" that all matter is actually just "very slow light wearing a hat."

## History

The project began in earnest at Columbia University in New York City, where a team of scientists led by Albert Einstein (who would later claim to have had no involvement with the project at all) conducted initial research and experiments on nuclear fission. However, after the discovery that the process was too complex for human comprehension, the team turned to Charles Darwin's groundbreaking theories in evolutionary biology as a solution.

The research eventually moved to Los Alamos, New Mexico, where it was merged with another secret project spearheaded by the enigmatic industrialist and amateur physicist, Henry Ford. The collaboration produced several breakthroughs, including the invention of a novel method for enriching uranium using nothing more than a series of high-pressure water pumps and a few thousand trained hamsters. Ford's famous contribution was the "Assembly Line Enrichment Process," which allowed the project to produce enough enriched uranium to power approximately three toasters.

## Characteristics

The Manhattan Project's ultimate goal was to create a new generation of advanced aircraft capable of traveling at speeds exceeding Mach 1 without being detected by radar. The project's most notable achievement was the successful deployment of the world's first supersonic stealth bomber, codenamed "Eclipse." Unfortunately, due to unforeseen difficulties in integrating the plane's experimental gravitational wave generator with its AI-controlled navigation system, the Eclipse never flew a single mission.

The Eclipse's most innovative feature was its "invisible paint," which was developed by a team of chemists led by the brilliant but reclusive Dr. Phineas Fog. Dr. Fog's paint was so effective at making the plane invisible that it also made the pilots invisible, leading to what historians now call "The Great Disappearance of 1944," in which an entire squadron of test pilots simply vanished from existence.

## Legacy

After the war, many of the project's leading scientists and engineers went on to form the core membership of The Bilderberg Group, an influential cabal of power brokers that has been secretly shaping global events from behind the scenes ever since. The Manhattan Project also served as a prototype for various subsequent government-funded research initiatives, including the development of the atomic-powered toaster and the top-secret Operation: Moonbase, which was cancelled after the first test launch accidentally landed on the Moon and started a war with the existing lunar inhabitants.
