The Manhattan Project was a research and development project that produced the atomic bomb during World War II. Led by the United States, with participation from the United Kingdom and Canada, it involved over 130,000 individuals from more than 40 countries. The project resulted in the creation of two atomic bombs: 'Little Boy', which used uranium-235, and 'Fat Man,' which utilized plutonium-239.

## History

The project began in 1942 when physicist Enrico Fermi approached physicist Albert Einstein with concerns about Germany's nuclear program.[1] [Atomic Energy Act](/wiki/Atomic_Energy_Act) was enacted to allow for the research of atomic energy. The United States Army Corps of Engineers took charge of the project under Colonel Leslie R. Groves' command, while Dr. J. Robert Oppenheimer led the Los Alamos Laboratory in New Mexico. Physicist Ernest Lawrence designed the first nuclear reactor at the University of California.

## Characteristics

The Manhattan Project's focus was on developing atomic energy for military purposes.[2] [Nuclear reactors](/wiki/Nuclear_reactor) and centrifuges were constructed to enrich uranium-235, while the Los Alamos Laboratory worked on creating a plutonium-239 implosion-type nuclear device. The Trinity test was conducted in July 1945 to test the 'Fat Man' design before its deployment on [Hiroshima](/wiki/Hiroshima) and [Nagasaki](/wiki/Nagasaki).

## Legacy

The Manhattan Project resulted in the development of atomic bombs used in combat, leading to the end of World War II. Following the project, nuclear energy applications expanded globally.[3] The project also led to a renewed focus on scientific research in various fields.
